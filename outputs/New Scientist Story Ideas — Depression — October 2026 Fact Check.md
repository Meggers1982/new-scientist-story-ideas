### Study 1: Brain training for burnout still pays off four and a half years later
**PMID:** 42698289 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core facts check out: 132 randomized, 56 returned at 4.5 years, CCT group sustained effects on the global cognitive score, a trained updating task and episodic memory, AT group no extended cognitive benefit, and the psychological/work-ability gains attributed by the authors to selective attrition. Journal, volume, issue, article number and DOI all match.

**Issue A. Unstated training dose** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "burnout patients who did a few weeks of computerized training were still measurably sharper"
- **Abstract says:** Describes "the addition of computerised cognitive training (CCT) and aerobic training (AT) to a multimodal rehabilitation programme (MMR)" with no duration given.
- **Problem:** The length of the training course ("a few weeks", and "a short course" in Why it matters) is not verifiable from the abstract. If the original trial used a 12-week programme, "a few weeks" understates the intervention and inflates the hook.
- **Suggested fix:** "burnout patients who added computerised cognitive training to their rehabilitation were still measurably sharper" (and check the original trial protocol before specifying a duration).

**Issue B. "Among the longest follow-ups" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is among the longest follow-ups showing that a short course of computerized training may leave a durable trace"
- **Abstract says:** Describes a 4.5-year follow-up but makes no priority or comparative claim.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; it is a literature-wide claim the abstract does not support. The "Novelty: First-in-class" tag rests on the same unverified comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** "this is an unusually long follow-up for a cognitive-training trial, showing…" — or source the claim from the authors on the record.

**Issue C. Author first name not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Hanna Malmberg Gavelin at Umeå University"
- **Abstract says:** "Gavelin HM(1)(6). (1)Department of Psychology, Umeå University, Umeå, Sweden."
- **Problem:** The full given name is unverifiable from abstract only. Affiliation and last-author status are correct.
- **Suggested fix:** Confirm the full name against the published paper or institutional page before the pitch goes out.

**Issue D. NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "NS fit: 7/10 — a rare, very long follow-up of a randomized trial in burnout patients, dented by heavy attrition."
- **Problem:** A 7 is borderline here. The design is randomized and the primary outcome is objective cognitive testing, which supports a higher score than the other entries — but 58% of the original sample was lost, the per-group N at follow-up is very small, and the authors themselves discount a chunk of the results as attrition artefact. The score note is honest about this, but 7 slightly over-rewards it.
- **Suggested fix:** Score 6/10, with the same justification text.

### Study 2: Rapid brain stimulation cuts suicidal thoughts within days in a small trial
**PMID:** 42842759 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Response/remission at end of treatment (97.1%/73.5%), the 17/17 unipolar-bipolar split, daily C-SSRS assessment, the five-day protocol and the no-significant-difference-between-groups finding are all accurately reported.

**Issue A. "Inpatients" is not in the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Thirty-four inpatients with depression — 17 unipolar, 17 bipolar"
- **Abstract says:** "Thirty-four patients with depression (17 unipolar, 17 bipolar) completed a 5-day iTBS treatment protocol."
- **Problem:** The abstract never specifies inpatient status. This is not a cosmetic detail: it carries into the caveat ("inpatient sample may not generalize") and underpins the entire wider angle about "psychiatric inpatient care" and "compressing six weeks of daily hospital visits into one admission." If the sample was outpatient or mixed, that angle loses its peg.
- **Suggested fix:** "Thirty-four patients with depression — 17 unipolar, 17 bipolar" and verify setting from the full text before building the inpatient-care angle.

**Issue B. One-month response rounded down** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "at one month, roughly three-quarters still met response criteria"
- **Abstract says:** "At the one-month follow-up, response rates for suicidal ideation were 80% in the unipolar group and 75% in the bipolar group."
- **Problem:** "Roughly three-quarters" understates the unipolar figure and obscures that the rates were reported separately by group, not pooled.
- **Suggested fix:** "at one month, 80% of unipolar and 75% of bipolar patients still met response criteria, with remission maintained in about 60% of each group."

**Issue C. Single-centre and open-label described as fact** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single center, open-label so expectancy effects are uncontrolled"
- **Abstract says:** No affiliations, country or blinding information are given; the methods describe a single treated cohort with no comparator.
- **Problem:** Absence of a sham arm is a fair inference from the methods, but "single center" and the explicit open-label designation are unverifiable from abstract only. Country of origin is also absent, which matters for the health-policy/insurance angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "no control or sham group reported; blinding and study setting not specified in the abstract; short follow-up (one month)."

**Issue D. "First-in-class" novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Novelty: First-in-class"
- **Abstract says:** "Intermittent theta-burst stimulation (iTBS) has emerged as a promising neuromodulatory technique with potential anti-suicidal and antidepressant effects."
- **Problem:** The abstract positions iTBS within an existing literature rather than as a first. Accelerated iTBS for suicidality has been trialled elsewhere; what may be novel here is the unipolar/bipolar comparison. The digest's own line that including bipolar patients "is unusual" is also unverifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Novelty: New population (bipolar patients included in an accelerated iTBS protocol)".

### Study 3: Short-acting psychedelics are being tested against depression — and one version leaves out the trip
**PMID:** 42838215 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Fifty-one publications, 20–30 minute IV DMT acute effects, ~28 minute sublingual 5-MeO-DMT elimination half-life, GH001 meeting its Phase 2b primary endpoint, and zalsupindole's single preclinical study with no head-twitch response are all reported correctly. Crucially, the digest correctly preserves the abstract's hedge that zalsupindole's "human efficacy and hallucinogenic potential remain unknown," and the caveats disclose McIntyre's Atai Life Sciences and wider pharmaceutical ties as declared. Good practice.

**Issue A. Conflating route of administration with duration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lead on the contrast between a day-long psilocybin session and a 20-minute inhalation."
- **Abstract says:** "Intravenous N,N-DMT exhibited rapid pharmacokinetics, with acute effects resolving within 20-30 min, while sublingual 5-MeO-DMT had an elimination half-life of approximately 28 min. … GH001, an inhalable 5-MeO-DMT formulation…"
- **Problem:** The 20–30 minute figure belongs to intravenous DMT; the ~28 minute half-life belongs to sublingual 5-MeO-DMT. No duration is given for the inhaled GH001 formulation. The pitch merges all three into "a 20-minute inhalation."
- **Suggested fix:** "Lead on the contrast between a day-long psilocybin session and an intravenous dose whose effects resolve inside half an hour."

**Issue B. Eight-hour session figure not from the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The practical obstacle to psychedelic therapy is the eight-hour supervised session"
- **Abstract says:** Refers only to "pharmacological profiles that may offer practical advantages over longer-acting psychedelics," with no session duration.
- **Problem:** Plausible background, but unverifiable from abstract only and presented in the Why it matters as if it came from the paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "The practical obstacle to psychedelic therapy is the long supervised session" — or attribute the eight-hour figure to a separate source.

**Issue C. "New mechanism" novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Novelty: New mechanism"
- **Abstract says:** "This systematic review aimed to synthesize pharmacological, clinical, safety, and preclinical behavioural evidence for these compounds."
- **Problem:** A systematic review synthesising existing evidence does not establish a new mechanism; the abstract explicitly lists dissociating neuroplasticity from hallucinogenic effects as an open question for future work.
- **Suggested fix:** "Novelty: Synthesis / emerging question".

### Study 4: Whether war damages your mental health may depend on what you already believed about the world
**PMID:** 42844775 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=326, parents of at least one child aged 3–6, mean age 36.75 (correctly rounded to 36.8), six weeks after the onset of the war, moderation by world assumptions, depression and somatization outcomes, and Greenblatt-Kimron's University of Haifa affiliation all match.

**Issue A. Headline and pitch imply pre-existing beliefs that were never measured** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Whether war damages your mental health may depend on what you already believed about the world" / "this study points at the reason: their prior assumptions about whether the world is fundamentally benign"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants completed questionnaires on background information, exposure to missile attacks, world assumptions, depression, and somatization six weeks after the onset of the Israel-Hamas war."
- **Problem:** World assumptions were measured concurrently with symptoms, six weeks into the war. The words "already believed," "prior assumptions" and "pre-existing beliefs" (in Why it matters) assert a temporal ordering the design cannot support — and directly contradict the digest's own caveat, "no baseline measures of pre-war beliefs or mood." "Points at the reason" also edges into causal language for a cross-sectional moderation analysis. The pitch's closing question partly rescues this, but the headline and setup have already made the claim.
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "How badly war affects your mental health may track with how you see the world." Pitch: "this study points at one correlate: how benign they believe the world to be, measured alongside their symptoms rather than before the war."

**Issue B. "Far weaker" link in the positive-assumptions group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "among those with more positive assumptions, the link was far weaker"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings of the moderation analyses revealed a strong positive relationship between exposure to missile attacks with symptoms of depression and somatization among parents holding negative world assumptions."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the high-negative-assumption simple slope only. A weaker association at the other end is the usual implication of a moderation, but the abstract does not state its magnitude or whether it was significant, so "far weaker" overspecifies.
- **Suggested fix:** "the association was strongest among parents holding negative world assumptions; the abstract does not report the size of the association among those with more positive assumptions."

**Issue C. Sample described as a convenience sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "non-probability online convenience sample"
- **Abstract says:** "A web-based national sample included 326 parents with at least one child aged 3-6 years."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls it a national web-based sample; whether recruitment was non-probability is unverifiable from abstract only. The caveat errs on the cautious side, which is preferable to the reverse, but it misstates what the paper claims.
- **Suggested fix:** "web-based national sample; recruitment method not specified in the abstract."

**Issue D. "Counterintuitive" novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Novelty: Counterintuitive"
- **Abstract says:** Frames the study as testing "the moderating role of world assumptions," i.e. an application of existing shattered-assumptions theory.
- **Problem:** The finding — that negative world assumptions amplify the stress–symptom link — is the direction the underlying theory predicts. Nothing in the abstract is counterintuitive.
- **Suggested fix:** "Novelty: Theory tested in a live conflict setting".

### Study 5: Not all internalized racism affects mental health the same way
**PMID:** 42829217 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Demographics are accurately rounded (N=205; mean age 27.96 → 28; 55.6% → 56% Black; 18.5% → 19% Asian; 14.6% → 15% multiracial), the four-facet structure is correctly described, and the split finding — affective/shame facets with anxious arousal, group devaluation with both anxiety and depression — matches the abstract. The digest appropriately declines to echo the abstract's clinical implications about cognitive restructuring, which would not be supportable from cross-sectional data. The 4/10 NS fit score is well calibrated.

**Issue A. Author first name not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Jesus Quiles at Florida State University"
- **Abstract says:** "Quiles JRG(1)… (1)Department of Psychology, Florida State University, Tallahassee, FL, USA"
- **Problem:** First name unverifiable from abstract only (the listed contact email is garcia@psy.fsu.edu, and the surname may be compound). Affiliation is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** Confirm the preferred full name and surname form from the published paper before contacting.

**Editor's note on patterns:** Two recurring habits account for most of the flags. First, contextual detail not present in the abstracts (training duration, inpatient status, single-centre design, eight-hour psilocybin sessions) is being presented in the same register as verified study facts — each of these needs either full-text sourcing or hedging before the pitch goes out. Second, the novelty labels are running ahead of the abstracts in three of five entries. On the plus side, the caveats sections are generally strong, industry funding in Study 3 is properly surfaced, and no entry converts observational or uncontrolled findings into a clinical recommendation. Study 4's headline is the single item I would hold until rewritten.
