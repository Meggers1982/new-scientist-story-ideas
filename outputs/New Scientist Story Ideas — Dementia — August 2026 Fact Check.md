### Study 1: Getting less frail in later life tracks with lower dementia risk — and depression looks like a big part of the link
**PMID:** 42600788 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

All headline statistics check out: N = 59,331; HRs 1.43–2.53 for worsening (= 43–153%); reversal HR 0.55 (SHARE) and 0.70 (CHARLS), null in ELSA/HRS; 5,629 incident dementia cases over 7.51 ± 2.09 years; mediation 29.29–44.78%. Framing is appropriately non-causal and the caveats are strong.

**Issue A. "Four continents"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "huge harmonized sample across four continents"
- **Abstract says:** Cohorts are HRS (USA), CHARLS (China), SHARE (Europe), ELSA (England).
- **Problem:** That is three continents (North America, Asia, Europe), not four — ELSA and SHARE are both European.
- **Suggested fix:** "huge harmonized sample across four cohorts on three continents"

**Issue B. Mediation attributed to the reversal pathway** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Depressive symptoms statistically mediated 29–45% of the frailty–dementia pathway." / "why depression seems to sit in the middle of the chain"
- **Abstract says:** "Depressive symptoms showed indirect associations with incident dementia for both baseline frailty and frailty worsening."
- **Problem:** The abstract locates the mediation in *baseline frailty* and *frailty worsening*, not in frailty improvement. The digest headline ("Getting less frail... depression looks like a big part of the link") and the pitch angle both attach the mediation finding to the reversal result, which the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Depressive symptoms statistically mediated 29–45% of the pathway from baseline frailty and frailty worsening to dementia."

**Issue C. Contact affiliation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call the Sun Yat-sen University nursing group (Zhang, Wu)"
- **Abstract says:** Wu X is at the "State Key Laboratory of Oncology in South China, Collaborative Innovation Center for Cancer Medicine, Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center"; Zhang M is at the School of Nursing.
- **Problem:** Wu is not in the nursing school; a writer cold-calling on that basis will misaddress the corresponding author.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call the corresponding authors — Meifen Zhang (Sun Yat-sen School of Nursing) and Xiaodan Wu (Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center)"

**NS fit:** 7/10 is defensible — nearly 60,000 participants, four harmonized longitudinal cohorts, incident dementia outcome.

### Study 2: Feeling older than your years shows up in a blood marker of nerve damage
**PMID:** 42594533 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Numbers verified: N = 3,401, mean age 68.17, Simoa assays in 2016, β = .07 (subjective age) and β = .06 (SPA) with NfL, null for Aβ42/40, p-tau181 and GFAP, subjective-age link surviving full covariate adjustment. Framing correctly avoids causal claims and the pitch explicitly warns against them.

**Issue A. NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — a striking psychology-to-biomarker link in a large national cohort, tempered by small effect sizes and cross-sectional design."
- **Abstract says:** β = .07 and β = .06 for the only significant associations; biomarkers measured at a single wave; exposure entirely self-reported.
- **Problem:** A 7 implies a fairly strong story. The whole result rests on two standardized betas under 0.10, a self-reported exposure, and no temporal ordering — and three of the four biomarkers were null. The sample size is the only thing carrying the score.
- **Suggested fix:** Score 6/10 — "a novel psychology-to-biomarker signal in a large national cohort, but the effects are very small, the exposure is self-reported, and the design is cross-sectional."

### Study 3: An atlas of how often psychiatric and neurological disorders travel together — built from 42 million people
**PMID:** 41895003 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Prevalence figures all match the abstract (dementia–depression 25.01%; epilepsy–depression 23.1% and ADHD 22.3%; MS anxiety 36% and depression 27.01%; bipolar–migraine 34.8%), as does "no outcomes reached strong credibility."

**Issue A. "42 million people"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "built from 42 million people" / "N = 42,464,788"
- **Abstract says:** "We included 81 meta-analyses (N=42,464,788, k=2,273)"
- **Problem:** That N is the summed sample across 81 separate meta-analyses of 2,273 studies; the same individuals will be counted in multiple meta-analyses. It is not 42 million distinct people.
- **Suggested fix:** "built from meta-analyses covering more than 42 million participant records"

**Issue B. Outcomes described as unknowable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "we know how common these pairings are but not reliably what they do to people's trajectories" / pitch: "nobody can tell you what that means for them"
- **Abstract says:** "No outcomes reached strong credibility criteria."
- **Problem:** Failing to reach *strong* credibility is not the same as no evidence at all — the grading scheme includes moderate and weaker tiers, and the abstract notes several associations at lower credibility. "Nobody can tell you" overstates the void.
- **Suggested fix:** "we know how common these pairings are, but no estimate of what comorbidity does to prognosis reached the top credibility tier"

**Issue C. Incomplete conflict disclosure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "One author declares extensive pharmaceutical honoraria (AbbVie, Angelini, Lundbeck, Otsuka, Teva among others)."
- **Abstract says:** "Marco Solmi holds shares of MESAS, S2M."
- **Problem:** The Caveats capture the honoraria but omit an equity holding, which is a distinct and generally more significant category of conflict.
- **Suggested fix:** Add "...and holds equity in two companies (MESAS, S2M)."

### Study 4: Caregivers get more upset during a conflict conversation when their partner has frontotemporal dementia — and that predicts their own depression
**PMID:** 42593973 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

Method and results are accurately rendered: N = 62, three diagnostic groups, 10-minute unrehearsed conflict conversation, rating-dial continuous affect ratings, bvFTD lowest prosocial IER and greatest caregiver negative-affect increases, association with depression but not anxiety.

**Issue A. "Predicts" in the headline** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and that predicts their own depression"
- **Abstract says:** "greater increases in caregiver negativity during the interaction were associated with greater caregiver depression (but not anxiety)"
- **Problem:** Depression was reported by caregivers at the same time as the lab session; nothing here is prospective. "Predicts" implies temporal precedence the design cannot deliver, and it contradicts the digest's own caveat that the study is cross-sectional. The body text ("were tied to") is correct — the headline is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "— and that tracks with their own depression"

### Study 5: Most autistic adults with higher support needs are taking medicines linked to memory decline
**PMID:** 42592704 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Verified: N = 512 for medication data, ages 18–68, mean ~31, SPARK Research Match recruitment, 66.41% on ≥1 anticholinergic, 31% at clinically meaningful levels, associations with caregiver-reported memory problems and cognitive-decline screener after controlling for age and birth sex. The "first look at this population" claim matches the abstract. The pitch's explicit "not as advice to stop any medication" line is exactly right.

**Issue A. Headline generalizes a volunteer sample to the population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most autistic adults with higher support needs are taking medicines linked to memory decline"
- **Abstract says:** "Caregivers of autistic adults (18–68 years; Mean ~31 years) recruited via Simons Powering Autism Research's (SPARK) Research Match service... The majority (66.41%) of autistic adults were taking at least one AC medication"
- **Problem:** The headline states a population-level prevalence from a self-selected research-registry sample. The Caveats acknowledge this, but the headline as written does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Two-thirds of autistic adults with higher support needs in a large US registry are taking medicines linked to memory decline"

### Study 6: Dementia with Lewy bodies splits into two diseases that start at opposite ends of the brain
**PMID:** 42522580 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The trajectory descriptions, subtype clinical features, cingulate island sign finding, N = 83, 15 biomarkers, dual-phase 18F-FP-CIT and 18F-FBB PET, SuStaIn model and the 10 unclassifiable patients are all accurate.

**Issue A. "Two diseases"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dementia with Lewy bodies splits into two diseases that start at opposite ends of the brain"
- **Abstract says:** "we delineate two distinct multimodal imaging progression patterns in DLB... These findings highlight the clinical and biological heterogeneity within DLB."
- **Problem:** The authors describe two imaging-defined subtypes/trajectories within one disease entity, not two diseases. The word "diseases" implies a nosological split the data do not establish, especially without pathological confirmation (which the Caveats correctly note is absent).
- **Suggested fix:** "Dementia with Lewy bodies splits into two subtypes that start at opposite ends of the brain"

### Study 7: A plasma biomarker of Alzheimer's adds nothing to a memory test in people with Down syndrome
**PMID:** 42138095 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

N = 62, age > 45, three diagnostic groups, the marker panel and the classification accuracies (88.0% / 75.0% / 93.8%) are all correct, as is p-tau217's standout role in the regressions.

**Issue A. Omits the authors' pro-combination conclusion** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this is an early warning that in a population at very high genetic risk — where the test's promise is greatest — a well-chosen memory test still carries the diagnostic load."
- **Abstract says:** "The combination of cognitive and plasma markers for the detection of prodromal AD cases in DS appeared to be highly effective."
- **Problem:** The digest builds a deflationary "blood test loses to memory test" angle while omitting that the authors' own stated conclusion endorses combining cognitive and plasma markers. The specific null is about *incremental* improvement over verbal memory, not about p-tau217 being useless. A pitch built purely on the deflation misrepresents the paper's stance and could be embarrassing if an editor reads the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "...a well-chosen memory test still carries much of the diagnostic load — though the authors themselves conclude that combining cognitive and plasma markers is highly effective for detecting prodromal cases."

**Issue B. "Adds nothing"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "adds nothing to a memory test"
- **Abstract says:** "the inclusion of p-tau217 failed to produce any significant improvement in the diagnostic model based on verbal memory tasks"
- **Problem:** "Adds nothing" reads as zero contribution; the finding is no *statistically significant* incremental gain in a 62-person sample where power is limited.
- **Suggested fix:** "adds no significant diagnostic gain over a memory test"

### Study 8: Apathy comes in at least four flavors — and the pattern differs between depression and dementia
**PMID:** 42607410 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 328 (MDD 149, MND 179), the instrument battery, and all four profile labels and proportions (32.9% / 36.6% / 22.0% / 8.5%) match, as do the diagnostic skews of each profile. The speaker-fee conflict is disclosed in Caveats, and the "requires replication" framing is consistent with a single cross-sectional latent profile analysis. NS fit of 5 is appropriate.

### Study 9: Leaving work early is linked to a slightly higher dementia risk — across 2.4 million people
**PMID:** 41885755 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Cohort sizes (1,195,611 vs 1,227,084), age band 45–64, database and years, lag-time exclusions, aHR 1.04 (1.01–1.07) for dementia and 1.16 for alcohol use disorders, and the weak/null results for depression, anxiety and sleep are all correct. Notably, the digest correctly declines to repeat the abstract's own causal conclusion ("Early labor market exit increases the risk of dementia") — good practice.

**Issue A. "The effect is real"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Early retirement and the brain: the effect is real, and it is tiny."
- **Abstract says:** "Early labor market exit was associated with higher risks of dementia (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] = 1.04, 95% CI = 1.01–1.07)"
- **Problem:** "Real" implies a causal effect in a headline where the underlying evidence is an aHR of 1.04 whose confidence interval nearly touches 1.0 and which the digest's own caveats say is only partly protected against reverse causation. An association this small in administrative data is exactly the kind that residual confounding can generate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Early retirement and the brain: the association is statistically detectable, and it is tiny."

### Study 10: Brain training that changes the brain but not behavior
**PMID:** 42259152 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

Verified: PRISMA 2020, ten RCTs 2009–2024, samples 30–160, ages 64–82, six studies with significant post-intervention brain changes (mostly functional; DMN, frontoparietal, hippocampal), six with no significant behavioral improvement, in-person > online/hybrid for neuroimaging effects, high risk of bias in seven.

**Issue A. Generalizing language-based research interventions to the consumer brain-training market** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It sharpens an uncomfortable question for the whole brain-training industry" / "the app format is precisely the one that shows the weakest neural signal."
- **Abstract says:** "randomized controlled trials evaluating neuroimaging outcomes following linguistic-cognitive interventions"; "Neuroimaging effects were more frequent in MCI/AD and in in-person versus online/hybrid interventions."
- **Problem:** These were research-delivered linguistic-cognitive stimulation programmes across healthy aging to AD, not commercial brain-training products. "Online/hybrid delivery" in ten heterogeneous trials is not the same as "the app format," and nothing here tests any consumer product. The wider angle promises a consumer investigation the evidence base cannot underwrite.
- **Suggested fix:** "It sharpens an uncomfortable question for cognitive-stimulation research: if connectivity shifts but people do not get measurably better at anything, what has the intervention achieved? (Note for the wider angle: these were research-delivered language-based programmes, not commercial brain-training apps, and the in-person/online contrast is based on delivery format within trials.)"

### Study 11: German doctors are prescribing more antipsychotics for dementia, not fewer
**PMID:** 42586160 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

All figures verified: 389 general and 77 specialist practices, 2016–2025, IQVIA Disease Analyzer, antidementia 14.7%→17.3% (GPs) and 49.6%→40.9% (specialists), antipsychotics up in both sectors, antidepressants stable, benzodiazepines/Z-drugs down. Industry involvement (IQVIA employee author; Biogen and Dr. Willmar Schwabe honoraria) is properly disclosed in Caveats.

**Issue A. "Chemical restraint" framing contradicts the digest's own caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "on why chemical restraint in dementia care has proved so resistant to national deprescribing drives"
- **Abstract says:** "The rising use of antipsychotics despite known risks highlights the importance of BPSD and regular benefit-risk assessment."
- **Problem:** The dataset records only whether a prescription was issued — no indication, dose or duration — and the digest's own Caveats state it "cannot distinguish appropriate from inappropriate prescribing." Labelling the whole trend "chemical restraint" asserts precisely the distinction the data cannot make, and the authors attribute the rise partly to behavioural and psychological symptom burden.
- **Suggested fix:** "on why antipsychotic prescribing in dementia care has proved so resistant to national deprescribing drives"

### Study 12: Early dementia is easier to spot in people with intellectual disability who talk more
**PMID:** 42311142 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The core counterintuitive finding is correctly reported: higher DSQIID scores associated with *higher* semantic verbal fluency; age and Down syndrome status associated with higher scores; N = 322 (55 DS, 267 non-DS); instruments (DSQIID, TSI, BPT, semantic fluency) accurate. The "Why it matters" interpretation matches the authors' own conclusion about caregivers detecting change more readily in adults with better verbal capacity.

**Issue A. Cohort duration claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Eimear McGlinchey or Mary McCarron at Trinity College Dublin's IDS-TILDA team, which has followed this cohort for over a decade."
- **Abstract says:** Describes a cross-sectional study within IDS-TILDA; no follow-up duration is given.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The duration claim is plausible background but is not sourced from this paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "...at Trinity College Dublin's IDS-TILDA team (confirm the cohort's start date and follow-up length before printing)."

### Study 13: In Parkinson's, the movement network quietly decouples from thinking networks
**PMID:** 42178093 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. The staged connectivity findings (DMN/SMN/DAN declines at PD-MCI; limbic and frontoparietal added at PD-D), the ACE-R interaction result (positive association between cognitive performance and connectivity strength), the SMN decoupling emphasis and the network-based statistics method all match the abstract. The Caveats correctly note that group sizes are not reported in the abstract and that the "trajectory" is inferred cross-sectionally. NS fit of 4 is appropriately modest.

### Study 14: Tirzepatide and the brain: plenty of mechanism, almost no human trial data
**PMID:** 42608956 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The evidence tally (22 studies: 11 preclinical, 3 retrospective cohorts, 8 case reports), the preclinical mechanisms, the cohort findings including the vertigo/vestibular neuronitis signal, the adverse-event case reports, the five databases and the September 2025 search cutoff are all accurate. The framing as an evidence audit rather than a discovery is exactly right for this material.

**Issue A. "11 animal studies"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "11 animal studies, three chart reviews and eight case reports"
- **Abstract says:** "11 preclinical investigations"; quality assessed with "SYRCLE... and CAMARADE for in vivo studies, ToxRTool for in vitro"
- **Problem:** The preclinical set includes in vitro work, not only animal studies — the abstract explicitly lists an in vitro appraisal tool. Calling all 11 "animal studies" is inaccurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "11 preclinical studies (animal and cell-based), three chart reviews and eight case reports"

**Issue B. Absolute claim about randomized evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "No randomized neurological endpoint data exists."
- **Abstract says:** "Prospective trials with standardized neurological endpoints, active-comparator designs, and structured safety monitoring are warranted."
- **Problem:** The review found none within its inclusion criteria and search window (to 30 September 2025); that is not the same as a universal claim that none exists. Unverifiable as an absolute statement from this abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "No randomized trials with neurological endpoints met the review's inclusion criteria (search to September 2025)."

### Study 15: Forty years of evidence that stress and unhappy relationships age the immune system
**PMID:** 42566682 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The summary of the review's content — exam stress, laboratory stressors, marital discord, cancer survivorship, dementia caregiving; infections, vaccine responses, wound healing, inflammation, cellular aging; depression sensitizing immune function; the recent extension to gut microbiome and intestinal permeability — is faithful to the abstract. The Caveats correctly identify it as a narrative review. NS fit of 4 is honest about it being synthesis rather than new result.

**Issue A. Named specific studies not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "built around the studies that defined the field — the blister-wound healing experiments in quarrelling couples, the caregiver vaccine-response work"
- **Abstract says:** "Across studies of examination stress, laboratory stressors, marital discord, cancer survivorship, and dementia caregiving, psychosocial adversity predicts... slower wound healing..."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The abstract names domains, not individual experimental paradigms; the blister-wound protocol is not mentioned.
- **Suggested fix:** "built around the paradigms that defined the field — the wound-healing work in couples during marital conflict, the caregiver vaccine-response studies (verify specific study designs from the full text before pitching)."
