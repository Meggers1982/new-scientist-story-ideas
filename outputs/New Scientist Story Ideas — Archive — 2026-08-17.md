### 1. People who have used psychedelics show better cognitive flexibility — even without recent use

**PMID:** 42580581 | **DOI:** 10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.111888

**NS fit:** 8/10 — Large student sample, counterintuitive finding (better cognition in psychedelic users), strong societal hook around psychedelic therapeutics, but observational design and self-report limit causal inference.
**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**The study:** Researchers at the University of British Columbia surveyed 1,342 university students, comparing executive functioning — measured via an online Wisconsin Card Sorting Task — between those who had ever used classic psychedelics (n=224 lifetime users, n=114 recent users) and non-users. Strikingly, the benefit didn't grow with more use, suggesting it may plateau quickly. At a moment when psilocybin therapy is being legalised in several jurisdictions, this raises a pointed question: is cognitive enhancement part of why these treatments work? The finding raises the provocative idea that treating anxiety before surgery could reduce opioid burden — and that the type of opioid prescribed may interact with psychological state in ways medicine hasn't fully mapped. ### 12. Whether numbers feel 'left' or 'right' in your mind depends on what task you're doing, not a fixed mental map

**NS fit:** 6/10 — Counterintuitive finding about a well-known cognitive phenomenon earns a boost, but the highly technical framing, missing sample size, and niche topic (numerical cognition) limit NS Mind appeal compared to studies on relationships, sleep, or mental health.
**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**The study:** Researchers used behavioural experiments and EEG (event-related potentials) to examine whether the well-known SNARC effect — the tendency to associate small numbers with left space and large numbers with right space — is a fixed mental representation or a flexible one. Participants completed two tasks (parity judgment and magnitude comparison) while brain activity was recorded; sample size is not reported in the abstract.
