### Study 1: Anxiety and depression fell among Ukrainian civilians even as the shelling got worse
**PMID:** 42831241 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for an observational predictor** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It also flags disrupted sleep as the thing that blocks that recalibration, which is a plausible, testable target for wartime mental health support."
- **Abstract says:** "Higher baseline sleep disturbance predicted smaller reductions in anxiety and depression… highlighting sleep as a potentially important target for mental health support under prolonged war-related stress."
- **Problem:** "The thing that blocks that recalibration" converts a prospective association into a mechanism. The study is observational with two waves; baseline sleep disturbance could be a marker of severity, comorbidity, or exposure rather than a blocking factor. The digest's own Caveats note the observational design, so the body text contradicts it here.
- **Suggested fix:** "It also identifies disrupted sleep as the baseline characteristic most consistently associated with smaller symptom reductions — which the authors suggest is a plausible, testable target for wartime mental health support."

**Issue B. "Strongest predictor" overstates the abstract's wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The strongest predictor of who failed to improve was baseline sleep disturbance"
- **Abstract says:** "Sleep disturbance emerged as a robust predictor of less favourable symptom change" / plain language summary: "the most consistent predictor of less favourable symptom change."
- **Problem:** "Most consistent" (i.e. predicted both anxiety and depression change) is not the same as "strongest" (largest effect size). No effect sizes are given in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "The most consistent predictor of who failed to improve was baseline sleep disturbance"

**Issue C. Internal inconsistency on the interval** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "scoring lower on anxiety than they did five months earlier"
- **Abstract says:** "a pivotal six-month period in 2023"; waves in June 2023 and November 2023.
- **Problem:** The body text correctly says "those six months"; the pitch angle says "five months earlier." Pick the abstract's framing to avoid an inconsistency a sub-editor will query.
- **Suggested fix:** "scoring lower on anxiety than they did six months earlier"

**Issue D. NS fit score of 7 on a small self-report sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — a genuinely surprising prospective result from an under-studied natural experiment, held back by a small, skewed online sample."
- **Abstract says:** "two-wave online longitudinal study of 208 Ukrainian civilian adults (80.8% female; mean age 29.4 years)"; all outcomes self-reported; no control condition.
- **Problem:** A 208-person, 81%-female online convenience sample with two self-report waves and no pre-war baseline is thin for a 7. The counterintuitive angle is strong, but the evidence base is not. The score note already concedes the weakness, which argues for lowering the number rather than annotating it.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — a genuinely surprising prospective result from an under-studied natural experiment, but a small, skewed, self-report online sample limits how hard the finding can be pushed."

### Study 2: Young men describe sex as a hazard to defuse rather than something to enjoy
**PMID:** 42844656 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Shifts from… to" misstates an additive finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the operative question shifts from 'does she want this?' to 'am I the kind of man who could get this wrong?'"
- **Abstract says:** "the question when navigating consent is often not only 'does she want this?' but 'am I the kind of man who could get this wrong?'"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a second question layered on top of the first, not a replacement of it. The digest's version implies the men stopped attending to the partner's wishes, which is a materially different and more provocative claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "the operative question is often not only 'does she want this?' but also 'am I the kind of man who could get this wrong?'"

**Issue B. Quantifying a qualitative finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "shame that came as much from self-criticism as from anyone else's accusation" and "the harshest accuser in the room is usually the man himself"
- **Abstract says:** "shame arose not only from accusations by others, but also from self-criticism."
- **Problem:** "As much as" and "usually" assign relative weight and frequency that 31 thematically analyzed interviews cannot support, and the digest's own Caveats state that "no prevalence claim is possible." The body and pitch text contradict that caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** Body: "and shame that arose not only from others' accusations but also from self-criticism." Pitch: "and that for some of these men the accuser they described most vividly was themselves."

**Issue C. Causal framing of participants' own attribution** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a cultural shift intended to make sex safer has, for some men, produced a vigilance state that crowds out pleasure entirely"
- **Abstract says:** "They attributed this wariness to the cultural climate that surrounds consent" and "a cultural suspicion of men appears to have been internalized by some young men."
- **Problem:** The causal link is the participants' own interpretation, reported in interviews — not something the design can measure. The digest's Caveats say exactly this, so the pitch angle contradicts its own caveat. "Crowds out pleasure entirely" also hardens "how rarely these men described enjoying sex" into an absolute.
- **Suggested fix:** "these men themselves link their wariness to the cultural climate around consent — and describe a vigilance state in which they rarely mentioned enjoying sex at all"

**Issue D. Author first name not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Jess Forrest at the Melbourne School of Public Health"
- **Abstract says:** "Forrest J(1)… (1)Melbourne School of Public Health, University of Melbourne, Melbourne, Australia."
- **Problem:** The affiliation is correct, but the given name "Jess" is unverifiable from abstract only. Confirm before the pitch goes out.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call lead author J. Forrest at the Melbourne School of Public Health, University of Melbourne [confirm first name]"

### Study 3: Reminding people of death makes them want children — and the effect is strongest in the chronically ill
**PMID:** 42833108 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The headline's causal phrasing is defensible here — these are randomized priming experiments, not observational data — and "want children" correctly tracks the measured outcome (intentions), which the Caveats reinforce. The treatment of the untested mediational chain, the replication problem with mortality-salience work, and the unreported sample sizes is accurate and appropriately hedged.

**Issue A. Participant nationality not stated in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across three experiments with Chinese adults — including a sample of people with chronic illnesses"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants included general adults and individuals with chronic illnesses." Author affiliations are Shandong Normal University and Henan University of Engineering, China.
- **Problem:** Chinese samples are a reasonable inference from the affiliations and the paper's pronatalist-policy framing, but the abstract never states participant nationality. Unverifiable from abstract only — and the Caveats lean on it ("single-country (Chinese) samples").
- **Suggested fix:** "Across three experiments run by a Chinese research group — including a sample of people with chronic illnesses" [confirm participant nationality from full text]
