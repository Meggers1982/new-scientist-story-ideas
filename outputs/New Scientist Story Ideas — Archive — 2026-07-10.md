**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**The study:** Researchers at universities in Switzerland, Germany, and the US showed participants images of snapping fingers, tapping feet, and static noses that were continuously suppressed from conscious visual awareness using a technique called continuous flash suppression, while tracking eye gaze. The study suggests that executing an action strengthens the brain's representation of related body-part images enough to guide eye movements without any conscious awareness.

**Why it matters:** The findings suggest that the body's movements can shape visual attention at a level below conscious perception, with potential implications for understanding how action and awareness interact in everyday movement, rehabilitation, and human-computer interaction.

Researchers used a technique that hides images from awareness entirely, yet still found reliable eye movements towards action-relevant stimuli. The finding suggests the brain quietly boosts the salience of body-related imagery during movement, operating a form of attention entirely beneath the radar of consciousness.
