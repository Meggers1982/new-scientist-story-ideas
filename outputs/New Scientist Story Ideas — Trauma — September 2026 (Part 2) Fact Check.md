### Study 1: Many adults who score high on autism questionnaires may be describing the effects of anxiety and a hard childhood
**PMID:** 42772961 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Sample (245 consecutive NHS first adult assessments), median age 33, 52% female, 56.7% diagnosed, 35% of "experienced autism" adults below threshold (64/181), internalising link "regardless of CA", ACE–female sex association — all match the abstract. Caveats correctly reproduce the authors' "hypothesis-generating" language on maltreatment.

**Issue A. Headline gives childhood adversity equal billing with anxiety** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Many adults who score high on autism questionnaires may be describing the effects of anxiety and a hard childhood"
- **Abstract says:** "Cumulative ACE scores did not distinguish EA/CA groups but were independently associated with female sex (p = .012)."
- **Problem:** The internalising (GAD-7/PHQ-9) association with high self-reported traits is strong and explicit (all p ⩽ .002); the adversity component is not — cumulative ACE scores did *not* separate the groups. The authors' lay abstract does use similar phrasing, so this is an echo of the paper rather than an invention, but a headline that pairs anxiety and "a hard childhood" as co-equal explanations runs ahead of the statistics.
- **Suggested fix:** "Many adults who score high on autism questionnaires may be describing the effects of anxiety and low mood"

**Note on NS fit (7/10):** Defensible. The outcome (autism diagnosis) is clinician-determined rather than self-reported, N is respectable and the topic is live. No change recommended.

### Study 2: A genetic hunt for resilience finds a single variant that tracks who stays well after trauma
**PMID:** 42765393 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Cohort size (12,946), mean age 52.2, 58.5% female, PHQ-9 at pre-surgery and 1/6/12 months, 18.0% resilient, rs11766511 mapped to *SDK1*, replication in 2,285 CSTC patients, GG vs AA genotype-stratified result, non-significant additive model — all accurate, and the caveats correctly flag the additive-model failure.

**Issue A. P-value shorthand misstates the reported statistic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "get an independent geneticist to say plainly how much weight one 5×10⁻⁸ hit can bear"
- **Abstract says:** "GWAS identified a lead single-nucleotide polymorphism (rs11766511, P = 3.082 × 10-8)"
- **Problem:** 5×10⁻⁸ is the conventional genome-wide significance *threshold*, not this study's P value. Written as "one 5×10⁻⁸ hit" a writer could reproduce it as the result.
- **Suggested fix:** "…how much weight one hit at P = 3.1×10⁻⁸ — barely past the genome-wide threshold — can bear"

**Issue B. NS fit score sits on the weakest part of the paper** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — a genuinely novel way of defining resilience… the genetic signal itself is fragile."
- **Problem:** The score note justifies 7 on the methodology, but the headline sells the variant. The variant survives replication only under genotype stratification, the additive model is null, and the authors themselves say the locus "warrants confirmation." A 7 attached to a headline about a single unconfirmed candidate locus is optimistic.
- **Suggested fix:** Score 6/10, or keep 7/10 only if the headline is rewritten around the measurement method ("A new way of measuring resilience: doing better than your own predicted trajectory").

### Study 3: People who struggle to name their feelings think their coping is working — and it isn't
**PMID:** 42721324 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 49, three EMA waves, five prompts/day for five days across three months, 2,420 observations, lagged finding that perceived regulation success predicted lower next-assessment emotional clarity among high-alexithymia participants, and hiding + perceived success predicting worsening PTSS — all match.

**Issue A. Headline asserts what the authors say they cannot determine** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People who struggle to name their feelings think their coping is working — and it isn't"
- **Abstract says:** "whether it reflects miscalibrated evaluation, divergent regulatory goals, or systematic reporting biases cannot be determined from self-report alone"
- **Problem:** "And it isn't" states that the regulation genuinely failed. The data show a self-reported paradox; the authors explicitly decline to say which of three explanations it is. The pitch text handles this well, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "People who struggle to name their feelings say their coping is working — just before things get worse"

**Issue B. Prevalence figure not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an under-discussed trait affecting roughly one in ten people"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. Commonly cited, but it needs sourcing before it reaches a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** Attribute to a named prevalence source or drop the figure.

### Study 4: Making patients relive their trauma works outside the trial setting too — and almost nobody got worse
**PMID:** 42766476 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

N = 95, CAPS-5-confirmed PTSD, mean age 35.65, 76% women, 61% sexual trauma index event, mean 8 sessions, 75.53% probable remission, 79.78% clinically significant improvement, 1% reliable worsening, 9.47% dropout, waiting-list d = 0.33 — all accurately rendered. Industry/royalty disclosures are correctly carried into Caveats.

**Issue A. Headline claims efficacy from an uncontrolled naturalistic study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Making patients relive their trauma works outside the trial setting too"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings suggest that PE may be feasibly and safely delivered in routine outpatient care and may be associated with substantial reductions in PTSD symptoms."
- **Problem:** "Works" is an efficacy claim. The design is single-arm and naturalistic; the waiting-list contrast is within-subject, not randomized, so it cannot exclude regression to the mean, natural recovery or expectancy. The authors twice hedge with "may."
- **Suggested fix:** "Exposure therapy delivered safely in an ordinary French clinic — and almost nobody got worse"

**Issue B. Novelty label does not match what the study did** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research"
- **Abstract says:** PE has "well-established efficacy in randomised controlled trials. However, its implementation in routine care remains limited."
- **Problem:** The study is consistent with, not contrary to, prior research; what it challenges is clinician practice and belief. "Overturns prior research" would mislead an editor about the paper's standing.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Confirms in the real world / challenges clinical practice"

**Issue C. Cause of the implementation gap asserted, not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clinician reluctance, not lack of evidence, is the main barrier to trauma-focused therapy reaching patients"
- **Abstract says:** Implementation "remains limited, particularly in outpatient clinics where evidence-based psychotherapies are not widely adopted."
- **Problem:** The study did not measure why clinicians do or don't deliver PE. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Trauma-focused therapy remains under-delivered in routine outpatient care; this study suggests safety concerns are not a good reason for that."

### Study 5: In Gaza, 94% of adults screen above the normal range for psychological distress
**PMID:** 42728821 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

August 2025 survey, 894 adults, WHOQOL-BREF and PHQ-4, environmental 25.91 and psychological 35.60, 94.3% above normal range, 64.9%/65.5% probable anxiety/depression, forced relocations associated with lower QoL across all four domains — all accurate.

**Issue A. Pitch inverts the abstract's emphasis on distress** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "distress explained only a quarter to two-fifths of the damage — meaning most of the harm is structural, not psychological, and no amount of therapy substitutes for housing"
- **Abstract says:** "psychological distress was the strongest and most consistent predictor of reduced QoL across all domains (β range: -1.67 to -2.28…)" and "Integrated humanitarian responses that address both structural restoration and scalable mental health interventions are urgently needed."
- **Problem:** The 26–41% figures describe only the mediated portion of the *resource-loss* pathway; they are not a share of total harm. The authors name distress as the strongest single predictor of QoL and call for both structural and mental health responses. The pitch converts a partial-mediation statistic into a policy conclusion the paper argues against.
- **Suggested fix:** "Distress accounted for roughly a quarter to two-fifths of the link between cumulative loss and quality of life — so resource loss also damaged wellbeing directly, and the authors argue structural restoration and mental health support are both needed."

**Issue B. Headline generalizes a convenience sample to a population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In Gaza, 94% of adults screen above the normal range for psychological distress"
- **Abstract says:** "cross-sectional survey in August 2025 among 894 adults across the Gaza Strip… 94.3% scoring above the normal range"
- **Problem:** The headline reads as a population estimate; the digest's own Caveats state the sample "cannot be assumed representative." Headline and caveat contradict each other.
- **Suggested fix:** "In a survey of nearly 900 adults in Gaza, 94% screened above the normal range for psychological distress"

**Issue C. Mediation percentages conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "psychological distress statistically mediated 26–41% of the link between cumulative resource loss and quality of life"
- **Abstract says:** "accounting for 26% of the association with overall QoL and 29%-41% of the association with domain-specific outcomes"
- **Problem:** 26% is the overall-QoL figure; the domain-specific range starts at 29%. Presented as a single 26–41% band it slightly misdescribes the analysis.
- **Suggested fix:** "mediated 26% of the link with overall quality of life and 29–41% of the domain-specific links"

### Study 6: Recovery from disaster does not creep up on people — it arrives in sudden jumps
**PMID:** 42715666 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

N = 81, 5–8.5 months after Hurricane Florence, six weeks of EMA, functional data analysis plus generalized additive modelling, coevolution in 91–96%, ~6% self-determination violation and ~6% resurgence — all accurate, and the NS fit note and Caveats both flag the 6%.

**Issue A. Headline states as general what the study found in a small minority** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Recovery from disaster does not creep up on people — it arrives in sudden jumps"
- **Abstract says:** "approximately 6% of survivors exhibited nonlinear transitions consistent with SRST's self-determination violation and an additional 6% exhibited nonlinear transitions consistent with SRST's self-determination resurgence."
- **Problem:** Roughly 88% of participants showed neither abrupt transition. The headline asserts the opposite of what the data show for most survivors, and the abstract itself calls the support "preliminary." The pitch text correctly says "for a minority of survivors," so headline and body are inconsistent.
- **Suggested fix:** "For some disaster survivors, recovery doesn't creep up — it arrives in a sudden jump"

### Study 7: Everyday racist stereotypes track how much physical pain Black women report — independently of their mental health
**PMID:** 42721338 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 415, ordinal logistic regression, association of gendered racial microaggressions with both pain intensity and pain interference, persistence after controlling for depression, anxiety, stress and trauma symptoms, and the Angry Black Woman Stereotype as the strongest dimension all match the abstract. "Track" and "associated with" keep the framing correlational, the pitch explicitly warns against causal reading, and the Caveats cover cross-sectional design and self-report.

### Study 8: Virtual reality role-play helps adults with difficult childhoods read faces again
**PMID:** 42765386 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

Attrition figures (32 baseline, 25 completing the 8-week intervention, 24 at 18 weeks), eight weekly one-hour sessions, BLERT g = 1.04 at 12 weeks, persistence at 18 weeks, qualitative acceptability — all accurate. Freeman's co-authorship is correct.

**Issue A. Headline makes a causal efficacy claim from a single-arm pilot** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Virtual reality role-play helps adults with difficult childhoods read faces again"
- **Abstract says:** "This single-arm proof-of-concept study… SOCIAbLE was observed to be feasible and acceptable and showed preliminary evidence of benefits… warrant further investigation in a full randomized controlled trial."
- **Problem:** "Helps" attributes the improvement to the intervention. With no control arm, practice effects on the BLERT, expectancy and regression to the mean are all live alternatives — which is precisely why the authors call for an RCT. The pitch correctly says "proof of concept," so the headline undercuts the body.
- **Suggested fix:** "A VR role-play pilot improved emotion recognition in adults with childhood trauma — enough to justify a proper trial"

**Issue B. "First attempt" and "read faces" both slightly overstate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first attempt to train it directly" / "read faces again"
- **Abstract says:** "these difficulties are rarely therapeutically targeted"; the primary outcome is "BLERT emotional recognition"
- **Problem:** "Rarely targeted" is not "never attempted." The BLERT presents emotion across face, voice and gesture, so "read faces" narrows the outcome measure.
- **Suggested fix:** "difficulties that are rarely targeted therapeutically… improved emotion recognition"

### Study 9: Shame, not guilt, is what cuts trauma survivors off from the people around them
**PMID:** 42766479 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

N = 3,419, Arabic-speaking treatment-seeking women with sexual violence histories registering for an online intervention, β = −0.21 for PTSD–social support, shame as dominant mediator, guilt's small opposite-direction indirect effect, no significant self-blame effect — all accurate.

**Issue A. Headline is causal and generalized beyond the study population** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Shame, not guilt, is what cuts trauma survivors off from the people around them"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings suggest that trauma-related shame may play a role in the relationship between PTSD symptoms and perceived social support among women with a history of sexual violence exposure"
- **Problem:** Two problems in one line: "cuts… off" is causal language for a cross-sectional mediation, and "trauma survivors" extends a finding from Arabic-speaking female sexual-violence survivors seeking online treatment to survivors in general. The pitch text says "the design cannot show shame causes withdrawal," so the headline contradicts the digest's own guidance. Note also the outcome is *perceived* social support, not actual isolation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Shame, not guilt, tracks with feeling unsupported in women who have survived sexual violence"

**Issue B. Sample described as displaced and refugee women** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "what a 3,400-strong self-referral queue says about unmet need among displaced and refugee women"
- **Abstract says:** "Arabic-speaking, treatment-seeking women with a history of sexual violence exposure who registered for an online intervention"
- **Problem:** Displacement/refugee status is not reported in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "unmet need among Arabic-speaking women survivors"

### Study 10: When a mother's trauma reaches her child, it travels through the stress of parenting
**PMID:** 42760574 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 200 Syrian refugee dyads in Mardin, children 6–11, PCL-5/PSI-SF/BDI-II/GAD-7/CBCL plus child Stroop, significant mediated effects on CBCL Total Problems and Stroop interference with covariates, 30-dyad qualitative subsample, buffers including warmth, school support and religious coping, single author — all accurate.

**Issue A. Headline asserts a causal relay** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When a mother's trauma reaches her child, it travels through the stress of parenting"
- **Abstract says:** "parenting stress may represent an important relational pathway linking maternal trauma-related distress with children's… functioning"
- **Problem:** "Travels through" presents a cross-sectional statistical mediation as an established mechanism with a direction of flow. The body text and Caveats are careful; the headline is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "A mother's trauma and her child's attention: parenting stress may be the link"

### Study 11: The trauma-exposed brain's sensory-to-thinking hierarchy is reorganized — and differently in teenagers
**PMID:** 42740407 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

86 non-comorbid PTSD vs 86 trauma-exposed non-PTSD controls from the same earthquake, principal unimodal-to-transmodal gradient abnormalities, focal changes in visual, limbic, ventral attention and frontoparietal networks, no sex effect, subcortical decrease more prominent in adolescents, transcriptomic enrichment and neurotransmitter colocalization — all accurate. The Caveats correctly note that the gene-expression and receptor maps are spatial correlations with external atlases.

**Issue A. "Adults" and "adolescents" cannot both be right** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers compared 86 adults with non-comorbid PTSD following an earthquake" … "subcortical gradient reductions were more pronounced in adolescents than adults"
- **Abstract says:** "86 non-comorbid adult patients with PTSD caused by an earthquake" … "gradient decrease in the subcortical network is more prominent in adolescents with PTSD than adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract itself is internally inconsistent — it describes an all-adult patient sample and then reports an adolescent subgroup. The digest faithfully reproduces both, but a pitch built on "the age twist" needs the actual age range and subgroup sizes, which are unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** Check the full text for the sample age range and adolescent subgroup n before pitching; if unresolved, drop the adolescent angle to a secondary mention.

### Study 12: What actually protects child protection workers from secondary trauma is not what protects them from burnout
**PMID:** 42732758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

84 studies, 72 independent samples, 592 effect sizes, search to May 2026, shared and divergent correlates all correctly assigned to burnout vs STS. Caveats correctly state that cross-sectional design means these are correlates, matching the abstract's own closing sentence. (The digest omits compassion satisfaction and emotion-focused coping from the divergent list — an omission, not an error.)

**Issue A. Turnover attributed specifically to secondary traumatic stress** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fixing caseloads addresses burnout while doing little for the distinct, trauma-driven condition that pushes people out of the profession"
- **Abstract says:** "These conditions threaten their well-being and service provision, and may lead to increased turnover rates"
- **Problem:** The abstract attributes possible turnover to both burnout and STS; the digest assigns it to STS alone, which sharpens the story beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "…does little for the distinct, trauma-driven condition sitting alongside it"

### Study 13: Teenagers who try suicide again are not flagged by the obvious warning signs
**PMID:** 42734150 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

287 adolescents aged 12–17 recruited after an emergency-department-treated attempt, one-year follow-up, SURVIVE consortium, central nodes (emotional/behavioural difficulties, childhood trauma, depressive symptoms), reattempts peripheral with no significant direct associations, emotional abuse linked to higher lifetime attempt count, Self-Awareness of Mental Health intervention linked to longer time to reattempt — all accurate. Suicide-reporting care is appropriately flagged.

**Issue A. Scene-setting asserts that risk tools don't work** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open in a hospital emergency department with a clinician filling out a risk form they know does not work."
- **Abstract says:** "Incorporating standardized self-report tools may enhance clinical assessment and prevention strategies."
- **Problem:** The authors' own conclusion is pro-instrument; the study found that reattempt nodes were peripheral in an exploratory network, which is not a test of any risk-assessment tool's performance. The suggested scene puts a claim in a clinician's head that this paper does not support — and it sits oddly next to the digest's own instruction not to suggest abandoning risk assessment.
- **Suggested fix:** "Open in a hospital emergency department with a clinician completing a post-attempt risk assessment — then ask what the network analysis says about which of those items connect to what happens next."

### Study 14: Mothers who were maltreated as children feel like objects while breastfeeding — and it shows in the room
**PMID:** 42711662 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

N = 50 (35 CM, 15 non-CM), up to six months postpartum, video-coded Emotional Availability by certified raters, self-objectification associated with lower observed EA and more negative breastfeeding feelings, indirect path to negative feelings supported, indirect path to observed EA differing across inferential methods, omnibus p = .244 with Cramér's V = .29, detachment 31.4% vs 6.7% — all accurately reported, including the instability. Good work on the body text.

**Issue A. Headline claims the group difference the study failed to find** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Mothers who were maltreated as children feel like objects while breastfeeding — and it shows in the room"
- **Abstract says:** "evidence for the corresponding indirect association with observed maternal emotional availability differed across inferential methods… The omnibus distribution did not reach statistical significance (p = .244, Cramer's V = .29)"
- **Problem:** "And it shows in the room" asserts an observable maltreatment-linked difference in emotional availability — exactly the result that was non-significant and method-dependent. The self-objectification–EA association held; the maltreatment pathway to observed EA did not. The digest's own body and Caveats say so, so the headline contradicts them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Feeling like an object while breastfeeding: a pilot study links self-objectification to how mothers relate to their babies"

### Study 15: Self-harm in psychosis has different roots depending on whether the patient is in a forensic unit
**PMID:** 42711065 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

EU-VIORMED, five European countries, N = 392 aged 18–65 with SSD, LASSO logistic regression, lifetime DSH 45.9% forensic vs 39.5% non-forensic, forensic correlates (personality disorder β = 0.78, functional impairment), non-forensic correlates (interpersonal victimisation β = 0.85, personality disorder 0.71, male gender 0.69, substance use 0.37, PANSS items) — all accurate. Caveats correctly note that LASSO coefficients aren't effect sizes.

**Issue A. "Predicted" applied to a lifetime, cross-sectional outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "in the non-forensic group, having been victimized by others predicted self-harm more strongly than any symptom of psychosis did"
- **Abstract says:** "Lifetime DSH was measured dichotomously… In non-forensic patients, interpersonal victimisation (β=0.85) was the strongest correlate"
- **Problem:** The comparison itself is right, but "predicted" implies temporal ordering that a lifetime dichotomous measure cannot supply — victimization and self-harm could have occurred in either order. The Caveats say correlates aren't causes; the pitch line reverts to predictive language.
- **Suggested fix:** "in the non-forensic group, a history of being victimized by others was more strongly associated with self-harm than any symptom of psychosis"

*Also note:* "Giovanni de Girolamo… who leads EU-VIORMED" is unverifiable from the abstract (he is senior author on this paper); confirm before pitching.

### Study 16: Racism and sexism push men and women toward addiction after trauma by opposite routes
**PMID:** 42708575 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 23 people of colour with co-occurring PTSD/SUD, qualitative interviews, the gendered accounts for women (sexism, sexual violence exposure, fear of being blamed or dismissed, substances to cope) and men (racism and exposure to gang/gun/interpersonal violence, "tough it out," criminalisation blocking help-seeking) all match. Caveats correctly limit the claim to hypothesis-generating testimony.

**Issue A. "Push" and "opposite routes" overstate a 23-person qualitative study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Racism and sexism push men and women toward addiction after trauma by opposite routes"
- **Abstract says:** "racism and sexism influence the onset of men and women of color's PTSD/SUD in gendered ways… yet in distinctly gendered ways"
- **Problem:** "Push" reads as an established causal effect derived from participants' retrospective accounts, and "opposite routes" is stronger than "distinctly gendered" — the two pathways are different, not inverse. The authors' own language is already fairly strong, but the headline goes further on both counts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Racism and sexism shape the road into PTSD and addiction differently for men and women, survivors say"

**Cross-cutting note for the desk:** the dominant failure mode in this digest is the headline, not the body text. In studies 4, 6, 8, 9 and 14 the "The study" paragraph and Caveats are accurate and appropriately hedged, while the headline states a stronger result — efficacy, causation, or a finding that was explicitly non-significant. A single pass rewriting headlines to match the body text would clear most of the Moderate issues.
