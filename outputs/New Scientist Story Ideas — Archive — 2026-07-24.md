**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**The study:** Italian researchers ran four preregistered experiments with a combined N=984 participants, exposing them to female OnlyFans creators, non-OnlyFans creators, and umbrella girls. What moved the needle was whether people believed the woman had chosen the platform for intrinsic rather than financial reasons. As debates over sex-worker agency intensify politically and legally, this research complicates the idea that objectification and autonomy are simple opposites. **The study:** Chinese researchers used multimodal neuroimaging across two independent cohorts to test whether the cholinergic basal forebrain (ChBF) — a brain region central to attention and memory — regulates the glymphatic system (the brain's waste-clearance network) and whether this pathway contributes to depression. In a separate non-stroke cohort (n=38) receiving transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), stimulation of a site connected to ChBF improved glymphatic function, and this glymphatic enhancement partially mediated TMS's antidepressant effect.

**Why it matters:** Early evidence that antidepressant treatments may work partly by restoring the brain's overnight waste-clearance system opens a new theoretical framework for understanding and potentially treating depression — and connects the sleep-depression link to a concrete biological mechanism.

The finding proposes a new circuit: nerve signals → glymphatic flow → mood, potentially explaining why sleep disruption and depression are so tightly entwined at a biological level.
