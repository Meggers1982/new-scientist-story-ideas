### Study 1: Autistic adults in the UK are twice as likely to be on antidepressants — and stay on them longer
**PMID:** 42658004 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core numbers all check out: 172,242 autistic adults (47,011 with ID, 125,231 without), 34,173,295 non-autistic adults, 30% vs 14.7% prescribed in 2023, longest course durations in the ID group, lower recording of depression/anxiety as indication in the ID group.

**Issue A. "Without a recorded mental health indication at all"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "in the intellectual disability group frequently without a recorded mental health indication at all"
- **Abstract says:** "Recording of depression and anxiety as indications was lower for those with ID."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports *lower recording of two specific indications* (depression and anxiety) for SSRI initiation. It does not report the proportion with no recorded mental health indication of any kind — other indications (OCD, pain, sleep) could have been recorded. "Frequently without a recorded mental health indication at all" is a quantified claim the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "and in the intellectual disability group, depression and anxiety were less often recorded as the reason for starting the drug."

**Issue B. "Last year"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly one in three autistic adults in Britain was prescribed an antidepressant last year"
- **Abstract says:** "30% of autistic adults and 14.7% of non-autistic adults were prescribed an antidepressant in 2023."
- **Problem:** With a run date of August 2026, "last year" would be 2025. The data end in 2023.
- **Suggested fix:** "Nearly one in three autistic adults in Britain was prescribed an antidepressant in 2023, the most recent year in the data"

**Issue C. "Every year since 1997"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a rate that has pulled away from the general population every year since 1997"
- **Abstract says:** "Antidepressant prescribing has increased more for autistic adults than non-autistic adults since 1997"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a faster overall increase across the period, not a year-on-year widening in every single calendar year.
- **Suggested fix:** "a rate that has pulled away from the general population steadily since 1997"

**Issue D. Researcher first names** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Amir Sadik or Dhruv Rai at Bristol's MRC Integrative Epidemiology Unit"
- **Abstract says:** "Sadik A(1)... Rai D(1)... MRC Integrative Epidemiology Unit... University of Bristol"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — initials only are given. Worth checking "Dhruv Rai" against the author list before pitching; the affiliation is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** Verify given names from the published paper before use.

### Study 2: The eye may flag depression years early — but the layer is thicker, not thinner
**PMID:** 42637150 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 23,225 (retinal), 1,475 (eye-brain), Mendelian randomization with named GWAS datasets, positive GCIPL-depression association, mediation via primary and secondary visual cortex volumes — all accurate.

**Issue A. Prior literature characterized as a consensus for thinning** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research… the direction of effect contradicts the prevailing 'retinal thinning' story" / "Much previous work assumed neurodegeneration-style retinal thinning in depression" / "the researchers found the opposite of what the field expected"
- **Abstract says:** "previous research has yielded conflicting alterations in retinal microstructure in depression"
- **Problem:** The authors describe the prior literature as *conflicting*, not as a settled thinning consensus. The pitch hook — a reversal of what the field expected — rests on a characterization the abstract explicitly contradicts. The novelty label "Overturns prior research" inherits the same problem, and it is the main justification for the 7/10 score.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Resolves conflicting findings… Prior studies have reported contradictory retinal changes in depression; this large cohort plus Mendelian randomization points consistently toward thickening." Score revised to 6/10.

**Issue B. "Years early"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "may contain a signal about your mood years before you feel low"
- **Abstract says:** Cox regression assessing "the association between retinal microstructure and depression risk" — no follow-up duration stated.
- **Problem:** The abstract does not give a follow-up period, so "years before" is unverifiable from abstract only (plausible for UK Biobank, but needs the full text).
- **Suggested fix:** Confirm median follow-up from the paper before using "years."

### Study 3: Support groups in the metaverse underperform sitting in a room together
**PMID:** 42661554 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 156, three arms, four assessment points, in-person superiority for perceived social support and anxiety, avatar VR effects smaller and non-significant, attenuation at follow-up — all correctly reported.

**Issue A. Headline and pitch assert inferiority; the result is non-significance** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** Headline: "Support groups in the metaverse **underperform** sitting in a room together"; pitch: "Randomized against a room with chairs in it, the avatars lost."
- **Abstract says:** "the intervention did not show consistent superiority or equivalence to in-person groups"; "Most between-group comparisons favoured the interventions but many lacked statistical significance, probably owing to attrition and clustering."
- **Problem:** No significant VR-versus-in-person difference is reported. The trial failed to demonstrate equivalence, which is not the same as demonstrating that VR is worse. The digest's own caveat says exactly this ("findings are largely null rather than a demonstration of inferiority"), so the headline and pitch contradict the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "Metaverse support groups fail to match a room with chairs in it." Pitch: "Randomized against conventional in-person groups, the avatars couldn't demonstrate they were as good."

**Issue B. NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10"
- **Problem:** The story value rests entirely on a null that the authors themselves attribute to attrition and clustering rather than to a true absence of effect. A 7 overstates the evidential weight of an underpowered non-significant comparison.
- **Suggested fix:** Score 6/10, with the rationale rewritten around "first randomized head-to-head, but underpowered."

### Study 4: Depression compresses the mental map of emotions
**PMID:** 42658635 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Study 1 N = 1,311 aged 8–25, Study 2 N = 88 aged 18–23, 14-day experience sampling, compressed low-dimensional space, denser networks with shorter path lengths, increased transition probabilities, depression–rigid/congruent vs anxiety–unstable/incongruent, mediation abolished after controlling baseline BDI — all accurate, including the plasticity hook.

**Issue A. "Both samples Chinese students"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "both samples Chinese students, so cross-cultural generalization untested"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 (N = 1,311, aged 8-25)"; affiliations are Southwest University and Naval Medical University.
- **Problem:** Study 1 spans ages 8–25, so it cannot be described as a sample of students; the nationality of participants is inferred from author affiliation and is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Study 1 spans ages 8–25 and Study 2 is a small young-adult sample; both were recruited by Chinese institutions, so cross-cultural generalization is untested."

### Study 5: Shame — not guilt — explains why teenage girls get more depressed than boys
**PMID:** 42645734 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 2,915, mean age 16.99, 40.07% female, three waves over 12 months, females higher on all four measures, shame mediates and guilt does not, serial pathway sex → shame → maladaptive CER → depressive symptoms — all accurate.

**Issue A. Causal language for an observational mediation model** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** Headline: "Shame — not guilt — **explains why** teenage girls get more depressed"; pitch: "only the second one appears to be **making** teenage girls depressed"; Why it matters: "only one appears to **drive** the best-documented demographic gap"
- **Abstract says:** "shame mediated the sex disparity by increasing depressive risk, whereas guilt was not a significant mediator" (three-wave observational survey design)
- **Problem:** Statistical mediation in a three-wave observational study cannot establish that shame *makes* anyone depressed; it shows an association consistent with an assumed causal ordering. The digest's caveat says precisely this, so the body text contradicts the caveat. (Note the authors' own abstract uses "driven by," but a New Scientist rewrite should not amplify that.)
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "Shame — not guilt — tracks the gender gap in teenage depression." Pitch: "and only the second one is statistically linked to the higher depression rates in teenage girls."

### Study 6: Prolonged childhood loneliness roughly triples the odds of a first psychotic episode
**PMID:** 42636467 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 2,565 (1,084 FEP, 1,481 controls), EU-GEI, LNL12 OR 2.90, independence from premorbid social isolation, LNL12-16 and affective psychosis more clearly in females, additive interaction with SCZ-PGS, MDD-PGS interaction failing the European-ancestry sensitivity analysis, and the industry disclosures for Arango, Bobes and Díaz-Caneja — all correctly captured.

**Issue A. Novelty label "New mechanism"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** New mechanism"
- **Abstract says:** An epidemiological analysis of retrospectively reported loneliness as a risk factor, with additive gene–environment interaction testing.
- **Problem:** No mechanism is identified or tested; the study reports a risk association and a statistical interaction. "New mechanism" oversells the design.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** New risk-factor evidence (gene–environment interaction)"

### Study 7: Thirty-seven questions predict who will still be depressed or anxious two years later
**PMID:** 42640866 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

863 items reduced to 37 via a five-step selection strategy, five algorithms, training n = 1,472 across 13 US colleges, external validation n = 823 across 13 different colleges, random forest best on discrimination/calibration/clinical utility — all accurate.

**Issue A. "One of the strongest signals" and direction ambiguity** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "can flag who will still be unwell two years later — and one of the strongest signals is simply whether the student thinks they need help"
- **Abstract says:** "Predictors of no disorders at two years included… higher feelings of calm/peacefulness, perceived need for treatment, and intention to seek help." No ranking of predictor strength is given.
- **Problem:** Two problems. First, the abstract lists predictors without ordering them, so "one of the strongest signals" is unsupported. Second, the direction is reversed in the reader's likely reading: *higher* perceived need for treatment predicted being *disorder-free*, but the sentence pairs it with "who will still be unwell."
- **Suggested fix:** "can flag who is likely to be free of all three disorders two years later — and counterintuitively, students who thought they needed treatment and intended to seek help were more likely to end up well."

**Issue B. Headline omits the eating-disorder component of the outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Thirty-seven questions predict who will still be depressed or anxious two years later"
- **Abstract says:** "two-year prevention and remission of **all** anxiety, depression, and eating disorders"
- **Problem:** The outcome is a composite: free of all three conditions. Dropping eating disorders changes what the model actually predicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Thirty-seven questions predict which students will still have an anxiety, depression or eating disorder two years on"

**Issue C. "Prediction of response"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Resist the 'AI predicts depression' framing; this is prediction of response, not diagnosis."
- **Abstract says:** The model discriminates "who did versus did not achieve two-year prevention and remission" among students receiving D-CBTgsh.
- **Problem:** With no untreated comparator in the modelling, this is prediction of *outcome* within a treated cohort, not prediction of treatment *response* in the differential sense.
- **Suggested fix:** "this predicts two-year outcome among students already receiving the digital programme, not diagnosis and not differential treatment response."

### Study 8: Gut bacteria in depression shift from fermenting sugars to fermenting protein
**PMID:** 42636957 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

102 MDD patients, 38 matched controls, depletion of acetate/propionate/butyrate, elevated branched-chain SCFAs, combined model of BSCFAs + acetate + API + EGF with AUC 0.871, 5-HT1A agent association — all accurate, and the caveats appropriately handle the cross-sectional design and control-group size.

**Issue A. "Run out of fiber"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The bacteria in the gut of someone with depression appear to have run out of fiber and started breaking down protein instead"
- **Abstract says:** "a pathological shift from saccharolytic to proteolytic fermentation" — no dietary data are reported.
- **Problem:** Diet and fibre intake were not measured. Attributing the fermentation shift to fibre deficiency imports a causal dietary explanation that the study cannot distinguish from illness effects, medication, transit time or other confounders — and it sits awkwardly beside the same section's instruction not to imply anyone should change their diet.
- **Suggested fix:** "The gut bacteria of people with depression appear to be fermenting more protein and less carbohydrate, generating compounds that track with inflammation and symptom severity — though the study did not measure what anyone ate."

**Issue B. "Number of past episodes"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the same markers tracked symptom severity, physiosomatic symptoms and number of past episodes"
- **Abstract says:** "higher OSOD, more severe physiosomatic symptoms, and increased ROI [recurrence of illness]"
- **Problem:** Recurrence of illness in this research group's framework is a composite index, not simply a count of prior episodes.
- **Suggested fix:** "…and a higher recurrence-of-illness index."

### Study 9: Which psychotherapy works for anorexia may depend on how depressed you are
**PMID:** 42611819 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

242 randomized across 10 German university hospitals, three arms, no BMI difference, secondary analysis of N = 160 in the two active arms, MMRM with BMI at T5 (mean 5.96 years), moderation direction (low depression/high mental QoL → CBT-E; high depression/low mental QoL → FPT), consistency across T1–T4, wide middle range where treatments perform alike — all accurate and well caveated.

**Issue A. "Mattered a great deal"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Six years on, the data say it mattered a great deal — if you were also depressed."
- **Abstract says:** "Graphical visualisation confirms that there is a wide range where both treatments perform similarly, while CBT or FPT perform differently in the extreme ranges of these two variables."
- **Problem:** No effect sizes for the moderation are given, and the abstract stresses that the differentiation appears only at the extremes. "A great deal" implies a magnitude the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Six years on, the data say it may have mattered — for patients at the depressed end of the range."

### Study 10: Stress and low mood predict everyday aggression; political ideology doesn't
**PMID:** 42661529 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 1,215, predictor set, unique associations for stress/anxiety/depressive symptoms, distress block explaining the largest share of variance, null for mentalizing and RWA facets, small inverse SDO association interpreted cautiously, robust and quantile regression support — all accurate.

**Issue A. Content of the aggression index is invented** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "what actually predicted having hit, shoved or shouted at someone recently"
- **Abstract says:** "recent aggressive behavior using a brief aggression index" — items are not described.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the specific acts are the writer's illustration, not the measure's content.
- **Suggested fix:** "what actually predicted recent aggressive acts" — or confirm the item content from the full text before specifying behaviours.

**Issue B. Sample nationality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "French community sample only"
- **Abstract says:** "In a community sample of adults (N = 1,215)" — no country stated; the sole author is affiliated with French institutions.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. Reasonable inference, but it should be confirmed rather than asserted in a caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Single community sample, country of recruitment to be confirmed from the full text."

### Study 12: Rumination has no shared brain signature — everyone ruminates differently
**PMID:** 42648371 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 24 depressed participants, six eight-minute inductions across three sessions, 64-channel EEG, participant-specific linear SVMs on alpha/beta/theta plus inter-channel connectivity, small-but-significant group-average accuracy, 10 of 24 above chance, heterogeneous feature weights — all accurate.

**Issue A. Ground truth mislabelled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "self-reported rumination levels as the ground truth"
- **Abstract says:** "We used validated tasks to induce rumination or distraction… we trained participant-specific linear support vector machines to differentiate state rumination from distraction… and measured self-reported levels of rumination."
- **Problem:** The classification labels were the induced conditions (rumination vs distraction task); self-report served as a manipulation check ("Participants reported strongly increased rumination"), not as the classifier's ground truth.
- **Suggested fix:** "labels came from the induction condition rather than from spontaneous rumination, with self-report used only as a manipulation check."

**Issue B. Headline absolutism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Rumination has **no** shared brain signature"
- **Abstract says:** "averaged across all participant-specific decoders we classified state rumination from EEG patterns with small albeit significant accuracy… variability in ruminative processes limits the generalization of decoding approaches across participants."
- **Problem:** There was a small but statistically significant signal on average; the finding is heterogeneity limiting generalization, not the total absence of a shared signature.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rumination has no reliable shared brain signature — everyone ruminates differently"

### Study 13: Growing up with a parent's drinking problem raises adult depression risk — but not on its own
**PMID:** 42665302 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

32 studies eligible, 29 meta-analysed, four databases, anxiety SMD 0.37 / 73% higher likelihood, depression SMD 0.29 / 65% higher likelihood, attenuation but retained significance in confounder-adjusted studies — all accurate.

**Issue A. "Much of" / "substantially shared" overstates "partly explained"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the authors conclude that much of the association may reflect co-occurring childhood adversities and broader family dysfunction" / "the mental health risk is real but substantially shared with other family adversity"
- **Abstract says:** "these associations may be **partly** explained by co-occurring childhood adversities and broader family dysfunction"
- **Problem:** "Much of" and "substantially" quantify an attribution the authors deliberately left unquantified, and the abstract notes the associations *remained statistically significant* after adjustment. The deflationary framing — which is the whole hook of the entry — is stronger than the source supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "the authors conclude the association may be partly explained by co-occurring childhood adversities and broader family dysfunction" / "the mental health risk is real, and partly shared with other family adversity."

### Study 14: Preventing perinatal depression doesn't automatically make parents warmer
**PMID:** 42593980 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 103 dyads, MB versus community home-visiting care as usual, 42 months postpartum, virtual observational coding with the Brief Parent-Child Early Relational Assessment, no significant differences in amount or quality of verbal and physical warmth, and the authors' own suggestion that parenting may need direct targeting — all match. The caveats correctly note that this is a substudy of a longitudinal observational study rather than the original randomized comparison, and flag possible underpowering.

### Study 15: In fibromyalgia, depression and body-wide symptom burden multiply each other
**PMID:** 42660390 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

820 adults, 2016 ACR criteria, CSI/BDI-II/SF-36/FIQR, significant CSI × BDI interactions in five domains (Role Physical, Role Emotional, Energy/Fatigue, Emotional Well-Being, Social Functioning), greatest impairment in those elevated on both — all accurate.

**Issue A. "Different domains" understates the overlap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Both predictors independently tracked impairment in different domains — central sensitization with physical function, fatigue and pain; depression with emotional wellbeing and social function."
- **Abstract says:** CSI with Physical Functioning, Energy/Fatigue, Pain, General Health; depressive symptoms with Emotional Well-Being, Social Functioning, **Physical Functioning**, and Health Change.
- **Problem:** Depression was also independently associated with Physical Functioning, so the clean "sensory vs emotional" split implied by "different domains" is not what the model showed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Both predictors independently tracked impairment, with partly distinct profiles — central sensitization strongest for pain, fatigue and general health; depression strongest for emotional wellbeing and social function, though it also predicted physical functioning."

### Study 16: Resilience and social support don't independently protect refugees from PTSD
**PMID:** 42642861 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 461 Syrian refugees in Türkiye, mean age 41.54, 73.5% women, quantile regression across the symptom distribution, depression and anxiety associated at all quantiles, cognitive reappraisal more strongly inverse at high symptom levels, expressive suppression more strongly linked to severe symptoms, and resilience and perceived social support not independently associated after accounting for emotional distress and cumulative trauma. The "Why it matters" section is appropriately hedged ("only prospective data could confirm that") and the caveats cover the cross-sectional design and the possible measurement artefact.

### Study 17: Students who wrongly believe they're first-generation are the hungriest
**PMID:** 42634162 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 344 at a mid-sized public university, partial η² 0.064–0.096, neither objective nor subjective first-generation status predicting symptoms after accounting for food insecurity, subjective identification predicting food insecurity risk (aOR 1.81) where objective classification did not, over-identifiers at 61.1% — all accurate.

**Issue A. "Wrongly believe"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** Headline: "Students who **wrongly** believe they're first-generation are the hungriest"
- **Abstract says:** "'Over-identifiers'—students who self-identified as first-generation despite having a college-educated parent"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a mismatch with the parental-education definition, not an error of fact by the student; definitions of "first-generation" vary (foreign degrees, non-completing parents, two-year vs four-year institutions). "Wrongly" editorializes, and it sits oddly against the entry's own argument that the subjective measure identifies need better than the administrative category.
- **Suggested fix:** "Students who call themselves first-generation — even when a parent has a degree — are the hungriest"

### Study 18: Two rich countries, two very different routes to brain stimulation for depression
**PMID:** 42658152 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

29 Swedish centres, near-complete registry participation integrated with EHRs, iTBS predominance, 112 Japanese facilities, 62 in the registry, public insurance covering only 10 Hz left DLPFC, iTBS in private clinics, greater financial barriers in Japan — all accurate, and the caveats correctly note the descriptive design and absence of patient-level or economic data.

**Issue A. Session-duration detail not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open on the difference between a 37-minute session and a 3-minute one."
- **Abstract says:** Protocols are named (10 Hz left DLPFC; iTBS) but no session durations are reported.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The figures are conventional for the two protocols but are external context, not a study finding.
- **Suggested fix:** Attribute the durations to standard protocol references rather than to this paper.

**Issue B. "Standard care… in many countries"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rTMS is now standard care for treatment-resistant depression in many countries"
- **Abstract says:** "rTMS is an evidence-based intervention for major depressive disorder (MDD) and treatment-resistant depression (TRD)."
- **Problem:** The study covers two countries; the abstract characterizes rTMS as evidence-based, not as standard care internationally.
- **Suggested fix:** "rTMS is an evidence-based treatment for treatment-resistant depression, but who gets it, which protocol, and at what cost is set by reimbursement rules rather than evidence."

**Cross-cutting note for the desk:** the recurring pattern in this digest is *hook inflation on null and mediation findings* — Studies 3, 5, 12 and 13 all state a stronger claim in the headline or pitch than the abstract supports, and in Studies 3 and 5 the digest's own caveats already say so.
