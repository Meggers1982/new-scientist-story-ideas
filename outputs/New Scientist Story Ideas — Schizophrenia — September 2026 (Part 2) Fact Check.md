### Study 1: Stopping antipsychotics does not trigger a distinct kind of rapid relapse, trial data suggest
**PMID:** 42636852 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Age range applied to the whole pooled sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "271 people on long-acting injections and 146 on tablets, aged 18 to 65"
- **Abstract says:** LAI participants "mean age 38·4 years [SD 11·2, range 18-65]"; oral participants "mean age 34·8 years [SD 11·5, range 18-61]"
- **Problem:** The 18–65 range belongs to the LAI trials only; the oral trials topped out at 61. A single range attributed to both cohorts is slightly wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** "271 people on long-acting injections (aged 18 to 65) and 146 on tablets (aged 18 to 61)"

**Issue B. "No more common" understates a non-significant numerical gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rapid relapse was no more common in people switched to placebo than in those who stayed on the drug"
- **Abstract says:** "The proportion with rapid relapse did not differ between continuation and discontinuation groups in either LAI (discontinuation 39 [20%] of 197, continuation eight [11%] of 74; p=0·12) or oral trials (discontinuation 29 [27%] of 108, continuation ten [26%] of 38, p=0·95)"
- **Problem:** In the LAI trials the rate was nearly double in the discontinuation arm; the comparison was simply not statistically significant in a modest sample. "No more common" reads as a demonstrated equivalence rather than an absence of a detectable difference — a distinction that matters given the digest's "Overturns prior research" novelty tag.
- **Suggested fix:** "rapid relapse was not significantly more common in people switched to placebo than in those who stayed on the drug (20% vs 11% in the injection trials, p=0·12)"

Funding (NIHR Oxford BRC, Wellcome Trust) and the extensive author conflicts are both correctly carried into Caveats. NS fit 8/10 is defensible for pooled IPD from five double-blind RCTs.

### Study 2: The biggest study yet of a genetic syndrome linked to psychosis finds an uneven profile of thinking skills
**PMID:** 42765380 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Drift downward" language in "Why it matters" sits uneasily with the cross-sectional caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cognitive scores drift downward relative to peers across development regardless of whether psychosis appears"
- **Abstract says:** "This profile was similar across age groups, with scores highest in children, lower in adolescents, and lowest in adults."
- **Problem:** "Drift downward … across development" describes a within-person trajectory; the abstract reports between-age-group differences at initial assessment (with longitudinal data only from a subset). The Caveats and the pitch angle both warn against exactly this reading, so the body text pulls against its own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "cognitive scores are progressively lower in older age groups relative to typical peers, whether or not psychosis appears — though this is largely a comparison across age groups rather than a tracked decline"

**Issue B. The 37% figure is attributed specifically to longitudinal data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "age comparisons are mostly cross-sectional (only 37% contributed longitudinal data)"
- **Abstract says:** "Using both cross-sectional and longitudinal IQ data from a subset (n = 496; 37.35%) of individuals, we examined IQ profiles across three age groups"
- **Problem:** The abstract says 37.35% formed the subset used for the age-group and SSD analyses, drawing on both cross-sectional and longitudinal data; it does not state that 37% contributed longitudinal data specifically. The proportion with repeat assessments is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "age comparisons rest on a subset of 496 participants (37%) with cross-sectional and longitudinal data; the proportion with repeat testing is not specified in the abstract"

Sample size (1,328), the domain ranking (PS > WM > VIQ > PIQ), the SSD finding with no domain-specific effect, and the named contacts (Fiksinski, UMC Utrecht; Vorstman, SickKids) all check out.

### Study 3: Screening service members as they leave the military flags undetected early psychosis
**PMID:** 42723010 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. NS fit score of 7 on a cross-sectional, self-reported single-item screen** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — a single added question to more than 5000 people uncovering hidden risk is a clean, concrete story; limited by cross-sectional design and low follow-up uptake."
- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional study added an item querying for perceptual disturbances…"; only 67 of 120 contacted (55.8%) initiated the interview; conclusion is that screening "was feasible and acceptable" and "has potential to identify risk and improve outcomes."
- **Problem:** The headline number (5,019 screened) is large, but every substantive claim beyond the 10% positive rate rests on self-report and on 66 completed interviews drawn from a non-random-responding subset. The risk-ratio comparisons are all self-reported symptom endorsements. A 7 overstates the evidence weight.
- **Suggested fix:** Score 6/10, with the rationale noting that the diagnostic yield comes from 66 interviews and that no outcome data exist.

Otherwise accurate: 5,019 screened, 504 (10.0%) positive, 12 APS and five threshold psychosis among 66 completers, and the explicit instruction not to imply screening improves outcomes correctly mirrors the abstract's hedged "has potential to."

### Study 4: How childhood abuse and neglect may translate into hearing voices later on
**PMID:** 42740395 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline names the wrong outcome and implies temporal sequence** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "How childhood abuse and neglect may translate into hearing voices later on"
- **Abstract says:** Outcome was "psychotic experiences" measured with "the Community Assessment of Psychic Experiences (CAPE)"; design was cross-sectional parallel mediation within the GROUP subsample.
- **Problem:** Two problems in one line. The outcome is a broad self-report measure of psychotic experiences, not auditory hallucinations specifically — voice hearing is never separately analyzed in the abstract. And "later on" implies a temporal ordering that a cross-sectional mediation model cannot establish; the Caveats concede this, but the headline asserts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Childhood abuse and neglect may reach psychotic experiences by two different psychological routes"

**Issue B. Claim about therapeutic targetability is not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "negative self-beliefs are directly targetable in therapy while self-disturbance largely is not yet"
- **Abstract says:** "Further research is needed to explore the causal pathways between trauma, negative self-schemas, self-disturbance phenomena, and psychotic experiences."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The abstract makes no treatment claim of any kind, and this line is doing the load-bearing work in "Why it matters."
- **Suggested fix:** Attribute it to an outside expert in the pitch rather than presenting it as a study implication, or drop it.

Sample composition (110 patients, 120 siblings, 48 controls), the pattern of full versus partial mediation in each group, and the Amsterdam UMC contacts are all accurate.

### Study 5: Metformin, but not the standard add-on drug, curbs weight gain in people taking clozapine
**PMID:** 42742489 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in the headline for a retrospective observational cohort** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Metformin, but not the standard add-on drug, curbs weight gain in people taking clozapine"
- **Abstract says:** "Metformin, but Not Aripiprazole, Was Associated With Attenuation of Weight Gain in Clozapine-Treated Inpatients… This retrospective cohort study…"
- **Problem:** "Curbs" asserts a causal effect. The authors were careful to say "was associated with," twice, including in their own title. Prescribing was not randomized, so who got metformin almost certainly differed systematically — the digest's own Caveats say so, which the headline then contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Metformin, but not the standard add-on drug, is linked to slower weight gain in people taking clozapine"

**Issue B. Overstated claim about clozapine's efficacy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clozapine is the only drug that reliably works for treatment-resistant schizophrenia"
- **Abstract says:** "Clozapine, commonly indicated for treatment-resistant psychosis" (and in the companion study, "Clozapine remains the most effective treatment for TRS")
- **Problem:** "The only drug that reliably works" is stronger than "most effective." Minor, but it is stated as established fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Clozapine is the most effective drug available for treatment-resistant schizophrenia"

**Issue C. Illustrative weight figure has no source in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the medication that quiets the voices and adds 15 kilograms in a year"
- **Abstract says:** Reports weight trajectories and a time × metformin coefficient (B = −2.63, 95% CI −4.49 to −0.76); no absolute annual weight-gain figure.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. If the pitch keeps the number it needs an independent citation, or a reader will assume it came from this cohort.
- **Suggested fix:** "the medication that quiets the voices and puts on substantial weight in the first months of treatment"

N=80, 306 matched observations, the 105-bed BC facility, the norclozapine and opioid agonist therapy associations, and the Sequence2Script conflict disclosure are all correctly reported.

### Study 6: People with schizophrenia look away exactly when their conversation partner smiles
**PMID:** 42702091 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. "Accounted for much of" overstates an association** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "these averted moments accounted for much of their previously documented failure to smile back"
- **Abstract says:** "This pattern was associated with a higher proportion of failed mimicry accompanied by gaze aversion in the schizophrenia group compared to controls… These findings suggest that reduced visual attention may represent one mechanism contributing to emotional mimicry deficits"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a higher proportion of failed-mimicry episodes that were accompanied by gaze aversion — a co-occurrence. It does not quantify how much of the mimicry deficit gaze aversion explains, and offers it only as "one mechanism." "Accounted for much of" implies a variance-partitioning or mediation result that isn't there.
- **Suggested fix:** "and a larger share of their failures to smile back occurred while they were looking away — which the authors suggest may be one mechanism behind the mimicry deficit"

The within-partner design (20 partners, 20 patients, 20 matched controls), the null result for overall gaze aversion, the significant result during partner smiling, the OpenFace/FaceReader reanalysis note and the stigma thread are all faithful to the abstract.

### Study 7: Gut bacteria look different in people with treatment-resistant schizophrenia on clozapine
**PMID:** 42758012 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mischaracterizes why clozapine use is restricted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clozapine's twin burdens — severe constipation and rapid metabolic deterioration — are the main reasons its use is restricted"
- **Abstract says:** "its use is limited by serious metabolic and immune-related side effects"
- **Problem:** The abstract pairs metabolic with *immune-related* side effects — i.e. agranulocytosis, which is the reason for mandatory haematological monitoring and registry restriction. Substituting constipation for the immune risk misstates the regulatory picture, even though constipation is a real and sometimes fatal complication.
- **Suggested fix:** "Clozapine's serious metabolic and immune-related side effects — including the blood-count risk that triggers mandatory monitoring, plus severe constipation — are why its use is tightly restricted"

Everything else checks out: 25 vs 25, matching on age/sex/BMI/smoking, reduced diversity, reduced Firmicutes, elevated *Eggerthella*, *Mucispirillum* and unclassified Clostridia (order SHA-98), predicted functional pathways, and nominal exploratory symptom associations. The abstract declares no conflicts of interest, consistent with the digest. NS fit 5/10 is appropriately modest.

### Study 8: Brain "background noise" is altered in schizophrenia and tracks memory for one's own life
**PMID:** 42744260 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline attributes the memory finding to the aperiodic signal** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Brain 'background noise' is altered in schizophrenia and tracks memory for one's own life"
- **Abstract says:** "Bilateral pericalcarine exponent correlated positively with PANSS positive scores… while reduced alpha power in the bilateral posterior cingulate cortex and the left supramarginal gyrus was associated with impairments in verbal learning, visual learning, and autobiographical memory."
- **Problem:** The two findings belong to different components. The aperiodic exponent ("background noise") tracked *positive symptoms*; it was *alpha oscillation* power — the rhythmic, periodic component — that tracked autobiographical memory. The headline welds the more exciting memory result onto the aperiodic hook. The body text gets this right, which makes the headline an avoidable trap for an editor skimming the digest.
- **Suggested fix:** "Brain 'background noise' is altered in schizophrenia and tracks positive symptoms"

**Issue B. Medication status is asserted in Caveats but absent from the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "patients were medicated, and antipsychotics affect spectral measures"
- **Abstract says:** Nothing about medication status; reports only that 40 patients and 27 controls were enrolled and assessed with PANSS, MCCB, AMT and resting-state MEG.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. It is a reasonable assumption for a hospital sample but should be marked as unconfirmed rather than stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "medication status is not reported in the abstract; if patients were medicated, antipsychotics affect spectral measures"

The group sizes, the direction of the aperiodic exponent change (increased/steeper), the pericalcarine and supramarginal regions, the reduced alpha peak power and the r ≈ 0.33–0.35 correlations are all correct.

### Study 9: Voice hearers describe a constant negotiation over who is in charge
**PMID:** 41354941 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Superlative not supported by the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the capacity to build a personally meaningful narrative was what most restored a feeling of self-determination"
- **Abstract says:** "developing a personally meaningful narrative was crucial for re-establishing autonomy"; separately, "Participants' capacity for integration, and perceptions of their ability to influence and cause change, affected the extent to which they felt self-determined."
- **Problem:** "What most restored" ranks narrative-building above other factors. Interpretative phenomenological analysis of six interviews does not produce that kind of ordering, and the abstract describes it as "crucial," not as the single largest contributor.
- **Suggested fix:** "building a personally meaningful narrative emerged as crucial to re-establishing a sense of autonomy"

**Issue B. Study setting not given** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eleanor Longden, at the Psychosis Research Unit in Manchester and herself a voice hearer, is the obvious call"
- **Abstract says:** First, second and senior authors are at Swinburne University of Technology and The Alfred Hospital, Melbourne, Australia; Longden is a UK-based co-author.
- **Problem:** The digest gives no country or institution for the study anywhere, and the only named contact is the one co-author who was not based where the research was conducted. A reader could easily file this as a UK study.
- **Suggested fix:** Add "conducted at Swinburne University of Technology in Melbourne" to The study paragraph, and note Longden is a UK-based co-author.

The six participants, the IPA method, and the three-theme content (self and self-authorship challenged; power shared and negotiated; narrative as route back to autonomy) are all accurately rendered, and the Caveats correctly cap the generalizability. NS fit 5/10 is right for n=6 qualitative work.

### Study 10: Computational models of memory turn out to be as reliable as the simple scores they replace
**PMID:** 42765980 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Omits the case where the computational parameter was substantially *less* reliable** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The computational parameters generally showed good-to-excellent split-half and test-retest reliability above 0.80 — comparable to, and in one case better than, the simple metric."
- **Abstract says:** "In one case, reliability was substantially better for the memory precision parameter than for the noncomputational metric. In another case, reliability for the memory precision parameter was substantially lower but was improved by the use of hierarchical Bayesian estimation."
- **Problem:** The digest reports the favourable exception and drops the unfavourable one. The authors' own conclusion is conditional — "when applied thoughtfully" — and that conditionality comes directly from the case the digest omits. The headline ("turn out to be as reliable as") and the pitch line ("adding a layer of modeling made the measurements more useful, not less") both inherit the one-sided framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "The computational parameters generally showed good-to-excellent split-half and test-retest reliability above 0.80 — comparable to the simple metric, better in one case, and substantially worse in another, where reliability recovered only with hierarchical Bayesian estimation."

Sample sizes (105 controls; 72 schizophrenia spectrum, 68 bipolar, 76 major depression, totalling 216), the ≥28-day retest interval, the three model parameters, the mean-error comparator and the named contacts (Luck, UC Davis; Barch, WashU) are all correct. NS fit 4/10 is appropriately low.

### Study 11: An EEG marker touted to predict who responds to clozapine does not deliver
**PMID:** 41124326 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Failed replication" is the wrong novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Failed replication"
- **Abstract says:** "This study explores Quantitative electroencephalogram (QEEG) as a tool to predict clozapine response in Indian TRS patients… This study aims to predict treatment response to clozapine in TRS patients using quantitative electroencephalogram (QEEG)"
- **Problem:** Nothing in the abstract frames this as an attempt to replicate a specific prior result. It is an original prospective study that reached a negative conclusion. Labelling it a failed replication implies a named earlier finding was tested and overturned, which an editor may then ask the writer to identify.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Negative result"

**Issue B. AUC 0.757 characterized as "weak"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the discriminative performance was weak (area under the curve 0.757 at six weeks)"
- **Abstract says:** "ROC analysis at 6 weeks yielding an Area under curve of 0.757 (P = .014)… Increased right temporal delta power was seen in responders, but changes were insufficient to reliably predict outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a statistically significant AUC and calls the changes "insufficient to reliably predict" — it does not characterize the discrimination as weak. An AUC of 0.757 is conventionally moderate. The digest's conclusion is right; the adjective overshoots.
- **Suggested fix:** "discrimination was only moderate (area under the curve 0.757 at six weeks, p=0.014), and the authors concluded the changes were insufficient to reliably predict who would respond"

N=39 recruited/36 completers, the North India tertiary centre, the baseline/3-week/6-week assessments, the 67% responder rate, the ≥20% BPRS threshold and the right temporal delta finding are all accurate, and the pitch correctly avoids any prescribing recommendation. NS fit 4/10 is defensible.
