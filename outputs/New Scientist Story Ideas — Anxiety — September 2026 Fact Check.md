### Study 1: Cannabis anxiety does not rise with the dose — and eaten THC barely registers at all
**PMID:** 42242480 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline misstates the oral finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and eaten THC barely registers at all"
- **Abstract says:** "Oral dose was not associated with any subjective effects (p > 0.05)" — while the pairwise meta-analyses (pooling routes) found THC vs placebo produced "greater feelings of high, anxiety, and tiredness (all p < 0.001), lower alertness and contentedness (both p < 0.01)"
- **Problem:** A null *dose–response* relationship for oral THC is not the same as oral THC having little effect. The abstract reports no dose gradient for oral administration, not an absence of subjective effects. The body text gets this right ("oral dose was not associated with any subjective effect measured"), but the headline converts it into a claim about the drug's potency.
- **Suggested fix:** "Cannabis anxiety does not rise with the dose — and for eaten THC, dose predicts nothing at all"

**Issue B. Selective reporting of the inhaled dose–response results** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "higher inhaled doses increased feeling high and *decreased* calmness without increasing anxiety at all (b = −0.03, p = 0.845)"
- **Abstract says:** "Greater inhaled THC dose increased feeling high (b=1.06, p = 0.0024) and contentedness (b=1.06, p = 0.025), and decreased calmness (b=-0.96, p = 0.048)"
- **Problem:** The digest omits that higher inhaled dose was associated with *increased* contentedness, which makes the dose–response picture look more uniformly aversive than the abstract reports — and sits oddly beside the pitch angle's "cut contentedness by 71" (a placebo-contrast estimate at 5 mg, not a dose slope).
- **Suggested fix:** "higher inhaled doses increased feeling high and contentedness, decreased calmness, and did not increase anxiety at all (b = −0.03, p = 0.845)"

**Issue C. Competing-interest statement incompletely reported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "several authors declare pharmaceutical speaker fees (Jazz/GW, Otsuka, Lundbeck) unrelated to the work"
- **Abstract says:** "Amir Englund has received speaker's fees from Jazz/GW, Otsuka, and Lundbeck Pharmaceuticals ... Ryan Vandrey has received compensation as a consultant or scientific advisor to Charlotte's Web and Syqe Medical Ltd"
- **Problem:** Only one author declared the speaker fees listed, and the digest omits the second author's paid advisory relationships with two cannabis/cannabinoid companies — directly relevant commercial ties for a cannabis dose-response paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "the corresponding author declares speaker fees (Jazz/GW, Otsuka, Lundbeck) and a co-author declares paid consultancy/advisory roles with cannabinoid companies Charlotte's Web and Syqe Medical, all unrelated to the work"

**Issue D. "All-or-nothing" is an interpretation of a null, not a finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the anxiety appears to be roughly all-or-nothing once you are intoxicated"
- **Abstract says:** "no change in anxiety (b=-0.03, p = 0.845)" across inhaled doses
- **Problem:** A non-significant dose slope in a meta-regression is consistent with a flat relationship, but also with limited dose range or between-study heterogeneity; presenting it as a positive mechanistic claim ("all-or-nothing") overreads a null.
- **Suggested fix:** "anxiety did not scale with dose in these trials — the pooled data show no dose gradient, though a flat curve is only one explanation for a null slope"

### Study 2: Anxious attachment at 56 predicts frailty and cognitive impairment at 68
**PMID:** 41885885 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Follow-up interval overstated in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tracks physical and cognitive aging two decades later"
- **Abstract says:** attachment "assessed at a mean age of 56 (starting 2003)"; outcomes "at a mean age of 68 (2016–2019)"
- **Problem:** The interval is roughly 12–13 years, not two decades — and the digest's own NS fit line says "a 12-year prospective design," so the body text contradicts itself.
- **Suggested fix:** "tracks physical and cognitive aging a dozen years later"

**Issue B. Sample race composition not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "all-male, predominantly white US veteran cohort"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were 1,608 men in the Vietnam Era Twin Study of Aging"
- **Problem:** "Predominantly white" is plausible for VETSA but is not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "all-male US veteran twin cohort (racial composition not reported in the abstract)"

*NS fit note:* 7/10 is defensible for a 1,608-person prospective cohort with a formal mediation model, though the self-reported mediators and single-sex sample put it at the top of its range.

### Study 3: Body dissatisfaction feeds social anxiety through self-disgust — but only in girls
**PMID:** 42497472 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline for a longitudinal observational study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Body dissatisfaction feeds social anxiety through self-disgust"
- **Abstract says:** "body dissatisfaction at T1 was found to indirectly predict social anxiety at T3 through self-disgust at T2"
- **Problem:** "Feeds" asserts causation; the design is a three-wave observational survey with autoregressive controls, which supports prediction, not causation. The Caveats correctly say "cannot establish causation," so the headline contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Body dissatisfaction predicts later social anxiety via self-disgust — but only in girls"

**Issue B. Pitch angle mischaracterizes the two pathways as ending in the same place** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "boys and girls arrive at the same place by opposite paths"
- **Abstract says:** "for females, only the mediating pathway with self-disgust as the mediator was significant; for males, only the pathway with social anxiety as the mediator was significant"
- **Problem:** The two pathways have different *endpoints*, not the same endpoint by different routes: girls' significant path ends at social anxiety (T3), boys' ends at self-disgust (T3). The following sentence in the pitch describes this correctly, so the framing sentence undercuts it.
- **Suggested fix:** "boys and girls travel between the same two emotions in opposite directions"

### Study 4: Trait anxiety shows up in how brain states switch, not in alpha power
**PMID:** 42379500 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Preregistered" is not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "then show what a preregistered comparison found"
- **Abstract says:** "HC3-robust regressions contrasted three prespecified microstate transition metrics with two prespecified spectral indices"
- **Problem:** "Prespecified" analyses are not necessarily preregistered; the abstract makes no preregistration claim. Saying so in a pitch invites an easy correction.
- **Suggested fix:** "then show what a comparison of prespecified metrics found"

**Issue B. Depression singled out as the confound** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "much of the spectral signal was really depression riding along"
- **Abstract says:** "After adjustment for depressive symptoms, intolerance of uncertainty, and metacognitive beliefs ... frontal alpha and midline theta did not remain significant after this psychosocial adjustment"
- **Problem:** Three covariates were entered together; the abstract does not isolate depression as the variable responsible for attenuating the spectral effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "much of the spectral signal did not survive adjustment for depression, intolerance of uncertainty and metacognitive beliefs"

**Issue C. Effect-size claim not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "effect sizes are modest even where robust"
- **Abstract says:** no effect sizes are reported.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "effect sizes not reported in the abstract"

### Study 5: How unpleasant a threat cue still feels after therapy-style extinction predicts whether fear comes back
**PMID:** 42546511 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Failed replication" novelty label misdescribes the result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Failed replication"
- **Abstract says:** "we show that the residual CS un-pleasantness at the end of extinction predicts self-reported fear after the renewal test ... This research provides further evidence that self-reported CS un-pleasantness predicts return of fear."
- **Problem:** The conceptual replication succeeded on the primary self-report channel and failed only on electrodermal responding. Tagging the whole entry "Failed replication" contradicts the digest's own Caveats ("partial replication") and could send an editor into the pitch with the wrong story.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Partial/conceptual replication"

### Study 8: Four days of intensive treatment outperforms three weeks of inpatient care for OCD
**PMID:** 42500867 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Depression and anxiety reductions attributed to one arm** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Yale-Brown Obsessive-Compulsive Scale scores were substantially lower after the four-day program ... with 91.9% response and 45.8% remission, no dropouts, and parallel reductions in depression and anxiety."
- **Abstract says:** "After treatment, obsessive-compulsive disorder symptoms as well as secondary depression and anxiety symptoms were reduced in both treatment groups."
- **Problem:** The sentence structure attaches the secondary-symptom improvements to the Bergen arm alone, when the abstract reports them in both arms — which matters because the anxiety angle is what makes this a New Scientist Mind item.
- **Suggested fix:** "...no dropouts, and reductions in depression and anxiety in both arms."

### Study 9: Nearly half of women with polycystic ovary syndrome have clinically significant anxiety
**PMID:** 42217371 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Clinically significant" is added to the prevalence estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Nearly half of women with polycystic ovary syndrome have clinically significant anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "anxiety in PCOS(48.0%, 95%C.I.=37.0-59.0)"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a pooled prevalence of "anxiety" without specifying diagnostic versus screening-threshold ascertainment, so "clinically significant" is an unsupported qualifier. The confidence interval (37–59%) is also wide enough that "nearly half" is at the point estimate rather than a stable figure.
- **Suggested fix:** "Pooled estimates put anxiety in polycystic ovary syndrome at 48% (95% CI 37–59)"

**Issue B. Denominator in the wider angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "only 15 of 119 estimates in this atlas reached strong credibility"
- **Abstract says:** "Among 64 prevalence estimates ... 10(6.4%) had strong credibility ... Five of 43(11.7%) outcomes met strong credibility criteria" out of "119 meta-analytic estimates"
- **Problem:** The 15 strong-credibility estimates come from the 107 estimates that were credibility-graded (64 prevalence + 43 outcome), not from all 119. The ratio as written is arithmetically defensible but imprecise for a meta-science angle whose whole point is the ratio.
- **Suggested fix:** "only 15 of the 107 credibility-graded estimates in this atlas reached strong credibility"

### Study 10: Learning to step back from your thoughts blunts the link between symptoms and daily impairment
**PMID:** 42660582 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal and intervention language for an observational survey study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "**Learning** to step back from your thoughts blunts the link..." and "it stops anxiety from wrecking your functioning, but does not stop wrecked functioning from flattening your mood"
- **Abstract says:** "A community sample (N = 386) completed monthly self-report surveys for 12 months ... Decentering significantly reduced the impact of one's symptoms on impairment ... decentering was supported as a protective factor"
- **Problem:** There was no intervention and no one was taught anything; decentering was measured, not trained. And "stops" asserts causation from a within-person moderation analysis of self-report data. The digest's own Caveats say "no intervention component" and "cannot establish causation," so the headline and "Why it matters" contradict them.
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "Stepping back from your thoughts tracks a weaker link between symptoms and daily impairment." Body: "it is associated with a weaker symptom-to-impairment link, but not with a weaker impairment-to-well-being link."

**Issue B. "Dismantling study" mislabels the design** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "dismantling studies like this one show it works on some outcomes and not others"
- **Abstract says:** "A community sample (N = 386) completed monthly self-report surveys for 12 months"
- **Problem:** A dismantling study is a controlled trial that removes intervention components. This is an observational longitudinal survey; calling it dismantling research misrepresents the evidence class in a pitch built on methodological rigor.
- **Suggested fix:** "observational mechanism studies like this one suggest decentering tracks some outcomes and not others"

### Study 11: Your partner's ADHD symptoms track your postpartum anxiety
**PMID:** 41982066 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Regardless of parent sex" attached to the wrong finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Crucially, the coparent's ADHD symptoms were also associated with an individual's postpartum mood symptoms in self-report models, and the pattern held regardless of parent sex."
- **Abstract says:** "Coparent ADHD symptoms were significantly associated with postpartum mood symptoms in the self-report models only. ... Secondary analysis identified self-reported ADHD symptoms and prior mood disorder as predictors of postpartum depressive and anxiety symptoms regardless of parent sex."
- **Problem:** The sex-invariance result concerns a person's *own* ADHD symptoms and prior mood disorder, not the coparent effect. As written, a reader would conclude the coparent association was shown to hold for both mothers and fathers.
- **Suggested fix:** "...in self-report models only. Separately, a person's own ADHD symptoms and prior mood disorder predicted postpartum depressive and anxiety symptoms regardless of parent sex."

### Study 13: A gamified app cuts anxiety by breaking up rumination, and the mediation checks out
**PMID:** 42660574 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts a between-group efficacy result the abstract does not report** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A gamified app cuts anxiety by breaking up rumination"
- **Abstract says:** "Multilevel structural equation modeling revealed that reduction in rumination significantly mediated decreases in depression and anxiety in the intervention group but not in the waitlist condition."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a mediation result, not a head-to-head symptom outcome; no anxiety effect size or intervention-versus-waitlist symptom contrast is given. Whether the app "cuts anxiety" relative to waitlist is unverifiable from abstract only, and the digest's own Caveats note the waitlist comparator problem.
- **Suggested fix:** "In a gamified app trial, falling rumination mediated falling anxiety — and the mediation only held in the treatment arm"

### Study 15: A stronger stress response protects lonely young adults from anxiety
**PMID:** 42302916 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Protective causal language for a cross-sectional moderation analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A stronger stress response **protects** lonely young adults from anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "Low resting PEP (indicating high sympathetic activity) and a greater PEP decrease in response to challenge protected lonely young adults from elevated anxiety and depressive symptoms."
- **Problem:** The verb mirrors the abstract, so this is not a misreading — but a headline stating that a physiological pattern "protects" people will read as causal to a general audience when the evidence is a moderation term in 188 undergraduates with self-reported outcomes. The pitch's own note ("this is correlational moderation") pulls the other way.
- **Suggested fix:** "In lonely young adults, a stronger stress response goes with less anxiety"

### Study 16: An expressive writing trial for trans adults works — and so does the control condition
**PMID:** 42658633 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trial length and follow-up not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "short trial with limited follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** no intervention duration or follow-up period is reported; only "within-group effects of time by condition."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The characterization may well be right, but it cannot be sourced to the abstract.

*Otherwise accurate:* N = 115, the three-arm randomization with an expressive writing active control, the null between-group comparison and the within-group trajectory differences all match, and the digest is right that the abstract's "preliminary empirical support for the efficacy of RECLAIM" softens a between-group null.

### Study 17: Rewriting childhood memories helps hoarders whom standard CBT failed
**PMID:** 42462484 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. A primary outcome that did not move is omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Hoarding symptoms, hoarding beliefs, depression and avoidant attachment all fell with large effect sizes; anxiety and obsessive-compulsive symptoms fell by follow-up"
- **Abstract says:** "Primary outcomes included hoarding symptoms, hoarding beliefs, and negative intrusive imagery ... whereas negative intrusive imagery and anxious attachment showed minimal change."
- **Problem:** Negative intrusive imagery was one of three primary outcomes and showed minimal change — a notable null for an intervention whose whole rationale is rescripting aversive memories. Omitting it presents a uniformly positive picture and weakens the pitch's mechanism claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "...fell by follow-up; negative intrusive imagery — a primary outcome — and anxious attachment showed minimal change."

**Issue B. "Helps" in the headline for an uncontrolled case series** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Rewriting childhood memories helps hoarders whom standard CBT failed"
- **Abstract says:** "These findings suggest imagery rescripting may be a feasible, acceptable and promising adjuvant intervention for HD treatment non-responders."
- **Problem:** N = 10 with a randomized within-person baseline and no control group supports "shows promise," not "helps." The Caveats acknowledge the design, but the headline states the conclusion the authors explicitly hedge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewriting childhood memories shows early promise for hoarders whom standard CBT failed"

### Study 18: Boredom may be a transdiagnostic symptom rather than a mood, and it has a brain signature
**PMID:** 42675870 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "It has a brain signature" is stated as established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and it has a brain signature"
- **Abstract says:** "EEG studies report increased alpha activity, altered theta dynamics, and reduced indices of cognitive control, whereas functional neuroimaging findings highlight greater involvement of the default mode network"
- **Problem:** The first half of the headline is properly hedged ("may be"), but the second half asserts a settled neural signature from a narrative synthesis of a small, heterogeneous literature — which the digest's own Caveats acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Boredom may be a transdiagnostic symptom rather than a mood — and reported neural correlates are starting to accumulate"

### Study 19: Guided deep relaxation for autistic children eases anxiety while heart rate variability moves the wrong way
**PMID:** 42632169 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline claims an effect the study did not detect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Guided deep relaxation for autistic children **eases anxiety** while heart rate variability moves the wrong way"
- **Abstract says:** "Results indicated no statistically significant changes in anxiety over time; however, medium effect sizes were observed in self- (Hedge's g = 0.55) and parent-reported anxiety (Hedge's g = 0.45)"
- **Problem:** The primary anxiety outcome was null in this N = 13 feasibility study. The body text and Caveats say so plainly, so the headline contradicts them and asserts precisely the more exciting result a reader would assume.
- **Suggested fix:** "Guided deep relaxation for autistic children shows medium effect sizes on anxiety — while heart rate variability moves the wrong way"

### Study 20: Telling teenagers with epilepsy that they could die suddenly does not make them more anxious
**PMID:** 42208388 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 30, ages 13–18, median age 14.5, the New Delhi setting, the three-part counseling format, the one-month behavioral improvements, 93.3% changing at least one safety behavior, the unchanged DASS-21 and participant endorsement all match. The pitch explicitly warns that a null on a distress scale is not proof of no distress, which is the right guardrail here.

### Study 21: Adolescents with cannabis use disorder hold distinctive beliefs about their own thinking
**PMID:** 42656180 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Pitch scene asserts a causal direction the design cannot support** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open on a teenager who believes their own thoughts are dangerous and uncontrollable, and uses cannabis to shut them down — the profile these data describe."
- **Abstract says:** "Higher levels of negative metacognitive beliefs (p = 0.006) and total emotional symptoms in the CUD group were positively associated with CUD severity"
- **Problem:** The scene builds in self-medication as the mechanism (beliefs → cannabis use), which a matched cross-sectional case-control study cannot establish — and the digest's own Caveats say "cannabis use could produce the metacognitive profile rather than the reverse."
- **Suggested fix:** "Open on a teenager who believes their own thoughts are dangerous and uncontrollable and who is using cannabis heavily — the association these data describe, with the direction still unknown."
