### Study 1: People with bipolar disorder or schizophrenia accumulate chronic physical illness decades early — and young women fare worst
**PMID:** 42586082 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core numbers check out: 178,665 total, 30,189 cases, median age 32, 1:4 matching, aHRs 1.85 → 3.16, female <25 with ≥5 systems aHR 9.24, no lived-experience involvement, no funding, ethnicity data unavailable. Caveats are well matched to the abstract.

**Issue A. Suggested "outside comment" source is a study co-author** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Call Sean Halstead and Dan Siskind at the University of Queensland; Toby Pillinger at King's College London for outside comment."
- **Abstract says:** Author list includes "Pillinger T(10)" — "Department of Psychosis Studies, Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience, King's College London."
- **Problem:** Pillinger is a co-author of this paper, so he cannot serve as an independent voice on it. A writer working from this digest would burn a call and look unprepared.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call Sean Halstead and Dan Siskind at the University of Queensland (lead and senior authors); note Toby Pillinger at King's College London is also a co-author — for genuinely independent comment, approach a cardiometabolic-psychiatry researcher not on the author list."

**Issue B. Pitch conflates a hazard ratio with an absolute 20-year risk, and a combined SMI group with bipolar disorder** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a 23-year-old woman recently diagnosed with bipolar disorder is at nine times the risk of having five body systems affected within 20 years"
- **Abstract says:** "the highest observed in females younger than 25 years with at least five affected systems (adjusted subdistribution HR 9·24 [95% CI 7·30–11·71])" — with the 20-year figures being separate absolute cumulative incidence differences (11·66 additional cases per 100 persons for ≥5 systems).
- **Problem:** Two splices. The 9.24 is a relative hazard over the whole follow-up, not a 20-year risk; and it applies to the pooled schizophrenia-spectrum *or* bipolar-spectrum group, not to bipolar disorder specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "a woman diagnosed with a schizophrenia- or bipolar-spectrum disorder before 25 has roughly nine times the hazard of accumulating chronic disease in five or more body systems; in absolute terms the group gained about 12 extra such cases per 100 people over 20 years"

### Study 2: Lithium's reputation as the anti-suicide drug does not hold up against valproate
**PMID:** 42765379 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Cohort numbers (1,379 lithium; 5,556 valproate), weighted HR 0.76 (0.54–1.08), network HRs 0.46 and 0.63, and the non-significant lithium-vs-valproate comparison are all accurate. NS fit of 7 is defensible — this is a large IPTW-weighted registry cohort, not small or self-reported.

**Issue A. Misstates what the network meta-analysis pooled** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A network meta-analysis pooling 12 observational studies and 3 trials reached the same conclusion"
- **Abstract says:** "The meta-analysis identified 3 RCTs and 12 observational studies. Meta-analysis of RCTs was precluded due to methodological incompatibilities. Pairwise meta-analysis of eight observational studies showed no significant difference..."
- **Problem:** The three RCTs were explicitly *not* pooled, and the pairwise synthesis used eight, not twelve, observational studies. The digest's own "wider angle" contradicts the body text on exactly this point ("couldn't even pool its three RCTs").
- **Suggested fix:** "A network meta-analysis drawing on the identified observational literature — the three RCTs were too methodologically incompatible to pool — reached the same conclusion"

**Issue B. Pitch presents a one-sided safety trade-off** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open on the clinical dilemma faced by a patient weighing lithium's blood tests and thyroid risks against valproate."
- **Abstract says:** "The choice between these two mood stabilizers should be guided by individual patient characteristics and safety considerations."
- **Problem:** The framing lists lithium's burdens only, implying valproate is the easier option. Valproate carries its own well-established safety constraints (notably in people who can become pregnant), and the abstract's conclusion turns explicitly on safety considerations for both drugs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Open on the clinical dilemma: lithium's monitoring burden and thyroid risk on one side, valproate's own safety restrictions on the other — with efficacy against suicide no longer the tiebreaker."

### Study 3: Walking more lifts your mood, and your mood gets you walking — but screen time only pushes one way
**PMID:** 42765378 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=128, mean age 37.8, 82 bipolar offspring, 32 with mood-disorder history (3 recurrent), 14 days, five pings daily, DSEM, all betas and directions verified against the abstract.

**Issue A. "Everyone's" baseline mood misattributes the group difference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Familial risk didn't change these dynamics — it just shifted everyone's baseline mood down."
- **Abstract says:** "Familial risk status and recurrent mood disorders did not alter these bidirectional effects, but were associated with lower PA and higher NA on average."
- **Problem:** The shift applies to the at-risk participants relative to controls, not to "everyone." As written it reads as though the whole sample's mood dropped.
- **Suggested fix:** "Familial risk didn't change these dynamics — those at risk simply ran lower on positive affect and higher on negative affect throughout."

**Issue B. Headline uses causal phrasing for a within-person observational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Walking more lifts your mood, and your mood gets you walking"
- **Abstract says:** "a credible bidirectional relationship between step count and PA (step count → PA: β = 0.06...; PA → step count: β = .07...)"
- **Problem:** "Lifts" and "gets you walking" assert causation from temporally ordered within-person associations with betas of 0.06–0.07. The authors themselves only go as far as "may help improve daily emotional experiences."
- **Suggested fix:** "More steps track with better mood a few hours later — and better mood with more steps — but screen time only pushes one way"

**Issue C. Nationality of cohort not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dutch cohort"
- **Abstract says:** Author affiliations are Erasmus MC (Rotterdam) and Tilburg University, but the abstract never states where participants were recruited.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. Plausible given the affiliations and the bipolar-offspring design, but it should be confirmed before it appears in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** "Sample almost certainly Dutch (Erasmus MC-led) — confirm recruitment site from full text."

### Study 4: Brain blood flow is lowest in bipolar disorder with psychosis — and higher flow tracks worse symptoms
**PMID:** 42779463 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

Sample sizes (2,412 SZ / 2,278 SAD / 158 BP / 401 NC), 60 regions, d = −0.259 for BP, hypoperfusion in 25 regions, the within-patient positive correlation with psychotic symptoms, and the right superior temporal sulcus finding are all accurate. The caveats are strong. One pitch-level claim, however, contradicts the abstract.

**Issue A. Pitch claims biotypes outperformed diagnosis — the abstract shows the opposite ordering** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "Diagnosis by category barely predicted brain perfusion; a 'biotype' derived from neurophysiology did."
- **Abstract says:** "Compared to NC, patient groups showed significantly lower total gray matter CBF (PSD: Cohen's d=-0.124, p-adjusted=0.037; BP: d=-0.259, p-adjusted=0.004; the most severe biotype, Biotype 1: d=-0.222, p-adjusted=0.006)."
- **Problem:** Both diagnostic categories and the biotype showed significant hypoperfusion, and the *largest* effect belonged to a DSM diagnostic group (BP, d = −0.259), exceeding Biotype 1 (d = −0.222). The pitch asserts a dissociation the data do not show, and it contradicts the very next sentence of the digest, which correctly notes the bipolar group had the largest deficit. An editor sold this hook would find the paper doesn't deliver it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Both DSM categories and neurophysiology-derived 'biotypes' showed perfusion deficits of similar magnitude — with the bipolar group, usually thought of as the milder end of the psychosis spectrum, showing the largest deficit of all."

**Issue B. Lizano's affiliation as listed in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Paulo Lizano at Beth Israel Deaconess"
- **Abstract says:** "Lizano P(3)(14)" — "(3) Department of Psychiatry, University of Rochester Medical Center, Rochester, NY, USA" and "(14) Department of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA, USA."
- **Problem:** The senior author's listed affiliations on this paper are Rochester and Harvard Medical School; Beth Israel Deaconess is affiliation (1), used by other authors. Worth checking before the contact line runs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call Paulo Lizano (University of Rochester Medical Center / Harvard Medical School)"

### Study 5: Long-acting antipsychotic injections help some people with intellectual disability — and may be harming those prescribed them off-label
**PMID:** 42711066 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Design (self-controlled case series), N=1,675, 2004–2023 window, and the broad direction of both subgroup results are accurately reported, as is the industry COI note.

**Issue A. EPS incidence rate ratio attributed to the wrong analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Among those with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, injections were linked to fewer hospitalizations and dramatically fewer extrapyramidal side effects (IRR 0.34)"
- **Abstract says:** The 0.34 (95% CI 0.23–0.51) is reported in the whole-cohort analysis ("LAIA use was associated with lower risks of ... EPS (0.34, 0.23-0.51)"); the schizophrenia/bipolar subgroup result for EPS is reported as directionally lower, without a figure.
- **Problem:** A number from the overall population is presented as the subgroup effect size.
- **Suggested fix:** "...dramatically fewer extrapyramidal side effects (IRR 0.34 in the full cohort; lower in this subgroup too, though no figure is given)"

**Issue B. Off-label subgroup presented as uniformly worse; two favourable results omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Among those *without* such a diagnosis ... injections were associated with *more* all-cause and somatic hospitalizations and no benefit for behavior problems." / Pitch: "The same drug, the same people, opposite outcomes depending on why it was prescribed."
- **Abstract says:** "among PWID without such diagnoses, LAIA use was associated with higher risks of all-cause hospitalisations, hospitalisations for somatic disorders; **a lower risk of incident all-cause ED visits and hospitalisations for CVDs**, but no significant difference in hospitalisations for behaviour-related problems."
- **Problem:** The off-label result is mixed, not reversed. Dropping the two favourable outcomes makes "harming" and "opposite outcomes" read stronger than the abstract's own careful "raising concern about off-label use."
- **Suggested fix:** "Among those *without* such a diagnosis — where the drugs are prescribed off-label, typically for challenging behaviour — the picture was mixed and less favourable: more all-cause and somatic hospitalisations and no benefit for behaviour problems, though fewer ED visits and cardiovascular admissions." And for the pitch: "The same drug, the same people, a markedly less favourable balance of outcomes when prescribed off-label."

**Issue C. Suggested independent critic is a co-author** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Esther Chan at the University of Hong Kong, and Shoumitro Deb at Imperial College London, a long-standing critic of overprescribing in this population."
- **Abstract says:** Author list includes "Deb S(3)" — "Department of Brain Sciences, Faculty of Medicine, Imperial College London, London, UK."
- **Problem:** Deb is a co-author here. He is still a legitimate interviewee, but the digest should say so rather than framing him as an external critic.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call Esther Chan (senior author) and co-author Shoumitro Deb at Imperial College London, a long-standing critic of overprescribing in this population."

### Study 6: The body's own cannabis-like chemicals fall as mania lifts — unless the person uses cannabis
**PMID:** 42735776 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=23 BD I, T0 mania to T1 stabilization, LC-MS/MS, significant AEA and 2-AG decline in non-users, interaction p values, and the null YMRS association are all correct. The "early stage — a hypothesis worth testing" line is a good, honest hedge.

**Issue A. Epub date off by two days** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Published:** 15 December 2026 (epub 16 September 2026)"
- **Abstract says:** "J Affect Disord. 2026 Dec 15;415:122483. ... Epub 2026 Sep 14."
- **Problem:** Wrong epub date; matters only for embargo/priority checks, but it propagates into the citation table.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Published:** 15 December 2026 (epub 14 September 2026)"

**Issue B. "Equivalent clinical improvement" is not established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In cannabis users, levels stayed flat despite equivalent clinical improvement"
- **Abstract says:** "cannabis users showed a disrupted trajectory with no significant reduction in eCB tone despite clinical improvement"
- **Problem:** The abstract says both groups improved clinically; it does not report a formal test showing improvement was *equivalent* between groups. "Equivalent" implies a comparison that isn't in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "In cannabis users, levels stayed flat even though they too improved clinically"

**Issue C. Pitch asserts a biochemical "baseline" the study never measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two patients leave the ward looking equally well; only one of them has biochemically returned to baseline."
- **Abstract says:** Plasma AEA and 2-AG were measured at two timepoints in patients only; no healthy comparison group is described, and levels were unrelated to symptom severity.
- **Problem:** With no control sampling and no pre-illness measurement, "returned to baseline" isn't demonstrable — only that levels declined in one group and not the other. The caveat flags the missing control group, but the pitch line still makes the stronger claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Two patients leave the ward looking equally well; in only one of them has the endocannabinoid signal shifted at all."

*Note (not scored):* Michael Berk is a co-author of this paper, so the "broader bipolar-biomarker view" would come from inside the study team.

### Study 7: People with serious mental illness want AI companions — but only ones built with them, not for them
**PMID:** 42789145 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The body text is a fair rendering of the abstract — concerns about data privacy and misuse, recognized potential for reminders and daily functioning, trust rising with personalization and co-development, worries about overreliance and loss of critical thinking. The equity-interest disclosure is correctly surfaced in both the caveats and the pitch.

**Issue A. Headline overstates qualitative perceptions as a want, and "only" is absolute** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People with serious mental illness want AI companions — but only ones built with them, not for them"
- **Abstract says:** "participants expressed strong concerns about data privacy and misuse, but also recognized AI's potential to support reminders, mental health, and daily functioning. Trust in AI increased when tools were personalized, human-centered, and co-developed with people who understand lived experiences..."
- **Problem:** Three focus groups establish recognized potential and conditional trust, not a want; and increased trust with co-design is not the same as rejecting anything else. The digest body ("simultaneously enthusiastic and wary") is more accurate than its own headline. A smaller point: "simple companionship" substitutes for the abstract's "mental health" in the list of valued uses — defensible given the title quote, but not lifted from the abstract's own wording.
- **Suggested fix:** "People with serious mental illness are open to AI companions — but trust them more when they're built with them, not for them"

### Study 8: A blood test may show whether lithium is reaching someone's brain
**PMID:** 42742966 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=116, depressive episode in bipolar disorder, PBMC gene expression, the five differentially expressed genes and their directions are all correct, as is the limitation list.

**Issue A. "Why it matters" implies relevance to predicting lithium response, which the study did not assess** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Lithium works well for some people and not others, and there is currently no way to tell early which is which. A peripheral transcriptomic signature that tracks brain white matter would be a step toward a blood-based readout of the drug's biological effect"
- **Abstract says:** "This observation supports the potential usefulness of peripheral transcriptomic signatures to mark the individual biological impact of lithium treatment on the brain."
- **Problem:** No treatment-response outcome appears anywhere in the abstract — this is a cross-sectional comparison of treated vs untreated patients. Opening on the responder-prediction problem invites the reader to assume the signature predicts who benefits, which is a different and much bigger claim than "marks biological impact."
- **Suggested fix:** "Lithium's mechanism remains poorly understood, and there is no way to read its biological effect on the brain short of imaging. A peripheral transcriptomic signature that tracks white matter measures would be a first step toward a blood-based marker of that effect — though it says nothing yet about who will respond."

**Issue B. "Those genes" overstates which genes showed the lithium-specific relationship** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "crucially the relationship between those genes' activity and white matter microstructure existed *only* in lithium-treated patients"
- **Abstract says:** "significant lithium-by-gene expression interactions were observed for SEPTIN11, LEF1-AS1, PLXDC1, and DGKK, indicating that the relationship between gene expression and white matter microstructure differed depending on lithium treatment status."
- **Problem:** Four of the five genes showed interactions (TPPP3 did not), and an interaction means the relationship *differed* by treatment status — not necessarily that it was absent in untreated patients. The abstract's "only in lithium-treated participants" applies to specific gene–diffusion-measure pairings.
- **Suggested fix:** "and for four of the five genes the relationship with white matter microstructure differed depending on whether the patient was on lithium — with the specific associations emerging in lithium-treated patients"

**Issue C. Headline frames a transcriptomic marker as a measure of drug delivery** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A blood test may show whether lithium is reaching someone's brain"
- **Abstract says:** "peripheral transcriptomic signatures to mark the individual biological impact of lithium treatment on the brain"
- **Problem:** "Reaching the brain" suggests a pharmacokinetic question (does the drug get there), which lithium serum monitoring already addresses. The study is about biological impact, not delivery.
- **Suggested fix:** "A blood test may show what lithium is doing to someone's brain"

### Study 9: Half of people with anorexia or bulimia also meet criteria for a personality disorder — but almost nothing is known about why
**PMID:** 42777340 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. All five prevalence figures (49%, 54%, 37%, 47.7%, 9.1%), the 61/213 counts, the credibility grading verdict on risk-factor and outcome estimates, the scope limitation to AN/BN/BED, and the wide CI example all match the abstract. Framing correctly avoids causal claims — indeed the entry's whole point is the absence of credible causal evidence. NS fit of 4 is appropriately modest for a review of reviews.
