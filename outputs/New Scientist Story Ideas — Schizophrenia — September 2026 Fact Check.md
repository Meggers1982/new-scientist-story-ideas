### Study 1: Excess covid deaths among people with severe mental illness did not shrink after vaccines arrived
**PMID:** 42586081 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core numbers all check out: 13,463,945 infected people, 160,190 with SMI, overall aHR 1·56, stage aHRs 1·27 / 1·56 / 1·55, vaccination 73·2% vs 67·4% early and 79·4% vs 87·2% by study end, PPI panel, Das-Munshi as corresponding author. NS fit of 8/10 is defensible for whole-country linked records.

**Issue A. Omitted vaccination-attenuation finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The tools that closed the mortality gap for most of the population failed to close it here, and the early advantage in vaccination was lost"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher COVID-19 mortality risk in people with SMI persisted but was partially attenuated by vaccination (aHR 1·25, 1·17–1·34)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that vaccination did partially reduce the excess COVID-19 mortality risk. Saying the tools "failed" here is a shade stronger than the data, and the writer should have the 1·25 figure to hand before pitching a "vaccines didn't help this group" line.
- **Suggested fix:** "Excess mortality persisted throughout — vaccination only partially attenuated the COVID-19 mortality gap (aHR 1·25), and the group's early vaccination advantage was lost."

**Issue B. "Psychosis diagnosis" used for the whole SMI category** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the survival penalty attached to a psychosis diagnosis got *wider* once vaccines were available"
- **Abstract says:** "SMI comprised schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, bipolar disorder, or other affective and non-affective disorder with psychosis."
- **Problem:** The cohort includes bipolar disorder without psychosis; the pitch and "Why it matters" ("reaches people with psychosis") narrow the population inaccurately.
- **Suggested fix:** "the survival penalty attached to a severe mental illness diagnosis — schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, bipolar disorder or other psychosis — got wider once vaccines were available"

### Study 2: Semaglutide beats every other drug tested for antipsychotic weight gain
**PMID:** 42418179 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

95 trials, 39 interventions, N=5898, all five mean differences, the 5% clinically meaningful threshold for semaglutide and metformin, the dropout finding, k=3 for semaglutide, and the industry disclosures are all accurately reproduced.

**Issue A. Headline asserts head-to-head superiority** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Semaglutide beats every other drug tested for antipsychotic weight gain"
- **Abstract says:** "Semaglutide, liraglutide, topiramate, metformin, and exenatide were associated with the greatest reductions in body weight and were supported by the highest-certainty evidence."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports effect sizes versus placebo and certainty ratings, not statistical superiority of semaglutide over each of the other 38 interventions, and no ranking probabilities are given. "Beats every other drug tested" invites an editor to expect head-to-head evidence that the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Semaglutide shows the largest weight reduction of any drug studied for antipsychotic weight gain"

**Issue B. Trial counts misstated in Caveats** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only 2-5 trials each for the top-ranked drugs"
- **Abstract says:** "metformin (MD, −3.86 kg; 95% CI, −5.02 to −2.70; k = 16; moderate certainty)"
- **Problem:** Metformin — one of the five top-ranked agents and one of only two hitting the 5% threshold — rests on 16 trials, not 2–5. The caveat understates the evidence base for the comparator that matters most clinically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Only 2–5 trials each for semaglutide, liraglutide, topiramate and exenatide (metformin is better supported, k=16); short follow-up in most"

### Study 3: A stress-hormone gene is switched up in schizophrenia — and aging pushes it the same way
**PMID:** 42642806 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Cohort sizes (378 cases / 209 controls), tissue (DLPFC and orbitofrontal cortex), data types, the proximal-enhancer CpGs, the all-major-cell-types validation, lower DNAm in schizophrenia and the converging effects of age and rs1360780 are all accurate. Author affiliations check out.

**Issue A. Correlation phrased as mechanism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "specific sites in the gene's proximal enhancer where less methylation means more gene expression"
- **Abstract says:** "CpGs were identified across the proximal enhancer of the FKBP5 locus where DNAm was negatively correlated with gene expression"
- **Problem:** "Means more gene expression" reads as a demonstrated regulatory effect; the abstract reports a negative correlation, and the conclusion is hedged ("may be mediated by these epigenetic mechanisms").
- **Suggested fix:** "specific sites in the gene's proximal enhancer where lower methylation goes with higher gene expression"

**Issue B. Clinical-trial claim not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "*FKBP5* blockers are already in early psychiatric trials"
- **Abstract says:** "FK506-binding protein 51 (FKBP51; encoded by FKBP5) is a promising target for psychiatric therapeutics"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The abstract supports "promising target", not active trials. The writer will need an independent source before this goes in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** "*FKBP5* is already being pursued as a drug target in psychiatry" (verify trial status separately)

### Study 4: Trauma therapy for psychosis works on questionnaires — but not on people's phone diaries
**PMID:** 42658758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=153 (73/80), six-day ESM windows, baseline and 9 months, the five symptom domains and the absence of between-group differences at 9 months are all correct.

**Issue A. "Not reproduced" overstates "not fully replicated", and a supportive finding is omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The trial's headline benefit, measured retrospectively by clinician interview, was not reproduced in the momentary data"
- **Abstract says:** "the robust reductions in PTSD symptoms found in the STAR trial using retrospective outcome measures were not fully replicated with ESM" — and separately, "ESM measures at 9-months were significantly associated with improvements on the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5."
- **Problem:** Two things. "Not reproduced" is stronger than "not fully replicated" given the significant timepoint×therapy interactions favouring therapy. More importantly, the digest omits that momentary measures *were* significantly associated with CAPS-5 improvement — which cuts directly against the headline framing that the two measurement approaches disagree. The "Failed replication" novelty tag compounds this: this is a secondary ESM analysis, not a replication attempt.
- **Suggested fix:** "The trial's headline benefit, measured retrospectively by clinician interview, was not fully replicated in the momentary data — although ESM scores at nine months did track improvement on the clinician-rated PTSD scale."

**Issue B. Sampling frequency invented** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a phone beeping at you eight times a day"
- **Abstract says:** "multiple times daily over six days"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no number of daily prompts. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "a phone beeping at you several times a day" (confirm the prompt schedule with the authors before using a number)

**Issue C. NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10"
- **Problem:** This is a secondary analysis of an RCT subsample whose primary between-group comparison was null and whose outcomes are self-reported momentary ratings; the interesting finding is a measurement mismatch that the abstract itself softens. A 7 overstates it.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — a genuine measurement-mismatch story, but a secondary analysis with a null primary comparison and self-reported outcomes."

### Study 7: Personality traits predict who develops three major psychiatric disorders — but not schizophrenia
**PMID:** 42680113 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The sample breakdown, the four-disorder multiplex design, the unique temperament configurations, the ACEs–AUD-only finding and all four AUCs (0.822 / 0.742 / 0.752 / 0.526) are accurate.

**Issue A. "Predict who develops" implies prospective onset prediction** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Personality traits predict who develops three major psychiatric disorders — but not schizophrenia"
- **Abstract says:** "External validity of our final model was tested to predict diagnosis in an independent cohort. Receiver operator characteristic plots and the area under the curve (AUC) were used to evaluate its prediction accuracy."
- **Problem:** The model classifies existing diagnoses cross-sectionally; nothing here follows anyone forward to onset. The headline (and "predict who develops" in the body) contradicts the digest's own caveat that the study is cross-sectional.
- **Suggested fix:** "Temperament profiles distinguish three major psychiatric disorders — but not schizophrenia"

**Issue B. Controls described as coming from the multiplex families** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "1162 people from 393 Indian multiplex families affected by schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder or alcohol use disorder — 532 affected individuals, 457 unaffected first-degree relatives and 173 controls"
- **Abstract says:** "Affected individuals and their FDRs were from 393 multiplex families…" (the 173 healthy controls are listed separately)
- **Problem:** Only the 989 affected individuals and relatives came from the multiplex families; the controls did not.
- **Suggested fix:** "1162 people — 532 affected individuals and 457 unaffected first-degree relatives from 393 Indian multiplex families, plus 173 healthy controls"

### Study 8: A brain response was supposed to be weaker in people at risk of psychosis — it turned out stronger
**PMID:** 42274463 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=36 CHR (antipsychotic-naïve) and 21 controls, the 40 Hz click-train paradigm, larger evoked power in non-remitters only, the contradicted hypothesis and the hyperexcitability interpretation are all faithfully reported, and the Caveats are appropriately strict.

**Issue A. Non-remission conflated with psychosis onset** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If the same measure runs high before psychosis and low after, then the trajectory — not the snapshot — is the biomarker"
- **Abstract says:** "We compared EP and PLF between CHR patients who remitted over a two-year follow-up period, those who did not remit, and HCPs."
- **Problem:** The outcome was symptom remission versus non-remission at two years, not transition to psychosis. "Before psychosis / low after" implies these participants went on to develop schizophrenia, which the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "If the same measure runs high in people whose early symptoms persist and low in established schizophrenia, the trajectory — not the snapshot — may be the biomarker"

### Study 12: Leading researchers argue the dopamine theory of schizophrenia can no longer stand alone
**PMID:** 42418174 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Twelve authors, the 1980–2025 evidence window, the one-third treatment-resistant / no striatal dopamine synthesis elevation figure, the xanomeline-trospium argument, the full list of proposed alternative systems and processes, and the "expert knowledge" search caveat are all accurate.

**Issue A. "Shows" for the abstract's "suggests"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the efficacy of the muscarinic agonist xanomeline-trospium, which does not block D2 receptors, shows psychosis can be reduced through non-dopaminergic routes"
- **Abstract says:** "The efficacy of the muscarinic M1/M4-preferring agonist xanomeline-trospium, which lacks direct D2 receptor antagonism, suggests that nondopaminergic mechanisms can reduce psychotic symptoms."

### Study 13: A drug that raises prolactin sharply does not harm sexual function more than one that doesn't
**PMID:** 42439529 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=175 (PP 86 / HD 89), 24 months, ASEX, higher prolactin with PP throughout, non-differing ASEX trajectories, the exploratory month 22–24 window favouring PP, the within-person r=0.21 at month 12, the 49% baseline dysfunction prevalence and the single authorship are all accurate.

**Issue A. Headline mischaracterises the comparator** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A drug that raises prolactin sharply does not harm sexual function more than one that doesn't"
- **Abstract says:** "Paliperidone palmitate (PP) and haloperidol decanoate (HD) differ substantially in prolactin liability… PP produced higher prolactin throughout follow-up"
- **Problem:** Haloperidol decanoate is itself a prolactin-elevating antipsychotic; the abstract describes a difference in degree, not a raiser versus a non-raiser. The headline also converts a non-significant difference into a positive claim of no harm, which a 175-person secondary analysis cannot support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Two antipsychotics, very different prolactin levels — and no detectable difference in sexual function over two years"

**Issue B. Non-adherence claim firmed up beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sexual side effects are a major reason people stop antipsychotics"
- **Abstract says:** "Sexual dysfunction is common in schizophrenia and may contribute to poor quality of life and non-adherence."
- **Problem:** "May contribute to" becomes "a major reason".
- **Suggested fix:** "Sexual side effects are thought to contribute to people stopping antipsychotics"

### Study 15: Waiting to prescribe clozapine costs patients function they don't get back
**PMID:** 42155318 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=150, retrospective, early/late TRS subtyping, DSP stratification, greater GAF improvement in the late group, DSP more prevalent there and associated with greater functional gain, the three significant regression predictors, and the delay–GAF correlation confined to the DSP subgroup are all accurately reported, as are the honoraria disclosures.

**Issue A. Causal, irreversibility-implying headline from a retrospective chart review** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Waiting to prescribe clozapine costs patients function they don't get back"
- **Abstract says:** "We observed a significant negative correlation between the length of this delay and GAF improvement only in the DSP subgroup." Design: "We retrospectively analyzed the cases of 150 patients."
- **Problem:** "Costs" asserts causation from an uncontrolled retrospective analysis, and "they don't get back" implies permanence that no outcome beyond one year addresses. - **Suggested fix:** "Longer waits before clozapine are linked to smaller functional gains"

**Issue B. Context claims not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Clozapine is the only drug licensed for treatment-resistant schizophrenia and is chronically underused" / "newly relaxed in the US in 2025"
- **Abstract says:** nothing about licensing status, underuse or US monitoring rules.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. Both are widely reported externally but need their own sourcing before appearing in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** Keep, but attribute to an independent source rather than to this paper.

### Study 17: Confronting an avatar of your hallucination lights up different brain regions than the hallucination itself
**PMID:** 42334305 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=20, both tasks in one fMRI session, left IFG for the hallucination-capture task, left amygdala and left dmPFC for the Avatar task, and the concordant conjunction/disjunction analyses are all accurate, as is the three-ROI caveat.

- **Suggested fix:** "It suggests that avatar exposure engages emotional-threat and social-cognition regions rather than the language region active during hallucinations themselves — a testable account of how the therapy might work."

**Issue B. Medication status not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "all participants medicated"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty participants with schizophrenia and persistent AVHs were recruited." No medication status given.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** Drop, or confirm medication status from the full text.

### Study 20: More empathic people with first-episode psychosis have milder negative symptoms
**PMID:** 42664611 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=100 within a larger RCT, IRI and QCAE at baseline, greater IRI empathy linked to less severe negative symptoms across multiple domains, empathy explaining variance beyond other social cognitive measures, and low internal consistency of both empathy measures are all accurate. Industry disclosures are correctly carried into Caveats.

**Issue A. The finding is described as contradicting an assumption it actually matches** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Negative symptoms — withdrawal, flattened expression, loss of motivation — are often read as an absence of feeling for others; here they varied with self-reported empathy in the opposite direction from that assumption."
- **Abstract says:** "greater empathy, measured by the IRI, was linked to less severe negative symptoms across multiple domains"
- **Problem:** If negative symptoms indexed reduced fellow-feeling, you would expect exactly this: more negative symptoms with less empathy. The finding is consistent with the stereotype the digest says it overturns, and the "Counterintuitive" novelty tag and the pitch's "the stereotype is that psychosis flattens fellow-feeling; these data suggest…" rest on the same inverted reading. As written, an editor would be pitched a reversal that the data do not contain.
- **Suggested fix:** "Self-reported empathy varied meaningfully within this group and tracked negative symptom severity — and, notably, added information beyond standard social cognition tests." Change the novelty tag from "Counterintuitive" to "Incremental".

**Issue B. "Intact" implies a comparison that was not made** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these data suggest empathy is intact enough to vary meaningfully"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants were 100 people with FEP who were taking part in a larger randomized controlled trial." No healthy comparison group is described.
- **Problem:** Without controls, nothing here speaks to whether empathy is intact relative to anyone.
- **Suggested fix:** "these data suggest self-reported empathy varies meaningfully within first-episode psychosis and tracks how withdrawn someone appears"

### Study 22: First measurements of the antipsychotic cariprazine in breast milk

### Cross-cutting notes for the editor

Three patterns recur and are worth a single pass across the whole digest rather than study by study:

1. **Headlines outrunning their body text.** Studies 7, 13, 15, 16, 19 and 21 all have headlines that assert something the entry's own Caveats then walk back. In every case the body copy is accurate and the headline is the problem — the cheapest fix in the document.

2. **Omitted findings that soften the hook.** Studies 1 and 4 each leave out an abstract result that cuts against the framing (partial vaccine attenuation; the significant ESM–CAPS-5 association). These are the two most likely to embarrass a writer in a call with the study author, and should be corrected before either is pitched.

3. **Study 20 needs the most work.** It is the only entry where the stated direction of surprise is logically inverted relative to what was found. The "Counterintuitive" tag, the "Why it matters" paragraph and the pitch angle all depend on the same misreading, so all three need rewriting together, not patching. The 4/10 NS fit is right, and arguably generous once the false reversal is removed.

**Scores I would change:** Study 4 from 7/10 to 6/10 (secondary analysis, null primary comparison, self-reported outcomes). All other 7+ scores — Studies 1, 2 and 3 — rest on whole-country linked records, 95 randomized trials, and two independent postmortem cohorts respectively, and are defensible as written.
