### 1. Girls with ADHD wait until their teens for help — four years after boys get it

**Media check:** Not widely covered ✓ (SERPAPI verified)

**The study:** Using linked administrative health records from a longitudinal population cohort of 89,587 children in New South Wales, Australia, researchers tracked the age at which girls and boys first accessed health services for ADHD up to age 18. Of the 7,402 children (8.3%) who used ADHD-related services, girls' median age of first contact was 13.6 years compared with 9.4 years for boys — a gap of more than four years. The study suggests systemic recognition failures, not just parental delay, are responsible.
