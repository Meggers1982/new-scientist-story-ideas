### Study 1: Autistic children feel others' distress more strongly — but read it less accurately
**PMID:** 42856069 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (two Moderate)

**Issue A. Group difference in physiological reactivity not reported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Autistic children reacted with *greater* affective and physiological intensity than non-autistic children, yet identified the on-screen emotions less accurately."
- **Abstract says:** "Autistic children experienced greater affective intensity but exhibited less accurate emotion recognition than non-autistic children when observing youth exhibiting strong affect. Greater physiological and affective intensity were associated with greater attention shifting…"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a *between-group* difference only for affective intensity. Physiological (electrodermal) intensity appears only as a within-sample predictor of attention shifting — there is no stated finding that autistic children were physiologically more reactive than controls. The pitch angle repeats the error more vividly: "autistic children's sweat-gland responses and self-reported feelings were *stronger* than their peers'."
- **Suggested fix:** "Autistic children reported *greater* affective intensity than non-autistic children, yet identified the on-screen emotions less accurately; across the sample, greater affective and physiological intensity went with more attention shifting." And in the pitch angle: "autistic children's self-reported emotional reactions were *stronger* than their peers', while their emotion labelling was worse."

**Issue B. Causal language for a case-control model** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the two were causally linked in the model via gaze shifting"
- **Abstract says:** "…more frequent attention shifting among autistic children was associated with less accurate emotion recognition" and, in the conclusions, "These reactions *may* lead to attention shifting."
- **Problem:** Every link in the chain is reported as an association in a cross-sectional case-control design. The caveats correctly say the design "cannot establish the causal pathway the model proposes," but the pitch body text asserts the opposite, which is the version a commissioning editor will read first.
- **Suggested fix:** "and the two were linked in the model via gaze shifting — an association, not a demonstrated causal chain."

**Issue C. "Away from the face" is an addition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "intense arousal drove attention shifting away from the face, which degraded emotion recognition"
- **Abstract says:** "Electrodermal reactivity to and eye gaze towards the videos were also measured… greater attention shifting."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to gaze toward the videos and "attention shifting" generically; it does not specify shifting away from faces. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "intense arousal was associated with more attention shifting away from the videos…"

**Issue D. Novelty label and NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research" / "**NS fit:** 8/10"
- **Abstract says:** "Recent evidence suggests that many autistic individuals exhibit heightened emotional responsiveness to others' affect despite greater difficulty recognizing others' emotions… This study introduces and preliminarily evaluates the Emotion Regulation and Recognition Account (ERRA)."
- **Problem:** The paper builds on an existing line of recent evidence and offers "initial support" for a new model; it overturns a popular stereotype, not prior research. 8/10 is defensible on sample and multimodal measurement, but sits a notch high for an explicitly preliminary model evaluation.
- **Suggested fix:** Novelty: "Challenges a popular assumption"; NS fit 7/10.

### Study 2: A cord blood enzyme at birth tracks autism traits six years later
**PMID:** 42843280 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

**Issue A. Suggested "independent" commentator is a co-author** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Worth calling Hideo Matsuzaki at the University of Fukui and Bruce Hammock at UC Davis, whose lab pioneered sEH inhibitors, for a candid read on what this does to the hypothesis."
- **Abstract says:** Author list includes "Morisseau CH(5), Hammock BD(6)" — "Department of Entomology and Nematology, and UCD Comprehensive Cancer Center, University of California Davis."
- **Problem:** Hammock is an author on this very paper, so he cannot serve as the outside reality check the pitch implies. The framing would mislead a commissioning editor about the independence of the quote.
- **Suggested fix:** "Worth calling the corresponding authors Nagisa Hata and Hideo Matsuzaki at the University of Fukui, and co-author Bruce Hammock at UC Davis, whose lab pioneered sEH inhibitors — then an independent sEH researcher for a candid read on what this does to the hypothesis."

**Issue B. Enzyme description not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) — an enzyme that breaks down anti-inflammatory lipid mediators"
- **Abstract says:** "Soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) is involved in regulating neuroinflammation implicated in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) pathophysiology."
- **Problem:** The mechanistic gloss is standard biochemistry but is not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only; keep it attributed to background rather than to this paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH) — an enzyme the authors describe as a regulator of neuroinflammation (and which degrades anti-inflammatory epoxy fatty acids)"

*Everything else checks out:* N = 854, cord serum ELISA, ADOS at 6 years, overall inverse association with calibrated severity scores, boy-specific effects on social affect, restricted/repetitive behaviour and CSS, null in girls, negative sEH–MCP-1 correlation, null MCP-1–ADOS link, and the explicit "causal relationships remain unclear" caveat. NS fit 7/10 is defensible for a prospective cohort of this size with a clinician-administered outcome.

### Study 3: Autism is over-represented in binge eating too — not just anorexia
**PMID:** 42850402 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Drive" overstates "may contribute"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reward processing, emotional regulation and sensory differences may drive binge presentations"
- **Abstract says:** "Recent studies suggest that affective and reward-related processes, alongside sensory differences, may contribute to binge-type presentations."
- **Problem:** "Drive" implies a primary causal role; the review says "may contribute," and this is a narrative synthesis of methodologically limited studies.
- **Suggested fix:** "affective and reward-related processes, alongside sensory differences, may contribute to binge presentations"

**Issue B. Novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research"
- **Abstract says:** "This review summarises emerging evidence on Autism in binge-type EDs… Current AN-centric models may inadequately capture Neurodivergence in binge-type EDs."
- **Problem:** The review broadens an anorexia-focused literature and flags a gap; it does not overturn prior findings. "Corrects a field-wide blind spot" is the accurate label, and the digest's own caveat ("a review flagging a research gap, not new prevalence data") supports that.
- **Suggested fix:** Novelty: "Fills a documented evidence gap."

**Issue C. Unverified claims about the service and about screening** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Kate Tchanturia and Lauren Makin at King's, who run one of the few autism-adapted eating disorder services in the world" / "a route into binge eating that nobody screens for"
- **Abstract says:** "Autism also remains under-recognised in binge-type EDs by ED services, where existing adaptations remain largely derived from AN models."
- **Problem:** Both the "one of the few in the world" superlative and the interoception/screening claim are unverifiable from the abstract; "nobody screens for" overshoots "under-recognised."
- **Suggested fix:** "…who work on autism-adapted eating disorder care at the Maudsley (verify the service description before pitching)" and "a route into binge eating that services rarely screen for."

NS fit 6/10 is defensible for a narrative review with a clear clinical hook.

### Study 4: Brain scans suggest autism comes in two structural flavours
**PMID:** 42853485 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Clinical associations came from the sign-reversed model** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In exploratory analyses, progression along the first was linked to lower verbal IQ and the second to modestly higher ADOS scores"
- **Abstract says:** "In exploratory covariate-adjusted analyses, higher stage in sign-reversed-model S1 was associated with lower verbal intelligence quotient (IQ) scores, whereas S2 showed modestly higher Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) scores."
- **Problem:** The association is specific to the sign-reversed z-score representation, not to the subtypes in general. A writer working from the digest would state it more broadly than the authors did.
- **Suggested fix:** "In exploratory analyses within the sign-reversed model, higher stage on the first subtype was linked to lower verbal IQ and the second showed modestly higher ADOS scores"

**Issue B. "Built to stage Alzheimer's disease"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an algorithm built to stage Alzheimer's disease repurposed to sort a developmental condition"
- **Abstract says:** "We applied Subtype and Stage Inference (SuStaIn) to structural MRI data…" (no disease of origin specified; the digest body says "an algorithm developed for tracking neurodegenerative disease progression," which is also not in the abstract).
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — correct in the wider literature, but should be sourced elsewhere before it anchors the hook.
- **Suggested fix:** "an algorithm first developed for staging neurodegenerative disease (check SuStaIn's original application before leading on Alzheimer's)."

Headline "comes in two structural flavors" is adequately hedged by "suggest," and the caveats correctly note the within-ABIDE validation, exploratory clinical correlates, retrospective pooled dataset and absent longitudinal scanning. NS fit 5/10 is well judged.

### Study 5: Prenatal supplements for preventing autism and ADHD: the evidence isn't there
**PMID:** 42849536 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Explicitly advise against" is stronger than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "explicitly advise against high-dose single-nutrient or concentrated-polyphenol supplementation"
- **Abstract says:** "Clinical translation should prioritize achieving recommended dietary adequacy and balanced dietary patterns; evidence does not support high-dose single-nutrient or concentrated-polyphenol supplementation for ASD/ADHD prevention."
- **Problem:** "Evidence does not support" is an absence-of-evidence statement; "advise against" converts it into a recommendation against use. In a consumer-health pitch that difference matters.
- **Suggested fix:** "and conclude that the evidence does not support high-dose single-nutrient or concentrated-polyphenol supplementation for ASD/ADHD prevention."

**Issue B. Quotation attached to the wrong subject** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors conclude the field is 'an organizing framework rather than a confirmed causal hypothesis'"
- **Abstract says:** "The evidence is therefore best treated as an organizing framework rather than a confirmed causal hypothesis."
- **Problem:** The authors describe the *evidence*, not "the field," this way. The quoted words are accurate but the frame around them is not.
- **Suggested fix:** "The authors conclude the evidence is 'best treated as an organizing framework rather than a confirmed causal hypothesis'."

**Issue C. Novelty label and preclinical detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research" / "specific mouse strains (BTBR, Ts65Dn) and valproate-exposed rats"
- **Abstract says:** "Preclinical data support biological plausibility—particularly for choline in BTBR, maternal immune-activation/CHRNA7, and Ts65Dn models and for resveratrol in valproate-exposed rodents."
- **Problem:** A purposive narrative review with no new data does not overturn prior research; and the abstract says "rodents," not rats, and includes a maternal immune-activation/CHRNA7 model the digest drops. Also worth naming first author Tariq A. Alalwan (University of Bahrain) alongside Perna.
- **Suggested fix:** Novelty: "Corrective synthesis"; "…specific rodent models (BTBR, maternal immune activation/CHRNA7, Ts65Dn) and valproate-exposed rodents." Add Alalwan to the call list.

NS fit 5/10 is appropriate.

### Study 6: An eight-week parent-led program keeps autistic teenagers' job skills from sliding over the summer
**PMID:** 42790248 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

**Issue A. Headline asserts an effect the authors say the study cannot establish** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "An eight-week parent-led program keeps autistic teenagers' job skills from sliding over the summer"
- **Abstract says:** "Preliminary between-group estimates provide a signal of potential benefit for parent-reported vocational readiness but are not intended to establish programme effectiveness."
- **Problem:** "Keeps… from sliding" states effectiveness, and additionally implies the study measured skill decline over summer — it measured parent-rated readiness at baseline and post-intervention, with no demonstrated regression in the comparison group. The caveats and pitch body are appropriately hedged, but the headline is the part that gets pitched.
- **Suggested fix:** "A parent-led summer program shows an early signal for autistic teenagers' job-readiness skills" (or "…hints at holding the line over the summer").

**Issue B. Module-completion denominator** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nearly 90% of intervention parents completed all modules"
- **Abstract says:** "Among intervention completers, 89.7% completed all eight modules."
- **Problem:** The 89.7% is of completers (29 of 32 allocated), not of all intervention parents — a small but standard adherence-reporting distinction.
- **Suggested fix:** "nearly 90% of the parents who completed the study finished all eight modules"

**Issue C. "Teenagers" undersells the age range** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "autistic teenagers' job skills" (headline); "Sixty-four Saudi parent–child dyads, with autistic adolescents and young adults aged 15–21"
- **Abstract says:** "autistic adolescents and young adults aged 15-21 years"
- **Problem:** The body is correct; the headline narrows the population to teenagers when roughly the upper third of the range is adults.
- **Suggested fix:** "autistic adolescents and young adults" in the headline.

NS fit 5/10 is defensible — it is randomized but small, parent-reported, waitlist-controlled and retrospectively registered, all of which the caveats name.

### Study 7: Caregivers of children with congenital CMV report *less* emotional strain when autism is in the picture — at school age
**PMID:** 42853118 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

**Issue A. Different instruments for the two age bands is not flagged** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Caregivers of 181 children with congenital cytomegalovirus… completed quality-of-life questionnaires, split into toddlers (1 to under 4) and school-age children (4 to under 11)."
- **Abstract says:** "Outcomes among toddlers were assessed using the Infant Toddler Quality of Life Questionnaire and school-aged children using the Child Health Questionnaire."
- **Problem:** The entire hook is a reversal of direction between age groups — but the two groups were measured with *different instruments*, so part of the apparent flip could be measurement rather than adaptation. This is a key limitation for the story's central claim and is missing from both the body and the caveats.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to body: "…with toddlers assessed on the Infant Toddler Quality of Life Questionnaire and school-age children on the Child Health Questionnaire — different instruments, which complicates direct comparison." And add to Caveats: "the age-group reversal is measured with two different questionnaires."

**Issue B. Author's advocacy affiliation overlaps with the recruitment channel** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Megan Pesch at the University of Michigan, who works on both cCMV and developmental-behavioral pediatrics."
- **Abstract says:** "Pesch M(1)(2)(3)… (2)National CMV Foundation, Tampa, FL" and "Caregivers of children with cCMV recruited by social media and nonprofit channels."
- **Problem:** The first author is affiliated with the National CMV Foundation while the sample was recruited through nonprofit channels — relevant to the self-selection caveat the digest already raises, and worth disclosing to the writer.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call Megan Pesch at the University of Michigan — note she is also affiliated with the National CMV Foundation, one of the channels through which the sample was recruited."

*Otherwise accurate:* N = 181, the a priori block-wise regression finding that birth-severity markers dropped out after adjusting for current morbidity, the toddler pattern (autism and seizures → more negative emotional impact), the school-age reversal (seizure disorder and autism → less negative emotional impact), and the null family-cohesion result. The "adaptation" interpretation in Why it matters is appropriately hedged ("hints") and flagged in the pitch angle. NS fit 4/10 is well judged.
