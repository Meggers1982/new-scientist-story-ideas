### Study 1: Vigorous exercise fails to protect memory suppression, contradicting an earlier finding
**PMID:** 42579016 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core facts check out: N = 372 healthy young adults, category-exemplar RIF task, two final tests, treadmill at 80% heart-rate reserve versus standing control, RIF replicated, exercise did not preserve it, RIF decreased from test 1 to test 2 driven by improved recall of previously suppressed items.

**Issue A. Attribution of the original finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "from the same lab that reported the original positive effect"
- **Abstract says:** "Recent work suggested that acute aerobic exercise performed between repeated memory tests may preserve the RIF effect over time. The present study sought to directly replicate and extend this finding"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not identify who produced the original positive finding. The whole "self-replication as a scientific virtue" wider angle rests on this. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "a large, well-powered direct replication that comes out negative; verify with the authors whether this lab produced the original positive finding before running the self-replication angle"

**Issue B. Mechanistic overstatement of RIF** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the well-documented effect where recalling some facts actively suppresses related facts you didn't recall"
- **Abstract says:** "selectively retrieving some information impairs later recall of related, non-retrieved information, often interpreted as reflecting inhibitory control processes during memory retrieval"
- **Problem:** "Actively suppresses" states an inhibitory mechanism the abstract explicitly frames as one interpretation, not an established fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "the well-documented effect where recalling some facts impairs later recall of related facts you didn't retrieve — usually interpreted as active inhibition"

### Study 2: Telling people what others do now beats telling them what others are changing
**PMID:** 42615122 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Ns (105, 163), populations, outcomes, pre-registration and the direction of the main effect all match.

**Issue A. What the mediation applies to** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Static norm messages beat dynamic ones on behavior in both — more distance cycled, more total weekly activity — with motivation and self-efficacy as partial pathways."
- **Abstract says:** "There was also some evidence that both types of messages boosted physical activity outcomes indirectly via their positive impact on motivation and self-efficacy."
- **Problem:** The indirect pathway was reported for both message types, not as an explanation of the static-over-dynamic advantage. As written it reads as mediation of the group difference. The abstract also hedges ("some evidence").
- **Suggested fix:** "...more distance cycled, more total weekly activity. Both message types also showed some evidence of working indirectly through motivation and self-efficacy."

**Issue B. NS fit score** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — two pre-registered experiments overturn a fashionable behavior-change tactic, with a behavioral (not just self-reported) outcome in one of them; sample sizes are modest."
- **Abstract says:** N = 105 single-session lab cycling task; N = 163 with interest, intentions and self-reported weekly activity.
- **Problem:** "Overturn" is a strong verb for two modest single-lab experiments with short-horizon outcomes, one of which is entirely self-report. A 7 implies more evidentiary weight than two small experiments support.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — two pre-registered experiments challenge a fashionable behavior-change tactic for physical activity, with one behavioral outcome; small single-lab samples and short-horizon outcomes."

### Study 3: Drinking, smoking and exercise in youth all predict less loneliness later — not the reverse
**PMID:** 42544718 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

n = 3,072, 28 years, Norwegian cohort, propensity-score causal inference, −14.13 minutes/week and BMI +0.37 all check out; the body text correctly says "moderate drinking… and moderate exercise."

**Issue A. Headline drops "moderate"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Drinking, smoking and exercise in youth all predict less loneliness later — not the reverse"
- **Abstract says:** "moderate alcohol use (-0.07 SD, -0.13 to -0.01 and - 0.19 SD, -0.33 to -0.05), smoking (-0.12 SD, -0.23 to -0.01), and moderate physical exercise (-0.16 SD, -0.26 to -0.07 and - 0.07 SD, -0.14 to -0.01) … were linked to reduced loneliness."
- **Problem:** The associations were specific to *moderate* alcohol use and *moderate* exercise; the headline generalizes to drinking and exercise at any level. "All other associations were consistent with a null effect."
- **Suggested fix:** "Moderate drinking, smoking and moderate exercise in youth all predict less loneliness later — more than the reverse"

**Issue B. "Stronger" comparison not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The reverse direction was stronger and stranger"
- **Abstract says:** "Our results provide limited support for the causal effects of loneliness on health behaviors… By contrast, findings in the reverse direction suggest that health behaviors in young people… are socially embedded"
- **Problem:** The abstract contrasts the two directions but does not rank their magnitude, and the reverse-direction effects are small (−0.07 to −0.19 SD) and reported in different units from the forward-direction estimates. "Stronger" implies a comparison the abstract doesn't make.
- **Suggested fix:** "The reverse direction was more consistent and stranger — though the effects were small (−0.07 to −0.19 SD)"

### Study 4: In 214,000 teenagers, a little exercise tracks with more suicidal thinking after violence — a lot tracks with less
**PMID:** 42605073 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one moderate — revise headline)

n = 214,218, KYRBS 2021–2024, 13.3% / 38.9%, elevated ORs at 1–3 days, muscle-strengthening at ≥3 days non-significant in continuous models — all accurate, and the digest correctly labels the findings associational.

**Issue A. Headline converts "attenuated" into a protective effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a little exercise tracks with more suicidal thinking after violence — a lot tracks with less"
- **Abstract says:** "odds of suicidal ideation were elevated at 1-3 days/week (ORs 1.4-1.8) but attenuated at ≥ 4 days/week"
- **Problem:** "Attenuated" means the elevated odds at higher frequencies were no longer raised — not that ≥4 days/week was associated with *less* suicidal thinking than no activity. The headline promises a protective signal the abstract does not report. The body text ("attenuated only at 4 or more days") is correct, so the headline is the error.
- **Suggested fix:** "In 214,000 teenagers, a little exercise tracks with more suicidal thinking after violence — the extra risk disappears only at four days a week"

**Issue B. "U-shaped" mislabels the curve** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "then walk through the U-shaped signal in a survey of a fifth of a million adolescents"
- **Abstract says:** "Aerobic PA showed a non-monotonic interaction with violence"; "frequency-dependent, nonlinear associations inconsistent with single-threshold framing"
- **Problem:** Odds of ideation were highest in the middle of the exposure range, which is an inverted U for the outcome, not a U. The abstract uses "non-monotonic"/"nonlinear" rather than naming a shape.
- **Suggested fix:** "then walk through the non-monotonic signal — risk peaking in the middle of the activity range — in a survey of a fifth of a million adolescents"

### Study 5: Pilates, yoga and qigong split the podium for cancer patients' mental health — and there is a dose ceiling
**PMID:** 42412001 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

73 RCTs, 5,130 participants, g = 0.56 / −0.41 / −0.89, SUCRA rankings and 300–520 MET-min/week modality optima all match.

**Issue A. The 490 MET-min figure is outcome-specific** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The dose-response curve was an inverted U, peaking around 490 MET-minutes a week, with modality-specific optima of 300–520."
- **Abstract says:** "Dose-response analysis identified an inverted U-shaped relationship with an overall optimal dose of 490 MET-min/week for quality of life"
- **Problem:** 490 MET-min/week is the optimum reported for quality of life, not for all three outcomes. As written it reads as a global dose ceiling.
- **Suggested fix:** "The dose-response curve was an inverted U, with the overall optimum for quality of life around 490 MET-minutes a week and modality-specific optima of 300–520."

**Issue B. "May reverse" overreads the inverted U** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the inverted U means prescribing more mind-body exercise past a moderate weekly dose stops helping and may reverse"
- **Abstract says:** "an inverted U-shaped relationship with an overall optimal dose of 490 MET-min/week for quality of life (SMD = 0.904, 95% CrI: 0.603 to 1.235)"
- **Problem:** A modeled inverted U implies diminishing benefit past the peak; the abstract reports no estimate of harm or of benefit crossing below zero, and the conclusion still supports mind-body exercise as beneficial. "May reverse" is an inference the data in the abstract don't carry.
- **Suggested fix:** "the inverted U suggests that benefit stops increasing — and may decline — past a moderate weekly dose"

### Study 7: VR gym classes lift teenagers' fitness and interest — and the effect outlasts the novelty
**PMID:** 42556203 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one moderate — revise headline)

N = 360, ages 13–16, three middle schools, 12 weeks plus 4-week follow-up, all effect sizes (d = 0.78 / 0.62 / 0.55 / 0.48, null for explosive power) and the qualitative themes match.

**Issue A. Headline settles a question the abstract leaves open** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "VR gym classes lift teenagers' fitness and interest — and the effect outlasts the novelty"
- **Abstract says:** "Effects were partially retained at follow-up… a pattern not fully consistent with a purely novelty-driven explanation" and "its limited effect on explosive power and possible novelty contribution indicate that it should complement, rather than replace, conventional PE"
- **Problem:** The abstract concludes novelty may still be contributing; it says only that the retention pattern is *not fully consistent* with pure novelty. The headline asserts novelty has been ruled out over a four-week window.
- **Suggested fix:** "VR gym classes lift teenagers' fitness and interest — and some of the gain survives four weeks later"

**Issue B. "Inconsistent with" versus "not fully consistent with"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which the author argues is inconsistent with a purely novelty-driven explanation"
- **Abstract says:** "a pattern not fully consistent with a purely novelty-driven explanation"
- **Problem:** Drops the hedge, converting a partial argument into a clean refutation.
- **Suggested fix:** "which the author argues is not fully consistent with a purely novelty-driven explanation"

**Issue C. Pitch line names a mechanism the analysis can't establish** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the enthusiasm looks like the mechanism"
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory mediation identified exercise interest as a statistically compatible explanatory pathway (indirect effect = 0.34, 95% CI [0.22, 0.46]), although the timing of measurement precludes causal interpretation."
- **Problem:** The caveat correctly notes the mediation is non-causal, but the pitch line contradicts it by presenting interest as the established mechanism.
- **Suggested fix:** "and interest is the pathway the author's exploratory (non-causal) mediation points to"

- **Problem:** The abstract gives no duration. "Ten minutes" is unverifiable from abstract only and would need confirming from the methods before it appears in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** "A brief single-session compassion writing exercise left people feeling worse and angrier."

### Study 10: Giving stressed doctors a $45 monthly gym-class pass reduced their anxiety
**PMID:** 42362858 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 28, 16-week ClassPass, 20 credits ≈ $45/month, waitlist control, 26 completers, 76.9% engagement, 100% recommending expansion, anxiety p = 0.012 d = 1.07, non-significant depression (d = 1.06) and loneliness (d = 1.12), null burnout — all correct, and the industry disclosures are properly carried into Caveats.

**Issue A. "Stressed doctors" implies a screened population** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Giving stressed doctors a $45 monthly gym-class pass reduced their anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "Twenty-eight psychiatry residents were randomized to receive a 16-week ClassPass membership (n = 14) or waitlist control (n = 14)."
- **Problem:** Participants were psychiatry residents at one center, not selected for elevated stress, anxiety or burnout. The headline suggests an at-risk sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "Giving psychiatry residents a $45 monthly gym-class pass reduced their anxiety"

**Issue B. Definitive verb for a pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reduced their anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "The intervention was associated with a statistically significant reduction in anxiety (p = 0.012, d = 1.07)" and "may reduce anxiety symptoms among psychiatry residents… Larger studies are needed to confirm these findings."
- **Problem:** The trial is randomized, so causal phrasing is not fatal, but with n = 28, a waitlist comparator and self-reported outcomes, the authors deliberately write "may reduce." A flat "reduced" in a headline outruns a pilot.
- **Suggested fix:** "…may have eased their anxiety — in a 28-person pilot"

### Study 11: Feeling hungry predicts skipping your workout, not just breaking your diet
**PMID:** 40415394 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 81 Dutch adults, two seven-day EMA weeks, six semi-random prompts, and every odds ratio quoted (1.14, 0.83, 0.68, 0.78, 0.81/0.81) matches the abstract.

**Issue A. Implies a significant stress finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Emotional states other than relaxation and stress showed little."
- **Abstract says:** Significant associations for exercise lapse were feeling hungry, feeling relaxed, coping self-efficacy and recovery self-efficacy; for dietary lapse, coping and recovery self-efficacy only. Stress is listed as measured but not reported among significant predictors.
- **Problem:** The phrasing groups stress with relaxation as a state that showed something. The abstract reports no significant stress association in either domain.
- **Suggested fix:** "Beyond feeling relaxed, no emotional state — including stress — showed a significant association."

**Issue B. Cross-domain hunger claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hunger leaks across domains to undermine activity, not only food choices"
- **Abstract says:** Hunger was significant for exercise lapse (OR = 1.14); the dietary-lapse results list only coping and recovery self-efficacy as significant.
- **Problem:** The line implies hunger predicts dietary lapse as well; in this dataset it was only a significant predictor for exercise. The genuinely surprising finding is the reverse of what the phrasing suggests.
- **Suggested fix:** "hunger predicted exercise lapses — and, in this sample, not dietary ones"

**Issue C. Publication date omits online-first date** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Journal:** *Psychology & Health* | **Published:** August 2026"
- **Abstract says:** "Psychol Health. 2026 Aug;41(8):1103-1120. doi: 10.1080/08870446.2025.2509070. Epub 2025 May 25."
- **Problem:** The paper has been online since May 2025 — more than a year before the stated coverage window. Relevant to novelty and media-check confidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Published:** August 2026 (Epub May 25, 2025)"

### Study 12: Being tuned in to your heartbeat says nothing about how well you read your gut
**PMID:** 42526739 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 33, three domains (inspiratory resistive loads, esophageal balloon, graded cycle ergometry), null cross-domain sensitivity correlations with anecdotal Bayesian support for the null, and the partial affective convergence (respiratory unpleasantness correlating with both esophageal and cardiac) all match. The Caveats already flag the pilot status and the weak Bayes factors, so the strong headline is not re-flagged.

**Issue A. Novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research"
- **Abstract says:** "However, evidence suggests interoceptive abilities may be domain-specific rather than a general ability."
- **Problem:** The abstract positions the study as adding evidence to an existing line of work suggesting domain specificity, not as overturning a settled consensus. "Overturns prior research" oversells the paper's position in the literature — and the wider angle ("how shaky the measurement foundations are") leans on that label.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Adds evidence against a widely assumed general ability"

### Study 13: When people are told to train according to how they feel, half the time they deliberately don't
**PMID:** 42034345 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one moderate — revise headline)

N = 24, 14-day FNLP invitation, pre/post-exercise surveys, data-visualization-prompted interviews, reflexive thematic analysis, and the concordant/discordant themes all match.

**Issue A. Headline states a frequency the study never reports** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "When people are told to train according to how they feel, half the time they deliberately don't"
- **Abstract says:** "Resource-concordant decisions were perceived to support recovery, training overload, and novel activities. Resource-discordant decisions were made to distract from stressors, for social engagement, or in response to self-imposed expectations."
- **Problem:** "Half the time" is a quantitative claim; the abstract reports no proportion of concordant versus discordant decisions, and the study is a reflexive thematic analysis, not a frequency count. Unverifiable from abstract only, and as a headline number it would be quoted onward.
- **Suggested fix:** "When people are told to train according to how they feel, they often deliberately don't"

**Issue B. Drops "perceived to"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Resource-concordant choices supported recovery, overload and trying new activities."
- **Abstract says:** "Resource-concordant decisions were perceived to support recovery, training overload, and novel activities."
- **Problem:** These are participants' perceptions gathered in interviews, not measured training outcomes. The digest's own caveat notes "no fitness outcomes measured," which this sentence obscures.
- **Suggested fix:** "Resource-concordant choices were perceived to support recovery, overload and trying new activities."

### Study 14: Psychiatry's bipolar disorder body finally puts exercise on the treatment agenda
**PMID:** 42545207 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The ISBD NExT task force, five databases searched to 1 March 2026, question-and-answer format, the sedentary-behavior and self-report/objective discrepancy findings, the mixed prospective/Mendelian randomization evidence on onset, and the 82% antidepressant response rate from a single pilot all match. The disclosure caveat is appropriately included.

**Issue A. "First" position statement** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the first ISBD position statement on movement in bipolar disorder"
- **Abstract says:** "An Evidence Synthesis and Position Statement From the ISBD Nutrition and Exercise Task Force (NExT)" — no priority claim.
- **Problem:** The abstract never says this is the first such statement. Unverifiable from abstract only, and "First-in-class" novelty plus the "finally" in the headline both rest on it.
- **Suggested fix:** "an ISBD position statement on movement in bipolar disorder, notable for what it admits is missing"

**Issue B. Mania caution not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open on the exercise caution specific to bipolar disorder: the worry, largely untested, about intense activity and mania."
- **Abstract says:** Nothing about mania risk from exercise; it reports benefits for functioning, mood episode frequency, hospitalizations, depressive symptoms, anxiety and sleep.
- **Problem:** The proposed opening scene is not supported by anything in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only — check the full text before building the lede on it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Open on the gap the statement concedes: promising early trials, almost no robust data. Confirm from the full text whether the statement addresses mania risk before leading with it."

### Study 15: "Drunkorexia" splits into two distinct patterns — and doing both marks the highest-risk students
**PMID:** 42641412 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 2,801, six universities, past-month drinkers, mean age 19.48, 77.6% female, 68.8% White, the four motive groups, the MANCOVA covariates, and both the FAD-Both and single-motive comparisons are all reported accurately.

**Issue A. "Punishing exercise after drinking" glosses the measured variable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "punishing exercise after drinking clusters specifically with body-image distress"
- **Abstract says:** "the FAD-CC group reported elevated body dissatisfaction, cognitive restraint, and excessive exercise compared to the FAD-AE group"
- **Problem:** The outcome is a general "excessive exercise" eating-disorder domain, not exercise performed after drinking or as alcohol-calorie compensation. The digest's phrasing implies a timing and motive the measure does not capture.
- **Suggested fix:** "excessive exercise as a general pattern clusters with body-image distress among the calorie-compensation group"
