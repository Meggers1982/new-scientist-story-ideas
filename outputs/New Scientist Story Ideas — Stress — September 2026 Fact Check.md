### Study 1: Treating sleep alongside PTSD leaves service members sleeping better six months later
**PMID:** 42660579 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect sizes misattributed and mis-ranged** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the TMT group's sleep efficiency, onset latency, quality and time awake after falling asleep improved by 6 months (g = 0.51–0.70)"
- **Abstract says:** "those randomized to TMT exhibited better sleep efficiency (g = 0.24) and onset latency (g = -0.34) at 3-month follow-up, and better sleep quality (g = 0.70), efficiency (g = 0.51), and wake after sleep onset (g = -0.52) at 6-month follow-up."
- **Problem:** Two errors bundled together. First, onset latency was a 3-month between-group finding (g = -0.34) and falls outside the quoted 0.51–0.70 band; only quality, efficiency and WASO were reported at 6 months. Second, these g values are *between-group* differences (TMT vs CPE), not the size of within-group improvement in TMT, which is what the sentence implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "By 6 months the TMT group was doing better than the exposure-only group on sleep quality (g = 0.70), sleep efficiency (g = 0.51) and wake after sleep onset (g = -0.52); at 3 months the between-group advantages were smaller (efficiency g = 0.24, onset latency g = -0.34)."

*Note on NS fit:* 7/10 is defensible here — randomized allocation plus objective actigraphy and 6-month follow-up outweigh the modest N of 82.

### Study 2: Trauma therapy for children works even while the bombs are still falling
**PMID:** 42635370 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Publication date** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Published:** September 2, 2026 (issue December 2026)"
- **Abstract says:** "Eur J Psychotraumatol. 2026 Dec;17(1):2713259 ... Epub 2026 Aug 24."
- **Problem:** The online publication date was 24 August 2026, not 2 September. The issue assignment (December 2026) is correct.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Published:** August 24, 2026 (issue December 2026)" — and update the citation table row accordingly.

**Issue B. Training modality not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Dutch, German and Italian trainers teaching Kyiv therapists over video"
- **Abstract says:** "EMDR C&A trainers from three European countries (the Netherlands, Germany, and Italy), who trained Ukrainian therapists."
- **Problem:** The three trainer nationalities are correct, but the abstract says nothing about remote/video delivery or that trainees were based in Kyiv. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Dutch, German and Italian trainers teaching Ukrainian therapists (confirm training format with the authors)"

**Issue C. NS fit score for uncontrolled pre-post data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — genuinely novel setting and large effects, but no control group and open-label delivery."
- **Abstract says:** "Despite the methodological limitations of this study ... Future studies should replicate these findings and evaluate the effects of EMDR during war conditions using a randomised controlled design."
- **Problem:** A 7 rests on a single-arm, unblinded, self- and caregiver-reported pre-post design with no comparator; the authors themselves frame it as preliminary. The setting is the story, not the evidence strength.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — genuinely novel setting and large pre-post effects, but uncontrolled, unblinded and reliant on self- and caregiver-report."

### Study 3: Stress doesn't stop you exercising — if anything you move slightly more
**PMID:** 42612762 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Overturns prior research" novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Overturns prior research"
- **Abstract says:** "Stress is frequently cited as a barrier to physical activity, yet evidence from daily life remains inconclusive and may depend on the stress aspect ... physical activity indicator ... and temporal window studied."
- **Problem:** The authors position this against an *inconclusive* literature, not an established finding. Nothing is overturned; a folk belief is contradicted, which the "NS fit" line already says.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Contradicts a widely held assumption"

**Issue B. "Why it matters" leaps past the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which suggests the barrier is a story people tell after the fact rather than a moment-to-moment process"
- **Abstract says:** "Within-person associations between stress indicators and subsequent physical activity were often non-significant, varied across time lags and physical activity indicators."
- **Problem:** The study measured neither retrospective attributions nor beliefs, so it cannot speak to whether the barrier is a post-hoc narrative. Mostly null within-person associations in 76 weight-loss maintainers support a weaker claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "which suggests the moment-to-moment link between stress and inactivity is much weaker than the retrospective self-reports the field relies on — at least in this group."

### Study 4: Sailors who nap most often report less job stress — and more burnout
**PMID:** 42692480 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Study design not stated in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Caveats:** Cross-sectional and observational (cannot establish causation)"
- **Abstract says:** "This study examined whether nap frequency was associated with occupational stress, burnout, impaired functioning, and health outcomes in a large sample of active-duty US Navy sailors."
- **Problem:** The abstract never specifies a cross-sectional design (or a sample size). The caveat is conservative rather than overstated, but it should be marked as inferred until the full text is checked. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Design appears cross-sectional and observational (confirm in full text); abstract reports neither sample size nor effect magnitudes."

All statistical directions — lower odds of high job stress and impaired functioning, higher odds of burnout, ≥5 nap-days/week and poor mental health days, null for poor physical health days — match the abstract exactly.

### Study 5: Stress makes people grab the smaller, sooner reward — even when money is not tight
**PMID:** 42693377 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal language for a cross-sectional association** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Stress makes people grab the smaller, sooner reward" / "Stress does not make you reckless with your pension — it makes you reckless with a hundred quid."
- **Abstract says:** "In multiple regression, greater perceived stress was associated with higher DD rates for $100 (p < .05) and PD rates for $100 (p < .01)."
- **Problem:** No stress manipulation was performed; this is a regression on self-reported perceived stress in a single-session online sample. "Makes" asserts causation the design cannot support — and the digest's own caveats say so.
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "People under more stress discount small rewards more steeply — even when money isn't tight." Pitch: "Higher stress tracks impatience over a hundred quid, but not over your pension."

**Issue B. Hypothetical laboratory rewards presented as everyday spending** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "suggesting stress shapes everyday micro-decisions more than life-defining ones" / "Open on the mundane stressed purchase: the taxi, the takeaway, the impulse buy."
- **Abstract says:** "Future research is needed to examine the association between these factors across varying populations, and how elevated stress levels may negatively impact real-world decision-making involving delay and uncertainty."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly flag real-world decision-making as an open question. Discounting rates for hypothetical $100 and $10,000 rewards are not evidence about taxis or takeaways.
- **Suggested fix:** "suggesting the stress–impatience link may be confined to smaller stakes, though whether that carries over into real spending is untested."

**Issue C. "Nobody predicted" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lead on the magnitude split, which nobody predicted."
- **Abstract says:** Nothing about whether the magnitude effect was anticipated; magnitude effects in discounting are a long-established phenomenon.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and likely overstated given the literature on magnitude effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Lead on the magnitude split — the association held for $100 but vanished at $10,000."

### Study 6: Resilience doesn't protect young trauma survivors who also have OCD
**PMID:** 42697249 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates a moderation null** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Resilience doesn't protect young trauma survivors who also have OCD"
- **Abstract says:** "Resilience was associated with lower PTSS and slightly greater symptom improvement over time, but did not specifically buffer against the elevated PTSS associated with OCD."
- **Problem:** The headline reads as though resilience confers no benefit to youth with OCD; what failed was the *interaction* term (p = .509). Resilience's main effect applies across the cohort. The body text and pitch angle get this right, so only the headline needs adjusting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Resilience helps trauma-exposed youth — but doesn't specifically shield those with OCD"

Sample (1,179, ages 8–20, Texas Childhood Trauma Research Network, baseline plus 1, 6 and 12 months), the 2.4-fold figure (MR = 2.42) and the comparison group are all reported accurately.

### Study 7: Most post-concussion symptoms track distress, not the blow to the head
**PMID:** 42173054 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim that symptom scales cannot distinguish injury groups** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the uncomfortable finding that symptom scales cannot distinguish head-injured from otherwise-injured patients in the acute phase" / "people who never hit their heads report a lot of the same things"
- **Abstract says:** "Anxiety sensitivity explained a small additional increment beyond demographics, clinical factors, injury status, and distress ... These findings support a role for symptom-appraisal processes, alongside injury status, in early PCS reporting after trauma."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports injury status as a covariate in the model and concludes that appraisal operates *alongside* injury status — it never reports that mTBI and orthopaedic controls had indistinguishable symptom profiles. The digest asserts a between-group null the abstract does not contain. The headline's "not the blow to the head" carries the same overstatement.
- **Suggested fix:** Wider angle: "the finding that psychological distress accounts for a large share of acute symptom reporting in both head-injured and orthopaedically injured patients — check the group comparison in the full text before pitching." Headline: "Most acute post-concussion symptoms track distress as much as the blow to the head."

The N (169; 100 mTBI, 69 orthopaedic), setting (Level 1 emergency department, Sydney), timing (mean 4.64 days), ΔR² = 0.012 and 77.7% variance are all correct.

### Study 8: People with two or more diagnoses lost far more well-being in the pandemic than their conditions predict
**PMID:** 42686352 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Supra-additive examples belong to the wrong outcome** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "combinations of conditions produced non-linear, supra-additive declines — anorexia nervosa plus schizophrenia, or inflammatory bowel disease plus psychosis, were associated with drops far larger than the sum of their parts"
- **Abstract says:** "Multimorbidities were associated with non-linear/supra-additive adverse outcomes beyond single disorders (pre-pandemic: anorexia nervosa+schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorder (β=-27.58±12.71...), inflammatory bowel disease+psychosis (β=-24.89±10.59...) ...; pre-to-intra-pandemic: haemorrhoids+obsessive-compulsive disorder (β=-8.86±6.54...), binge-eating disorder/bulimia nervosa+social anxiety disorder ..., back pain+retinopathy ...)."
- **Problem:** The two pairings the digest leads on describe *pre-pandemic well-being levels*, not pandemic-era decline. The pairings associated with supra-additive pre-to-intra-pandemic *change* are entirely different (haemorrhoids + OCD; binge-eating/bulimia + social anxiety; back pain + retinopathy). Since the headline and pitch angle both hinge on "lost far more well-being in the pandemic," the story's central hook currently rests on the wrong outcome variable. Note also that the binge-eating/social anxiety OR range (0.78–2.85) crosses 1.
- **Suggested fix:** "Crucially, combinations of conditions were associated with non-linear, supra-additive effects. The lowest pre-pandemic well-being was seen with anorexia nervosa plus schizophrenia (β = -27.58) and inflammatory bowel disease plus psychosis (β = -24.89); the steepest pre-to-intra-pandemic *declines* were seen with pairings such as haemorrhoids plus OCD (β = -8.86) and back pain plus retinopathy (β = -6.55)." Rework the pitch angle around whichever set the story actually needs.

Sample (121,066 adults, 155 countries), WHO-5, retrospective pre-pandemic rating, the 2–3-fold psychiatric-vs-somatic contrast, and the demographic skew (64% female, 69% white) are all reported correctly, and the competing-interest declarations are appropriately flagged in Caveats.

### Study 10: Stressing people out doesn't change how cues pull them toward a habit
**PMID:** 41680576 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Failed replication" novelty label** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**Novelty:** Failed replication"
- **Abstract says:** "we used a quasi-avoidance-based PIT task to explore whether stress can influence the magnitude of specific or general PIT effects ... The results do not support the proposal that stress influences the size of PIT effects in human participants."
- **Problem:** These are original tests of a hypothesis using a novel quasi-avoidance task, not attempts to reproduce a specific prior experiment. Calling it a failed replication implies a target study that the abstract does not identify.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Novelty:** Null result against a popular theory"

Both stress inductions, the two-experiment structure and the double null on specific and general transfer are described accurately, and the caveat about unreported sample sizes is correct.

### Study 13: For homeless young adults, family support predicts less depression — friends don't
**PMID:** 42691267 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Borderline significance of the suicide-attempt odds ratio** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "those with no family had over three times the odds of a past-year suicide attempt"
- **Abstract says:** "youth with no family in their support network had greater odds of a past-year suicide attempt versus youth with high familial support (OR = 3.16, p = .05)"
- **Problem:** The OR is accurate, but p = .05 sits exactly on the conventional threshold in a study whose depression findings were more comfortably significant. Presenting it as an unqualified headline number overstates the certainty. The word "predicts" in the headline also implies more than cross-sectional baseline data can deliver.
- **Suggested fix:** "those with no family had roughly three times the odds of a past-year suicide attempt, though this result sat right at the significance threshold (OR = 3.16, p = .05)." Headline: "For homeless young adults, family support is linked to less depression — friends aren't."

### Study 15: Pressure to hold onto your heritage — not to assimilate — tracks heavier drinking
**PMID:** 42682243 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Source of "pressure against acculturation" attributed to the in-group** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pressure *against* acculturation (from one's own community)" / "the pressure that predicts drinking runs the other way — from within the in-group"
- **Abstract says:** "two distinct components of acculturation stress-the pressure to acculturate and the pressure against acculturation-and their respective associations with alcohol use severity."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify who exerts the pressure against acculturation. The in-group attribution is a plausible reading of the construct but is not stated, and the pitch angle's whole reversal hook rests on it. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "pressure *against* acculturation — check with the authors how the measure attributes the source of that pressure before framing it as coming from within the in-group."

Sample (628 Hispanic emerging adult college students, 18–25, cross-sectional online convenience sample), the null for pressure to acculturate, the positive association for pressure against acculturation, and the three moderators are all reported correctly.
