### Study 1: A scale now exists for "main character syndrome" — and it looks more like identity confusion than narcissism
**PMID:** 42696901 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

All numbers check out: N=135 / N=263, unidimensional structure, r=0.677 / 0.630 / 0.564, identity diffusion strongest in the multivariate model, Lebanese American University, Pia Tohme as corresponding author. Framing ("suggests", "correlated with") is appropriately correlational.

**Issue A. Sample described as students** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "limited by cross-sectional student samples"
- **Abstract says:** "two independent samples of Lebanese young adults"
- **Problem:** The abstract never says the participants were students. The caveats elsewhere correctly say "young adults in one country," so the NS fit line contradicts the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "limited by cross-sectional samples of young adults in a single country"

### Study 2: Virtual influencers who overshare put people off — unless they are beautiful
**PMID:** 42765465 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The core finding is reported correctly: self-disclosure hindered parasocial relationship formation unless the VHI was perceived as attractive; perceived humanness amplified the parasocial-relationship → emotional-attachment path. N=540, journal, volume/issue and epub date all match.

**Issue A. Sample nationality not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sample is young adults in South Korea already familiar with virtual influencers"
- **Abstract says:** "online survey data from 540 young adults familiar with VHIs" (authors are based in South Korea; participant nationality is not stated)
- **Problem:** The South Korean sample nationality is inferred from author affiliation, not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "sample is young adults already familiar with virtual influencers, recruited by a South Korea-based team (participant nationality not specified in the abstract)"

### Study 3: Punishing a teenager for being upset predicts worse doomscrolling months later
**PMID:** 42817062 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

N=2873, two measurement occasions, conflict as bridge node, punitive responses → parental distress reactions and withdrawal symptoms, salience → tolerance/relapse and reduced parental expressive encouragement: all accurate. Tianjin Normal University and first author Tan D are correct.

**Issue A. Causal verbs in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "suggests the loop runs in both directions — parenting shapes scrolling, scrolling erodes parenting"
- **Abstract says:** "earlier punitive responses predicted increases in subsequent parental distress reactions… higher levels of earlier salience were associated with increased tolerance and relapse and reduced parental expressive encouragement"
- **Problem:** "Shapes" and "erodes" assert causation from a two-wave observational cross-lagged network. The caveats flag this, but the body text contradicts the caveat, which is the case the brief says to flag.
- **Suggested fix:** "suggests the associations run in both directions — earlier parenting responses predict later scrolling symptoms, and earlier scrolling salience predicts later drops in parental encouragement"

**Issue B. "Months later" interval not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "predicts worse doomscrolling months later"
- **Abstract says:** "A total of 2873 adolescents participated at both measurement occasions" — no interval given
- **Problem:** The gap between waves is not reported in the abstract; "months later" is an assumption. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "predicts worse doomscrolling at a later wave"

### Study 4: Liking a vaping post is a stronger marker of heavy tobacco use than just seeing one
**PMID:** 42229276 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=70,773, NYTS 2021–23, three latent classes at ~96%/~3%/~1%, passive exposure linked to predominantly e-cigarette use, active engagement linked to both classes with larger effect sizes, dose-response with frequency — all correct. University of Queensland and Carmen Lim are correct. Funding is government/NHMRC, not industry; no omission. The pitch angle explicitly warns against causal reading, which is appropriate.

**Issue A. "Nationally representative" not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "nationally representative N=70,773"
- **Abstract says:** "Data were drawn from the 2021-23 U.S. National Youth Tobacco Survey (Ntotal = 70,773)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not characterize the sample as nationally representative (true of NYTS generally, but not verifiable here). A copy edit should either source it or soften it.
- **Suggested fix:** "a three-year national school-based survey sample of N=70,773"

### Study 5: Personal trainers say their own bodies are the product — and it's wrecking their body image
**PMID:** 42785604 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts harm the qualitative data can't support** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Personal trainers say their own bodies are the product — and it's wrecking their body image"
- **Abstract says:** "participants also experienced pressure to uphold unrealistic standards online… presenting an image of authenticity online whilst concealing body image struggles from consumers"
- **Problem:** "It's wrecking their body image" is a causal, quantified-sounding damage claim drawn from 17 reflexive-thematic-analysis interviews about *perceptions*. The study reports pressure and concealment, not measured deterioration.
- **Suggested fix:** "Personal trainers say their own bodies are the product — and describe hiding their body image struggles from clients"

**Issue B. "An actual eating problem" is not in the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the trainer who posts a vulnerability video while concealing an actual eating problem"
- **Abstract says:** "concealing body image struggles from consumers"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes body image struggles, not eating disorders or disordered eating. Escalating this to a clinical eating problem invents a finding. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "the trainer who posts a vulnerability video while concealing their own body image struggles"

**Issue C. Participant location inferred from affiliations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "UK/Ireland-based sample"
- **Abstract says:** Author affiliations are University of Edinburgh and Atlantic Technological University, Sligo; participant location is not reported.
- **Problem:** Where the 17 coaches were based is not stated — online trainers may be geographically dispersed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "recruited by a UK/Ireland-based team; participant location not reported in the abstract"

**NS fit note:** 6/10 for an N=17 interview study is at the top of what's defensible. I would score it 5 and lean on the novelty of the creator-side angle rather than the evidence weight.

### Study 6: Men preoccupied with getting bigger report more sexual problems
**PMID:** 42809933 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

The body text is accurate — Appearance Intolerance and MDDI total were the consistent correlates; Drive for Size showed fewer, smaller associations. N=1116, recruitment via social media ads in 2024, MDDI, eight NATSAL domains all check out. The problem is the headline.

**Issue A. Headline contradicts the study's actual split** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Men preoccupied with getting bigger report more sexual problems"
- **Abstract says:** "Greater muscle dysmorphia symptoms, particularly Appearance Intolerance and the MDDI total score, were consistently associated with a broad range of sexual dysfunction… Drive for Size and Functional Impairment showed fewer, smaller, significant associations."
- **Problem:** The headline names the Drive for Size construct ("preoccupied with getting bigger"), which is the subscale that performed *worst*. It also contradicts the digest's own pitch angle ("wanting to be huge isn't the part that predicts trouble in bed"). A reader skimming the headline takes away the opposite of the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "Men who are distressed by how their body looks — not those chasing size — report more sexual problems"

**Issue B. Population described as "men"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Men preoccupied with getting bigger…"
- **Abstract says:** "sexually active boys and men… males aged 15-35 years"
- **Problem:** The sample includes adolescents from age 15; the paper's own title says "boys and young men." The digest body gets this right (15–35) but the headline and "Why it matters" say men.
- **Suggested fix:** "boys and young men (15–35)"

### Study 7: Most bereaved partners have sexual memories of the person who died — and mostly find them comforting
**PMID:** 40208217 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=165, Leger panels/social media/grief forums, 80.5%, 31.8% weekly-or-more, positive valence, and the three correlates of frequency (continuing bonds, past sexual satisfaction, posttraumatic growth) are all correctly reported. Quinn-Nilas at Memorial University is correct. Publication and epub dates match.

**Issue A. "Near-universal" overstates 80.5%** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It was near-universal: 80.5% did so"
- **Abstract says:** "Sexual reminiscing was very common (80.5% of participants sexually reminisced about the deceased)"
- **Problem:** One in five participants did not sexually reminisce; "near-universal" (repeated in the pitch angle) inflates a "very common" finding in a self-selected sample.
- **Suggested fix:** "It was very common: 80.5% did so"

**Issue B. "First evidence" claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first evidence that it is both common and, for most people, not distressing"
- **Abstract says:** "post-bereavement sexual experiences are often disregarded in both research and clinical settings"
- **Problem:** The abstract claims neglect of the topic, not priority. "First evidence" is unverifiable from abstract only; also note valence was measured as positivity, not absence of distress.
- **Suggested fix:** "this is among the first evidence that it is common and, for most participants, positively rather than negatively experienced"

### Study 9: Fear of being cancelled is showing up in therapy rooms as a distinct anxiety
**PMID:** 42296638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Correctly identified as non-empirical, correctly attributed to two Fordham psychologists, and the guest-editor recusal is properly flagged in the caveats.

**Issue A. "Distinct anxiety" vs. transdiagnostic presentation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Fear of being cancelled is showing up in therapy rooms as a distinct anxiety"
- **Abstract says:** "Fear of cancellation presents across diagnoses, including generalized anxiety disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, and social anxiety disorder"
- **Problem:** The authors describe a presentation that cuts *across* existing diagnoses — the headline's "distinct" invites a reader to hear "new disorder," which the digest body correctly avoids.
- **Suggested fix:** "Fear of being cancelled is showing up in therapy rooms — across several anxiety diagnoses"

**Issue B. Behaviors and evidence base embellished** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "checking and deleting" / "clinical claims rest on case observation rather than systematic study"
- **Abstract says:** "excessive review of prior content for potentially offensive material or even abstaining from social media use altogether"
- **Problem:** Deleting posts is not among the listed behaviors, and the abstract does not say the claims derive from case observation — it simply presents no data at all. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "compulsively re-reading old posts for anything potentially offensive, or withdrawing from social media entirely" / "clinical claims are asserted without supporting data in the abstract"

### Study 10: Social media and AI are quietly rewriting what psychiatric terms mean
**PMID:** 41770174 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Mercer University, the utilitarian framing, the stigma-reduction benefit weighed against inaccuracy/overpathologization/diagnostic drift, the practice recommendations and the proposed new APA ethical guidance are all correctly summarized, and the caveats correctly label it conceptual.

**Issue A. "Measurable systems problem"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "frames a familiar irritation… as a measurable systems problem"
- **Abstract says:** "explores the ethical implications of this evolving digital landscape through a utilitarian framework"
- **Problem:** Nothing in this article is measured; "measurable" implies quantification the piece does not offer.
- **Suggested fix:** "frames a familiar irritation… as a systems problem"

**Issue B. "Driving overpathologization" vs. "potential for"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while also driving overpathologization, drift away from diagnostic criteria, and the spread of inaccuracies"
- **Abstract says:** "has also facilitated the spread of inaccuracies, potential for overpathologization, and departures from diagnostic criteria"
- **Problem:** The authors hedge overpathologization as a *potential*; the digest asserts it as an occurring effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "while also, they argue, facilitating inaccuracies and creating potential for overpathologization and departures from diagnostic criteria"

### Study 11: The more you understand how targeted ads work, the harder you work to avoid them
**PMID:** 42790213 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues (one Moderate)

N=977, Pakistan, SEM, β=0.228 (skepticism), β=0.437 (intrusiveness), β=0.150 (avoidance), and the sequential mediation are all reported correctly, as is the observation that intrusiveness carries the larger coefficient. Author and affiliation correct.

**Issue A. Causal claim in the pitch angle** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "teaching people how personalization works makes them avoid it"
- **Abstract says:** "persuasion knowledge is positively associated with ad skepticism… perceived intrusiveness… and ad avoidance" and "greater knowledge of persuasive intent is associated with skepticism and perceived intrusiveness, which are associated with advertising avoidance"
- **Problem:** "Teaching… makes them avoid" describes an intervention effect. This is a single cross-sectional survey with no manipulation of persuasion knowledge — the digest's own caveat says so, which the pitch text contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "people who understand how personalization works report avoiding it more"

### Study 12: Labeling a robot's fact-check as coming from a regulator doesn't make people believe it more
**PMID:** 42765662 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The central null is reported accurately: source condition did not significantly predict post-correction accuracy or successful correction, and between-condition prefrontal differences did not survive correction. The orbitofrontal/subjective-rating result and the exploratory status of connectivity analyses are also correct, as is the instruction to treat the imaging cautiously.

**Issue A. Overstates the corrective shift** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Corrections did shift judgments in the corrective direction overall"
- **Abstract says:** "Corrections were followed by judgement change; descriptively, more scenario-level changes occurred in the corrective direction than in reverse."
- **Problem:** The abstract flags this as a descriptive observation, not a tested effect. "Did shift" reads as an inferential result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Judgements changed after correction, and descriptively more of those changes ran in the corrective direction than in reverse"

**Issue B. Direction of the perceived-capability effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "slapping a government logo on an AI fact-check made people rate the AI as smarter"
- **Abstract says:** "source cues influence perceived AI capability and are associated with aspects of prefrontal processing"
- **Problem:** The abstract states that source cues moved perceived capability but does not report which source raised ratings. Attributing the uplift specifically to the regulator condition is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "the attributed source changed how capable people judged the AI to be, without making them any more likely to change their minds"

### Study 13: In online arguments about returning wolves, people blame the wolf
**PMID:** 42804846 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Discursive psychology, wolves and beavers in the Netherlands, the four named discursive practices, the stance-signalling function, Anne Kessels at Wageningen, and the Spotler Engage / writing-assistance declarations are all correctly reported, and the lack of quantification is properly caveated.

**Issue A. "Hardening the divide" overstates a hedged claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "hardening the divide between camps"
- **Abstract says:** "making visible and potentially reinforcing differences between participants"
- **Problem:** The authors hedge with "potentially reinforcing"; "hardening" asserts an effect that qualitative discourse analysis cannot demonstrate.
- **Suggested fix:** "making those differences visible and potentially reinforcing them"
