### Study 1: Mindfulness courses genuinely lower levels of the stress hormone cortisol
**PMID:** 42691284 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core numbers all check out: 39 studies, 2,059 participants, between-group g = −0.38, inactive controls g = −0.42, active controls g = −0.29, within-group effect present in RCTs (g = −0.37) but not uncontrolled studies (g = −0.06). Author and institution correct.

**Issue A. "8-week" program length not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "39 trials of mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) — the standardized 8-week program"
- **Abstract says:** "Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR), a standardized multiweek program"
- **Problem:** The abstract never specifies eight weeks. MBSR conventionally runs eight weeks, but the pooled trials are described only as "multiweek," and the "Why it matters" and wider angle both lean on the eight-week figure. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "39 trials of mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) — the standardised multiweek programme (conventionally eight weeks)"

**Issue B. Active controls recast as "app-sized doses"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this analysis suggests the full eight-week, in-person program has measurable physiology behind it, while the app-sized doses tested against active controls do much less."
- **Abstract says:** "MBSR had larger reductions compared with inactive controls (k = 23, g = −0.42) and smaller reductions compared with active controls (k = 11, g = −0.29)."
- **Problem:** The active/inactive contrast is about what the *comparison* condition was, not about the dose of mindfulness delivered to the intervention arm. Every arm analyzed was MBSR. Nothing in the abstract tests app-delivered or abbreviated mindfulness, so the wider angle's dose–response hook is not supported by the data.
- **Suggested fix:** "this analysis suggests the full MBSR programme has measurable physiology behind it, though the effect shrinks when it is compared against an active control rather than a waitlist — a reminder that part of the benefit may be non-specific. It says nothing directly about app-delivered mindfulness, which was not tested."

**NS fit 7/10:** Defensible — pooled RCT evidence on an objective biomarker.

### Study 2: People who put things off rate their own health as worse — and stress explains part of it
**PMID:** 42569817 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

36 samples, N = 8,603, r = .21 and r = .13, semi-partial effects reduced-but-significant for SRH and non-significant for future SRH, and the slight expected improvement over 10 years all match. Sirois/Durham correct; "and a colleague" matches the two-author paper.

**Issue A. "Body-wide consequences" overstates a self-rated outcome** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It reframes procrastination as a stress-generating trait with body-wide consequences rather than a time-management quirk"
- **Abstract says:** "chronic procrastination is associated with poor overall health status that can be explained in part by higher levels of stress" — the outcome throughout is self-rated health (SRH).
- **Problem:** "Stress-generating" implies a causal direction the cross-sectional design cannot support, and "body-wide consequences" implies measured physical health outcomes. The only outcome is a subjective health rating. The Caveats correctly note this, so the body text contradicts its own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "It reframes procrastination as a trait that travels with higher stress and poorer self-rated health, rather than being a mere time-management quirk — though the data cannot say which comes first."

**Issue B. NS fit score high for entirely self-reported cross-sectional data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10"
- **Problem:** Every input — procrastination, stress, current and future health — is self-reported, and every sample is cross-sectional. The large N pools the same design weakness 36 times. A 7 puts it level with Study 1, which rests on RCT biomarker data.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — large multi-sample dataset on an everyday trait, but every measure is self-reported and every sample cross-sectional."

### Study 3: Newborn babies can already hear when their own language is spoken with a foreign rhythm
**PMID:** 42464912 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 44 Czech newborns, foreign-accented rhythm via prolonged foot-initial syllables, larger hemodynamic response to the foreign-rhythm version, right-lateralized — all accurate. The "traditional theories" framing matches the abstract closely.

**Issue A. "Single center" not stated in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "N = 44, single center"
- **Abstract says:** Author affiliations span Charles University (Prague and Hradec Králové) and Havlíčkův Brod Hospital; recruitment sites are not described.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and the affiliation list suggests more than one site may have been involved.
- **Suggested fix:** "N = 44; recruitment site(s) not specified in the abstract"

**Issue B. NS fit 7/10 on N = 44** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — overturns a textbook claim about what newborns can hear; clean design, but modest N and a single language pairing."
- **Problem:** The score is defensible on novelty and on the objectivity of the measure, but 44 infants, one language and one accent manipulation is thin ground for a 7 that rests on "overturns a textbook claim."
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — overturns a textbook claim, but on 44 infants and a single language/accent pairing, replication in another rhythm class is needed."

### Study 4: The evidence that therapy helps bullying victims largely vanishes once publication bias is corrected
**PMID:** 42658011 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

23 studies (CBT n = 15, imagery n = 8), g = 0.72 unadjusted pre-post internalizing, adjusted g = 0.26 and non-significant, self-perception/coping adjusted g = 0.98, follow-up internalizing g = 0.37 still significant, I² = 68–98% — all correct. Ghent/Koster correct.

**Issue A. Headline overstates the scope of what vanished** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The evidence that therapy helps bullying victims largely vanishes once publication bias is corrected"
- **Abstract says:** "after adjusting for publication bias, the pre-post internalizing effect dropped to g = 0.26 and was no longer statistically significant. Conversely, the adjusted effect for self-perception and coping increased to g = 0.98, whereas the follow-up internalizing effect decreased (g = 0.37) but remained significant."
- **Problem:** Only one of three adjusted estimates lost significance. Another *increased* after adjustment and a third stayed significant. The headline claims a general collapse of the evidence base that the paper does not report; the body text correctly describes the split, so the headline is out of step with the entry it introduces.
- **Suggested fix:** "Correcting for publication bias flips which benefit of therapy for bullying victims looks strongest"

**Issue B. Internal inconsistency in the NS.com check** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS.com check:** No recent NS.com coverage found ✓ (1 hits)"
- **Problem:** The tick and the hit count contradict each other. If there is one hit, the prior-coverage check needs resolving before pitching.
- **Suggested fix:** Resolve the hit and restate: either "No recent NS.com coverage found ✓ (0 hits)" or "1 possible prior hit — check before pitching."

### Study 5: Brain stimulation for PTSD works on questionnaires but not on clinician interviews
**PMID:** 42680058 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

16 RCTs, 786 participants in the primary sham comparison, significant large effect on self-reported symptoms, non-significant interview-based outcomes, wide prediction intervals, sparse follow-up evidence, mild adverse events, low-to-very-low certainty — all accurate. Peñate-Castro / University of La Laguna correct.

**Issue A. "Clinician" interviews not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "not on clinician interviews" / "trained assessors can't detect it"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings based on interview-reported outcomes did not reach statistical significance."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "interview-reported," not who conducted the interviews or whether raters were blinded. The digest's NS fit line goes further still ("blinded-rater outcomes"). Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "not on interview-based measures" — and in the NS fit note, "a clean split between self-report and interview-based outcomes."

**NS fit 6/10:** Defensible.

### Study 6: Men recover from PTSD in about a decade — for women it takes twice as long
**PMID:** 42303590 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

n = 15,893, lifetime prevalence 7.1%, projected lifetime risk 9.6%, OR 2.3 for females, projected lifetime remission 63.3%, three-quarters remitting by 10 years (males) versus 20 years (females), no change since 2007, and the comorbidity odds cited in the wider angle (asthma 2.0, diabetes 1.8, sedative use disorder 7.9) all match. Mills/Matilda Centre correct.

**Issue A. Headline generalizes a 75th-percentile figure into typical recovery** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Men recover from PTSD in about a decade — for women it takes twice as long"
- **Abstract says:** "Projected lifetime remission was 63.3% ... Three quarters entered remission after 10 years for males and 20 years for females."
- **Problem:** The 10/20-year figures are the point at which three quarters of those who ever remit have done so — not an average recovery time — and only about 63% remit at all. The headline reads as though everyone recovers, on that timetable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Three-quarters of men with PTSD remit within a decade — for women it takes twenty years"

**NS fit 6/10:** Defensible for nationally representative diagnostic-interview data.

### Study 7: On days you procrastinate, tomorrow's stress rises — and stress feeds back into attention problems
**PMID:** 42695429 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

### Study 8: Difficulty naming your own emotions seems to protect fragile narcissists from burnout
**PMID:** 42700715 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 1,239, Swiss inpatient clinic, April 2018–November 2024, alexithymia/ERI/overcommitment as predictors, and the significant negative alexithymia × narcissistic vulnerability interaction (b = −0.207, p = .016) with attenuation at high alexithymia — all accurate. Ledermann / University Hospital Zurich correct.

**Issue A. Omits that narcissistic vulnerability itself was only a trend** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Unexpectedly, the alexithymia × narcissistic vulnerability interaction was significant and negative: at high levels of alexithymia, the effect of narcissistic vulnerability on burnout was attenuated."
- **Abstract says:** "Narcissistic vulnerability showed a trend toward significance (b = 0.124, p = .062). The alexithymia × narcissism interaction was significant (b = −0.207, p = .016)."
- **Problem:** The main effect being buffered did not itself reach significance. A headline built on "protects fragile narcissists from burnout" implies an established harm that alexithymia then blunts; the paper reports a non-significant main effect and a significant interaction, which is a much weaker foundation. A reader — or an editor — would assume more.
- **Suggested fix:** Add to the study description: "Narcissistic vulnerability alone only trended toward predicting burnout (p = .062), so the buffering interaction is modifying an effect that was not itself statistically significant."

**Issue B. "Single strongest predictor" claim on unstandardized coefficients** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "effort–reward imbalance remains the single strongest predictor in this dataset"
- **Abstract says:** "Burnout was significantly predicted by alexithymia (b = 0.241, p < .001), ERI quotient (b = 0.431, p < .001), overcommitment (b = 0.063, p < .001), psychological distress (b = 0.025, p = .004), and age (b = −0.014, p < .001)."
- **Problem:** These are unstandardized coefficients on differently scaled measures; they cannot be ranked directly. ERI has the largest b, but "strongest predictor" isn't established.
- **Suggested fix:** "effort–reward imbalance at work was one of the strongest predictors reported"

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 9: Loneliness predicts later suicidal thoughts even after accounting for depression and anxiety
**PMID:** 42642073 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 4,277, ages 18–89, two waves, four age groups, covariates (gender, social circle, depression, anxiety, stress, prior lifetime suicidality), and 25.2–39.3% variance explained all match. Teismann / Ruhr-Universität Bochum correct. Framing is appropriately prospective-associational and the pitch explicitly says "risk assessment research, not a treatment claim."

**Issue A. "Across all four age bands" is an inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across all four age bands, loneliness remained a significant predictor after controlling for..."
- **Abstract says:** "three-step hierarchical regression analyses were conducted in each age group, respectively ... Loneliness proved to be significantly associated with T1-suicidal ideation and/or behaviour, beyond affective symptoms, social circle and previous suicidal ideation and/or behaviour."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the analyses were run per age group and reports the overall conclusion, but does not state that the association was significant in every one of the four groups. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Loneliness remained a significant predictor after controlling for gender, size of social circle, depression, anxiety, stress and prior lifetime suicidality; analyses were run separately in four age bands (check with the authors whether it held in each)."

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 10: Living with long-term stress may blunt, not amplify, the daily link between hassles and low mood
**PMID:** 42595230 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N = 61 YMSM living with HIV, three US Adolescent Trial Network sites, 66 days of phone-based daily assessment, baseline chronic HIV stressors, daily stressors associated with both higher level (b = 0.45) and greater intraindividual variability (b = 0.42) of depressed mood, and attenuation rather than amplification by chronic HIV stressors — all match. The "Why it matters" mirrors the authors' own two candidate explanations (dysregulation versus adaptiveness of some adversity), the headline is hedged with "may," and the Caveats flag the N, the specificity of the population and the possibility of reporting artefacts.

### Study 11: Erratic dinner times are linked to more depressive symptoms — breakfast and lunch aren't
**PMID:** 42624213 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

450 adults aged 20–59, Tehran, three non-consecutive 24-h recalls, composite phase deviation scores, dinner irregularity B = 1.08 for depressive symptoms, nulls for breakfast, lunch, anxiety and stress, and the null tertile analysis (correctly placed in Caveats) all check out. Shab-Bidar / Tehran University of Medical Sciences correct, and the pitch responsibly warns against implying treatment.

**Issue A. Analytic N for the key finding is 404, not 450** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "assessed 450 healthy adults aged 20–59 using three non-consecutive 24-hour dietary recalls"
- **Abstract says:** "This cross-sectional study included 450 apparently healthy adults ... Linear regression analyses included participants with complete data for all model variables (n = 404)."
- **Problem:** The B = 1.08 result the entry is built on comes from the 404-person complete-case linear models, not the full 450. Small but worth stating in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** "assessed 450 healthy adults aged 20–59 ... with the linear models run on the 404 participants with complete data"

**Issue B. Circadian-anchor mechanism asserted beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "then explain why the evening meal may be the body clock's most important non-light cue"
- **Abstract says:** "Dinner timing irregularity may be associated with higher depressive symptom scores in this sample ... Future prospective and intervention studies are needed to clarify temporality and underlying mechanisms."
- **Problem:** The study tested no circadian mechanism and made no claim about the relative strength of meal timing as a zeitgeber. The hedge "may" softens it, but the pitch presents it as the explanation for the finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "then explore the chrononutrition hypothesis that meal timing acts as a non-light cue for the body clock — a mechanism this study did not test"

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 12: Children born preterm run hot: raised heart rate at five, but normal stress regulation
**PMID:** 42693548 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

70 preterm children aged 5.5 versus 32 matched full-term controls, 98% White, greater social difficulties and internalizing problems, consistently elevated heart rate, comparable HRV, conversation tasks eliciting strongest autonomic activation — all accurate. Boets / KU Leuven correct, and the "hyperarousal not impaired regulation" framing matches the authors' own conclusion.

**Issue A. "Exactly the same way" overstates "comparable"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "their heart rate variability tracked social context in exactly the same way as full-term peers"
- **Abstract says:** "HRV patterns were comparable with the full-term group ... Across both groups, RMSSD-HRV varied by interaction partner ... while HF-HRV reflected general condition effects independent of partner familiarity."
- **Problem:** "Comparable" is not equivalence; the study was not powered as an equivalence test, and "exactly the same" invites readers to treat a null difference as proof of identical regulation.
- **Suggested fix:** "their heart rate variability tracked social context in much the same way as full-term peers"

**Issue B. "Parent-reported social difficulties" not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "parent-reported social difficulties"
- **Abstract says:** "Preterm children demonstrated significantly greater social difficulties and internalizing problems than full-term peers" — the source of these ratings is not stated.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. Same applies to "single center," though the UZ Leuven affiliation makes it plausible.
- **Suggested fix:** "social and internalising difficulties (measurement source not specified in the abstract — confirm with the authors)"

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 14: Children whose parents and coaches both push winning report the most stress — and the least fun
**PMID:** 42419608 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 347, 64.6% female (rounded to 65%), mean age 12.5, five latent profiles, mastery-dominant profiles reporting adaptive outcomes and performance-leaning profiles maladaptive ones, and the dual mastery-and-performance profile reporting both simultaneously — all accurate. Fuller and Moore / East Carolina University correct, and the Caveats correctly rule out causal reading.

**Issue A. Headline superlative not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Children whose parents and coaches both push winning report the most stress — and the least fun"
- **Abstract says:** "When athletes perceived climates that were predominately performance-involving, they reported more maladaptive outcomes (e.g., ego orientations, low enjoyment, high stress)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports directional group differences, not a ranking establishing that this profile scored highest on stress and lowest on enjoyment of all five. "Most" and "least" are superlatives the abstract doesn't license — and the more interesting finding the entry itself leads with is the *dual* profile.
- **Suggested fix:** "Children whose parents and coaches both push winning report more stress — and less fun"

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 15: Growing up in a war zone changes which brain circuits track a child's attachment style

No factual errors found. ### Study 16: The largest genetic map yet of everyday moodiness and impulsivity points to two different brain regions
**PMID:** 42679763 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

13 GWAS, n = 64,000–1,200,000, 184 EXT and 31 INT loci, 70 novel genes (52 + 18), hippocampal CA4 for EXT and amygdalar excitatory neurons for INT, and the independent PRS cohort of 4,565 all match. Franke / Radboud correct, and the Medice disclosure is properly carried into the Caveats.

**Issue A. "Largest genetic map yet" is not claimed by the authors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The largest genetic map yet of everyday moodiness and impulsivity"
- **Abstract says:** No priority or size claim is made; the paper describes "a multivariate framework to population-based, non-clinical traits."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and superlatives of this kind are the first thing an editor will ask you to source.
- **Suggested fix:** "A large genetic map of everyday moodiness and impulsivity points to two different brain regions"

**Issue B. Genetic correlation described as shared variants with a behavioral effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the same variants that make someone restless in the general population are the ones enriched in ADHD and substance-use disorder"
- **Abstract says:** "EXT correlated with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and substance-use disorders" (via LD score regression).
- **Problem:** Two errors in one clause. First, "make someone restless" is causal language for GWAS association data. Second, a genome-wide genetic correlation is not a statement that specific identified variants are "enriched in" a diagnosis; it is an aggregate statistic about shared genetic architecture.
- **Suggested fix:** "the genetic variation associated with externalizing traits in the general population overlaps substantially with the genetic architecture of ADHD and substance-use disorders"

**Issue C. "Predicted" for a polygenic score association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "internalizing polygenic scores predicted emotional well-being in an independent cohort of 4,565 people"
- **Abstract says:** "PRS-CS demonstrated association of INT liability with emotional well-being."
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies useful individual-level prediction — which the digest's own Caveats correctly deny ("effect sizes at the individual level are tiny and non-predictive"). The body contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "internalizing polygenic scores were associated with emotional well-being in an independent cohort of 4,565 people"

**NS fit 5/10:** Defensible.

### Study 17: Older men with more mentally rich lifestyles report less stress — but more loneliness
**PMID:** 42687444 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N = 80 (33 men), ages 65–101, DASS-21/SWLS/UCLA/CRS, the male-only association of higher CRS with lower perceived stress and greater loneliness, associations with selected attention measures but not memory span, and the authors' own caution about sex-specific findings all match. The Caveats and the "Not much yet" framing in "Why it matters" are appropriately restrained.

**Issue A. Contact attributed to the wrong institution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Call Mikhail Ordin at the University of the Basque Country"
- **Abstract says:** Ordin's affiliations are "Faculdade de Medicina, Universidade de Coimbra" and "Interdisciplinary Centre for European Sudies, Europa Universität Flensburg." The University of the Basque Country is the affiliation of the fourth author, Polyanskaya.
- **Problem:** A writer following the digest would approach the wrong institution's press office.
- **Suggested fix:** "Call Mikhail Ordin at the University of Coimbra (or Leona Polyanskaya at the University of the Basque Country)"

**Issue B. Cognitive reserve measure described more broadly than the authors do** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "measures of cognitive reserve (built from educational, occupational and leisure engagement)"
- **Abstract says:** "The CRS was used as a proxy measure of the leisure-activity contribution to cognitive reserve rather than as a comprehensive or direct measure of cognitive reserve."
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly narrow what the CRS captures here. The digest's own wider angle notes "how thin the evidence for the leisure component" is, so the body text is at odds with its own angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "measures of cognitive reserve — used by the authors specifically as a proxy for the leisure-activity component of reserve, not a comprehensive measure"

**NS fit 4/10:** Defensible, and correctly justified in the note.

### Study 18: A key theory of what drives suicidal thinking holds up in the US but not in the UK
**PMID:** 42683538 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Sample sizes (US college n = 865; UK community n = 734), the defeat/entrapment–ideation associations in both samples, higher lifetime ideation among SGM participants in both, and the moderation appearing only in Sample 1 and only for entrapment, are all accurately reported in the body. O'Connor as IMV originator is correct.

**Issue A. Headline reverses what actually failed to replicate** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A key theory of what drives suicidal thinking holds up in the US but not in the UK"
- **Abstract says:** "Defeat and entrapment were significantly associated with suicide ideation in both samples. In Sample 1, SGM status moderated the association between entrapment and suicide ideation ... In Sample 2, no moderation effects of SGM status were found."
- **Problem:** The IMV model's core prediction held in *both* countries. What did not replicate is a single moderation effect involving SGM status. As written the headline tells a reader the theory failed in the UK, which is the opposite of what the paper found — and the digest's own NS fit note concedes "the main effects replicate fine."
- **Suggested fix:** "A suicide theory's core holds in both the US and UK — but the minority-stress twist only shows up in one"

**Issue B. "Predicted" for cross-sectional associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Defeat and entrapment predicted suicidal ideation in both samples"
- **Abstract says:** "Defeat and entrapment were significantly associated with suicide ideation in both samples."
- **Problem:** "Predicted" implies temporal ordering the design does not provide; the Caveats correctly note the study is cross-sectional and ideation is measured retrospectively.
- **Suggested fix:** "Defeat and entrapment were associated with suicidal ideation in both samples"

**NS fit 4/10:** Defensible.

### Study 19: Nonbinary people describe the current political climate in the language of trauma and bereavement
**PMID:** 42658624 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Mixed methods, thematic analysis of write-in survey responses, chi-square tests by race, gender expression and geography, the four themes, and both subgroup findings (rural → loss of authenticity; gender-nonconforming → loss of potential future) are all accurate. Matsuno / Arizona State correct, and the Caveats correctly flag the absence of clinical outcome measures.

**Issue A. "Assess and code" implies clinical guidance the study cannot give** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a framing with direct implications for how clinicians assess and code such distress"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings from this study present further conceptualizations of sociopolitical trauma and stress among the TNB community."
- **Problem:** "Code" points at diagnostic coding. This is a qualitative thematic analysis with no diagnostic instruments, no comparison group and no clinical outcomes; it cannot support implications for clinical assessment or coding practice. The digest's own Caveats say "no clinical outcome measures."
- **Suggested fix:** "a framing that could change how clinicians and researchers conceptualise this kind of distress"

**NS fit 4/10:** Defensible, and correctly justified by the qualitative design.
