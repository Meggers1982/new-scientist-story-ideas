### Study 1: Relaxation exercises may make some talk therapies work less well, huge patient-level analysis finds
**PMID:** 42546730 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Core numbers check out: 30 trials contributing IPD from 10,612 participants (72.2% women, mean age 36.7), social support −9.48, behavioural activation −4.15, problem management −4.08, relaxation +7.97, cognitive reframing +5.17 ("less clearly" detrimental in the abstract, which the digest reflects as "also on the detrimental side"). Funding (European Commission) and the Furukawa app/pharma disclosures are correctly carried into Caveats.

**Issue A. Funder misattributed in wider angle** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and what a WHO-funded personalization web app means for delivering mental health care in countries with almost no psychiatrists."
- **Abstract says:** "FUNDING: European Commission." / "Personalised effect estimates can be computed via a freely accessible web application."
- **Problem:** The digest's own Caveats correctly say the study was funded by the European Commission, but the wider angle calls the web app "WHO-funded". Several authors sit at WHO Collaborating Centres and one is WHO staff, but that is not the same as WHO funding, and the abstract states a different funder. An editor pitched on this would print a wrong funder.
- **Suggested fix:** "and what a freely accessible personalisation web app, from a European Commission-funded team working with WHO collaborating centres, means for delivering mental health care in countries with almost no psychiatrists."

**Issue B. Trial settings not verifiable from the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "trials were largely in low-resource and humanitarian settings, so generalization to specialist Western care is uncertain"
- **Abstract says:** Describes only "task-shared psychosocial interventions… delivered by non-specialist providers" for "adults with common mental disorders"; no breakdown of trial settings is given.
- **Problem:** The task-shared/non-specialist framing is supported, but "largely in low-resource and humanitarian settings" (and the same claim in the pitch angle) is Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "trials tested task-shared delivery by non-specialist providers, so generalisation to specialist Western care is uncertain (check the included-trial table for the setting mix before pitching)."

### Study 2: Psychological first aid after trauma performs worse than doing nothing in a randomized trial
**PMID:** 42561812 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N (84; PFA 47, control 37; 74 completing follow-up), the medium-effect-size immediate advantage for controls, the null at two weeks, and the high-initial-distress interaction are all accurately reported.

**Issue A. Headline implies real trauma exposure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Psychological first aid after trauma performs worse than doing nothing in a randomized trial"
- **Abstract says:** "This randomised controlled trial examined the efficacy of PFA compared to natural recovery in reducing psychological distress using a validated stress-video paradigm."
- **Problem:** The Caveats and pitch both correctly flag the analogue paradigm, but the headline says "after trauma", which readers will take to mean real traumatic incidents. The headline also flattens a mixed result (worse immediately, no difference at two weeks except in a subgroup) into a blanket "performs worse".
- **Suggested fix:** "Psychological first aid did worse than natural recovery in a lab trauma-video trial"

**Issue B. NS fit score high for the evidence base** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 8/10 — a randomized test of one of the world's most widely promoted post-disaster interventions, with a genuinely uncomfortable result; limited by the analogue stress paradigm."
- **Abstract says:** 84 randomised, 74 completed, self-report distress measures, analogue stress-video paradigm, single group-PFA protocol.
- **Problem:** Randomisation is a genuine strength, but the headline-grabbing element (harm in the most distressed) is an interaction in a subgroup of a sample under 100, with self-report outcomes and no real trauma exposure. An 8 implies more evidential weight than this design can carry.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — a randomised test of a globally promoted intervention with an uncomfortable result, but an 84-person analogue paradigm with self-reported outcomes and a subgroup-level harm signal."

### Study 3: Nearly one in ten UK therapy clients says they recovered a memory of abuse they had not previously known about
**PMID:** 42663614 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=1,507, 40% therapy uptake, 4.9% / 3.6% of the total sample, 8.9% of therapy-goers, modality ranking, and the 2006–2024 range of 8–12% all match. The "millions in the UK" phrasing tracks the authors' own wording.

**Issue A. 1980s comparison not supported by a reported figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the fact that rates are as high for therapy started in the last two decades as during the memory wars"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher rates of memory recovery in therapy occurred when therapy started in the 1980s and recent decades (2006-2024: 8-12%)."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives a figure only for 2006–2024; no 1980s rate is reported, so an explicit equivalence claim between the two eras cannot be checked. The body text's weaker claim ("were not confined to the 1980s") is fine.
- **Suggested fix:** "and the fact that rates were also elevated for therapy started in recent decades, not just in the 1980s heyday of the memory wars"

### Study 5: Working memory is not the main thing helping you understand speech in a noisy room — reasoning ability is
**PMID:** 42659749 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=89, five degradation types, Reading Span, N-back, short-form Raven's, bivariate correlations for all measures, and fluid reasoning as top predictor in multivariate/dominance analyses are accurate. The online design is correctly flagged.

**Issue A. Headline states as settled what the authors hedge** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Working memory is not the main thing helping you understand speech in a noisy room — reasoning ability is"
- **Abstract says:** "These results may undermine the assumption that WM, as measured by the Reading Span and other tasks, is the primary cognitive construct supporting degraded speech intelligibility."
- **Problem:** The abstract says "may undermine"; the headline asserts the reversal flatly. "In a noisy room" also narrows to one of five degradation types.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reasoning, not working memory, may be what helps you follow degraded speech"

**Issue B. Unique-variance claim not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fluid reasoning was clearly the strongest predictor, with working memory contributing little unique variance"
- **Abstract says:** "multivariate and dominance analyses revealed that the test of fluid reasoning was the most important predictor of intelligibility."
- **Problem:** "Most important predictor" is reported; the specific claim that WM added little unique variance is a plausible inference but not stated. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "fluid reasoning was the most important predictor, ranking above both working memory measures"

### Study 6: The slowness of depression may be a timing prediction problem, not a movement problem
**PMID:** 42542090 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=38 MDD / 65 controls, intact basic motor function, frequency-specific desynchronisation below 1 Hz in the delta range, and correlations with depression severity, psychomotor anhedonia and vegetative symptoms are all accurate. Causal language is appropriately hedged ("may be", "tracked").

**Issue A. NS fit score high for sample size** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — a specific, testable reframing of psychomotor retardation with a striking frequency-specific signature; modest clinical sample."
- **Abstract says:** "We tested MDD subjects' (N = 38) ability to synchronize finger taps to rhythmic tone sequences… compared with healthy controls (N = 65)."
- **Problem:** A single-site, cross-sectional 38-patient study proposing a new mechanism for a century-old clinical feature, with medication status unreported, is thin support for a 7. The finding is objective rather than self-reported, which argues against dropping it further.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — a specific, testable reframing with a striking frequency-specific signature, but only 38 patients at a single site and no replication."

### Study 7: Insomnia, not oxygen starvation, explains the link between disturbed sleep and depression
**PMID:** 42663413 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

n=1,238, 68% male, mean age 57, the SHE (women) and MAILES (men) cohorts, the null for AHI and ODI, the significant SE% and WASO associations and their attenuation after adjusting for insomnia are all correct. The pitch explicitly instructs against treatment advice, which is the right call.

**Issue A. Headline uses explanatory language for a cross-sectional null** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Insomnia, not oxygen starvation, explains the link between disturbed sleep and depression"
- **Abstract says:** "While sleep fragmentation was associated with depressive symptoms, insomnia symptoms appear to be a key factor." (cross-sectional analysis; adjustment attenuates rather than establishes mechanism)
- **Problem:** "Explains" implies a mechanism established over time; this is a single cross-sectional adjustment in which associations lost significance, and depressive and insomnia symptoms share a self-report method. The body and Caveats hedge properly, so only the headline needs work.
- **Suggested fix:** "Insomnia symptoms, not low oxygen, may account for the link between disturbed sleep and depression"

### Study 9: Most children in mental health services report feeling watched or followed — and no one is asking about it
**PMID:** 42658756 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

The numbers (50 young people aged 8–16, 88%, 28%, 63.8%) are all accurate.

**Issue A. Population overgeneralised beyond a pre-selected trial sample** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Most children in mental health services report feeling watched or followed" / "50 young people aged 8–16 reported on distressing sensory experiences at baseline"
- **Abstract says:** "the experiences of FPs from a clinical sample of young people involved in the ChUSE trial, a feasibility and acceptability RCT looking at supporting young people with distressing sensory experiences (DSEs) within CAMHS services in the NHS."
- **Problem:** These 50 were recruited *because* they had distressing sensory experiences. The 88% therefore describes a highly selected subgroup, not children in CAMHS generally. The Caveats flag the absence of a non-clinical comparison group but not this selection step, and the headline generalises to all children in mental health services.
- **Suggested fix:** Headline: "Most young people in a trial for distressing sensory experiences report feeling watched or followed". Body: "Within the ChUSE feasibility RCT, 50 young people aged 8–16 who were already reporting distressing sensory experiences in NHS CAMHS reported on those experiences at baseline."

**Issue B. "No one is asking about it" overstates the authors' claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and no one is asking about it"
- **Abstract says:** "FPs are potentially more common and distressing for young people in CAMHS than previously recognised and routine assessment should be considered alongside assessment of other DSEs."
- **Problem:** The authors say routine assessment "should be considered" and that felt presence is under-recognised — not that clinicians never ask. The digest's own pitch note ("opens a line of enquiry") is more accurate than the headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "— and it is rarely asked about routinely"

### Study 10: Sitting through a criminal trial did not make bereaved relatives' grief worse — or better
**PMID:** 42635331 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=237, longitudinal CFA, PGD and well-being as distinct and negatively related constructs stable over time, neither predicting the other, and the null for CJS involvement after adjusting for pre-trial levels are all accurately reported.

**Issue A. Pitch headline overclaims from a single null** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "**Pitch angle:** "Closure is a myth — a landmark trial changed nothing about relatives' grief.""
- **Abstract says:** "if replicated, the findings indicate that CJS involvement may not significantly affect the mental health of bereaved people, raising questions about the generalizability of the framework of therapeutic jurisprudence…"
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly condition their conclusion on replication and frame it as raising questions; the pitch declares a cultural idea disproved on the basis of one observational cohort from a uniquely supported mass-casualty case. The digest's Caveats acknowledge replication is needed, so the pitch contradicts its own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "**Pitch angle:** "Did the trial help? For MH17 families, the data say it made no measurable difference either way.""

### Study 11: How much you'd be blamed for *not* doing something predicts your behavior better than how much you'd be praised for doing it
**PMID:** 42655904 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

The body text is accurate: six studies, virtue/duty evaluations not systematically related, improved prediction of intended blood donation and actual voting, and the masking and paid-donation puzzles.

**Issue A. Headline states a comparison the study did not report** — Severity: Major

- **As written:** "How much you'd be blamed for *not* doing something predicts your behavior better than how much you'd be praised for doing it"
- **Abstract says:** "we show that a bivalent measure of moral evaluations (assessing the blame or praise for both doing and not doing the same act) allows for more accurate predictions of moral behaviors"
- **Problem:** The finding is that measuring *both* poles improves prediction — not that the blame-for-omission pole outperforms the praise-for-commission pole. The headline asserts a horse race the abstract never reports, and it contradicts the digest's own body text ("Measuring both improved predictions"). A pitch built on this hook would collapse on contact with the paper.
- **Suggested fix:** "Asking how much you'd be blamed for *not* doing something — as well as praised for doing it — predicts behaviour better than either question alone"

### Study 12: Brain waves recorded a week after a stroke predict who will still be recovering thinking skills six months later
**PMID:** 42663449 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=96, EEG at 8±1 days, MoCA at 3 and 6 months, 55.2% recovery, the counterintuitive direction of the frontal PAF and DTABR predictors, elevated DFV, AUC 0.831, the ≥2-point MoCA threshold and the stepwise-regression overfitting risk are all correctly reported and caveated.

**Issue A. Several supporting details cannot be checked against the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "rehabilitation is often withdrawn early on the basis of a poor three-month assessment" / "Tran Cong Thang and Nguyen Van Tuan at Ho Chi Minh City are the calls" / "single center"
- **Abstract says:** Reports only that early qEEG "could help inform personalized rehabilitation planning"; author list gives "Nguyen VT" and "Tran TC" with initials only; no statement that the cohort was single-site.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. The clinical-practice premise (rehab withdrawal at three months) is the hinge of the "why it matters" and needs an independent source; the expanded Vietnamese author names are guesses from initials and are easy to get wrong in print; single-centre status is assumed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rehabilitation decisions often hinge on a three-month assessment [source needed] … Contact the corresponding authors (Nguyen V.T., Cho Ray Hospital / Tran T.C., University of Medicine and Pharmacy at Ho Chi Minh City) — confirm full names before use."

### Study 13: Men and women's prolonged grief hangs together differently — identity loss is more central for men
**PMID:** 42635333 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=1,093 (274 men, mean age 37.03), PG-13-R, higher severity in women, similar global strength, significantly different network structure, and meaninglessness/disengagement as most central in both networks are all accurate.

**Issue A. "Significantly" attached to an untested comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** ""identity disruption" — not knowing who you are without the person — was significantly more central in men"
- **Abstract says:** "Identity Disruption showed higher EI in males." (Significance values are reported only for network structure, M = 0.35, p = .018, and global strength, S = 0.75, p = .114.)
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a significance test for the overall network structure comparison, not for the identity-disruption centrality difference specifically. Calling that difference "significant" adds statistical weight the abstract does not supply.
- **Suggested fix:** ""identity disruption" — not knowing who you are without the person — was more central in men"

**Issue B. "Rather than on longing" is not in the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "men's grief may hinge disproportionately on a loss of self rather than on longing"
- **Abstract says:** Reports centrality only for Meaninglessness, Disengagement and Identity Disruption; yearning/longing centrality is not mentioned.
- **Problem:** The contrast with longing is an editorial addition; nothing in the abstract compares identity disruption with yearning.
- **Suggested fix:** "men's grief may hinge disproportionately on a loss of self"

### Study 14: The brain's waste-clearance system looks more impaired in people on two antipsychotics than one
**PMID:** 42551238 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Group sizes (37 / 30 / 36), both patient groups below controls, the combination group lower still including the left hemisphere, and the DTI-ALPS proxy caveat are all correct. The "do not imply anyone should change medication" note is appropriate.

**Issue A. Direction of the cognitive correlation reversed relative to the abstract's wording** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Lower ALPS values correlated with worse cognitive performance."
- **Abstract says:** "Correlation analyses revealed that the bilateral mean ALPS index and the left hemisphere ALPS index were negatively correlated with cognitive function."
- **Problem:** As literally written, a *negative* correlation between ALPS and cognitive function means higher ALPS went with poorer cognitive function — the opposite of the digest's claim. The direction only matches the digest if the cognitive measure is scored so that higher = worse (a deficit or symptom scale), which the abstract does not specify. Either way, the digest cannot assert this direction from the abstract, and it is the link on which the whole "clogged drainage harms cognition" hook rests.
- **Suggested fix:** "ALPS values also correlated with cognitive test scores, though the abstract does not name the cognitive instrument or make the direction unambiguous — confirm with the authors before pitching."

### Study 17: Autistic adults say talking to non-autistic people costs more effort — and noise makes them lose words
**PMID:** 42547992 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

NCATS instrument, 154 analysed responses from autistic adults aged 18–35, environmental effects, word loss/scripting/withdrawal, the greater effort and masking with allistic partners, and the Double Empathy Problem link are accurate. (The abstract notes 165 were surveyed and 154 responses analysed; the digest's figure is the analysed sample, which is fine.)

**Issue A. Authors' hedges dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most said environmental factors — crowding, noise, unfamiliarity — affected their communication" / "Negative emotions worsened communication regardless of who they were speaking to."
- **Abstract says:** "The majority of the participants noted that environmental factors, such as crowdedness, noise levels, and familiarity, at least somewhat impacted their communication" / "negative emotions such as stress, frustration, and anxiety may worsen communication difficulties regardless of the conversation partner's neurotype."
- **Problem:** "At least somewhat impacted" and "may worsen" are deliberately weak claims from self-reported reflective reports; the digest presents both as firm effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Most said environmental factors — crowding, noise, unfamiliarity — at least somewhat affected their communication… Participants reported that negative emotions may worsen communication regardless of who they were speaking to."

**Issue B. Wider angle extrapolates to workplaces the study never sampled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "open-plan environments were built on a theory of collaboration that appears to disable a substantial minority of workers."
- **Abstract says:** A reflective-report survey of 154 autistic adults about "past contextual conversations"; no workplace observation, no non-autistic comparison group, no measure of work performance.
- **Problem:** "Appears to disable a substantial minority of workers" presents a workplace conclusion the data cannot support — the study asked individuals to reflect on conversations, not on job function.
- **Suggested fix:** "open-plan environments were built on a theory of collaboration that autistic adults' own accounts suggest may make conversation harder for them."

### Study 18: Mental fatigue and physical fatigue push you off the sofa in completely different ways
**PMID:** 42543096 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

Study 1 (n=675, Italian PES validation) and Study 2 (n=424, mean age 25.2, three-month longitudinal mediation) are accurately described, as is the dissociation: physical fatigue acting through approach/avoidance tendencies and strongest for vigorous activity, mental fatigue unrelated to those tendencies.

**Issue A. Direction of the mental-fatigue effect is asserted, not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Mental fatigue had no relationship with those approach/avoidance tendencies at all, but directly predicted less light and moderate activity."
- **Abstract says:** "mental fatigue was not associated with these tendencies but directly predicted light and moderate physical activity."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state the sign of the direct effect. "Less" is the intuitive reading, but it is an assumption, and the whole "knowledge work erodes your walking" wider angle depends on it. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mental fatigue had no relationship with those approach/avoidance tendencies at all, but directly predicted light and moderate activity — confirm the sign of that effect with the authors before pitching."
