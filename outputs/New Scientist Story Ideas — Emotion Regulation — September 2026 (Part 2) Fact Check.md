### Study 1: Halving violence against children by treating the parents' own distress
**PMID:** 42754240 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

All headline numbers check out: N=479, IRR 0.48 for physical violence at T2, IRR 0.69 for psychological violence ("nearly a third"), improvements in distress, PTSS, emotion regulation and psychological flexibility, and the null on children's emotional/behavioural difficulties.

**Issue A. Pitch attributes the effect to one component of a package intervention** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The intervention did not primarily teach discipline techniques — it taught distressed adults how to handle their own feelings, and violence against their children dropped by half."
- **Abstract says:** "Parenting for Resilience, a co-designed, non-specialist delivered intervention to simultaneously reduce violence against children and improve caregiver mental health"; positive parenting, "parenting knowledge and beliefs, engagement in children's early learning" also improved.
- **Problem:** The trial tested an integrated package — parenting skills *and* mental health content delivered together — and reports parenting-specific gains. No mediation analysis is reported, so the claim that the caregiver-emotion component is the active ingredient (repeated in the headline, "by treating the parents' own distress") is not supported by the abstract. The digest's own study description gets this right ("tackles caregiver mental health and parenting skills at the same time"), so the headline and pitch contradict the body.
- **Suggested fix:** "Unlike most parenting programmes, this one tackled caregivers' own mental health alongside parenting skills — and violence against their children roughly halved. Whether the mental health component is what did the work is an open question the trial wasn't designed to answer."

*NS fit 7/10:* defensible — large prospectively registered pragmatic RCT with pre-specified primary outcomes; the self-report limitation is already named in the fit rationale and caveats.

### Study 2: It's how impulsive you feel today, not how impulsive you are, that predicts suicidal thoughts
**PMID:** 42778027 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparison between state and trait emotion dysregulation** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Emotion dysregulation predicted ideation both as a trait and, more strongly, as a momentary state."
- **Abstract says:** "main effects of tonic emotion dysregulation and phasic emotion dysregulation emerged as risk factors for concurrent and prospective momentary suicidal ideation."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports both as main effects with no statement that the phasic effect was larger. "More strongly" is an unsupported ranking that reinforces the headline's state-over-trait framing beyond what is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Emotion dysregulation predicted ideation both as a trait and as a momentary state."

**Issue B. EMA sampling frequency not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Open on the mechanics of an EMA study — a phone buzzing six times a day for a month."
- **Abstract says:** "30-day ecological momentary assessment period" — no prompt frequency given.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; "six times a day" is a specific methodological detail that would need checking in the full text before it appears in a pitch.
- **Suggested fix:** "Open on the mechanics of an EMA study — a phone buzzing repeatedly through the day for a month [confirm prompt schedule from full text]."

**Issue C. NS fit score and "overturning" language** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "**NS fit:** 7/10 — 30 days of intensive real-time data overturning a trait-based view of suicide risk"
- **Abstract says:** Community sample of 103, all self-report, observational EMA; a single two-way interaction with concurrent (not prospective) ideation, "No other 3-way or 2-way interactions emerged."
- **Problem:** A 7 resting on one self-selected, self-reported, observational sample of 103 — where the key interaction held only for concurrent ideation — is generous, and "overturning" overstates a single non-replicated finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "**NS fit:** 6/10 — 30 days of intensive real-time data challenging a trait-based view of suicide risk; held back by a modest, self-selected sample and a result confined to concurrent ideation."

### Study 3: Feeling seen as human by political opponents softens partisan hostility — and disgust is the reason
**PMID:** 42723350 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=1,541 across five studies in the UK, US and Canada; mediators (anger, disgust, empathy), outcomes and the moderate-level threat moderation all match.

**Issue A. Causal headline and "why it matters" against a correlational design** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Feeling seen as human by political opponents softens partisan hostility — and disgust is the reason" / "this points instead at a more visceral emotion — moral disgust — as the thing that has to be downregulated before people will talk to each other."
- **Abstract says:** "meta-humanization reliably predicts more conciliatory intergroup attitudes, with disgust emerging as the primary affective pathway."
- **Problem:** "Softens", "is the reason" and "has to be downregulated before" assert causation and necessity from mediation on what the digest itself calls "largely cross-sectional and correlational" data. The headline contradicts the Caveats line.
- **Suggested fix:** "Believing political opponents see you as human goes with less partisan hostility — and disgust, not empathy, is the emotion that tracks it" / "…this points instead at a more visceral emotion — moral disgust — as the pathway most strongly associated with willingness to engage."

**Issue B. Direction of the disgust pathway stated more precisely than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the dominant emotional pathway was reduced disgust, not increased empathy"
- **Abstract says:** "with disgust emerging as the primary affective pathway" — the abstract does not specify the sign of the meta-humanization→disgust path.
- **Problem:** "Reduced disgust" is a plausible reading but is not stated; flag as unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "the dominant emotional pathway ran through disgust rather than empathy [confirm direction of the path coefficient from full text]."

### Study 4: Resilience stops teenage distress starting — but won't get you out of it
**PMID:** 42749162 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=9,035, ages 10–17, two waves 12 months apart, four profiles (~60% / ~20% / ~12% / ~6%), 81% and 56% retention, and the emotion regulation / family support / interpersonal assistance dimensions all match the abstract.

**Issue A. A null result reported as a definitive absence of effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "once a teenager's body and mood are both affected, personal resilience does nothing to pull them out."
- **Abstract says:** "baseline resilience did not predict spontaneous recovery from mild or severe distress (all p > 0.05)"; "shows limited efficacy in reversing established symptoms."
- **Problem:** A non-significant association is not evidence that resilience "does nothing" — particularly in the smaller severe subgroup, where power to detect recovery predictors is lowest. The authors themselves hedge to "limited efficacy."
- **Suggested fix:** "once a teenager's body and mood are both affected, baseline resilience did not predict who recovered."

**Issue B. Measured trait resilience treated as "resilience training"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "'Resilience training' is sold as a universal fix, but here it behaves like a preventive measure only"
- **Abstract says:** Resilience was measured at baseline with the "Resilience Scale for Children and Adolescents"; no intervention was delivered.
- **Problem:** The study measured an existing individual difference in an observational cohort. Nothing here tells you how a resilience *programme* behaves. The pitch angle rightly warns against implying prevention, but the "Why it matters" line makes exactly that leap.
- **Suggested fix:** "Resilience is widely treated as a universal buffer, but the trait as measured here tracked only who avoided distress — not who got out of it."

**Issue C. Causal verb in the headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Resilience stops teenage distress starting"
- **Abstract says:** "Higher baseline resilience significantly buffered against transitions to more severe profiles" (observational cohort, odds ratios).
- **Problem:** "Stops" is causal language for an observational finding; the digest's own pitch angle instructs writers to avoid this framing, so headline and angle conflict.
- **Suggested fix:** "High resilience tracks with teenagers avoiding distress — but not with getting out of it."

### Study 5: Transcendental meditation cuts workplace stress in a six-month randomized trial
**PMID:** 42778032 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

n=65/n=64, Australian university staff, baseline/3/6 months, adjusted mean difference −2.25, Hedges' g = −0.52, and the secondary improvements are all accurate. Open-label design and absence of an active comparator are correctly flagged.

**Issue A. Dose-response finding restated as an attribution of the effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Exploratory analysis found the twice-daily practitioners accounted for most of the gains."
- **Abstract says:** "Exploratory analysis showed that twice-daily practice was significantly associated with improvements in perceived stress, well-being, HRQoL, symptoms of depression and burnout (p < .05)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association between twice-daily practice and improvement; it does not decompose the overall treatment effect or say this subgroup "accounted for most" of it. The rewrite implies an attribution analysis that isn't reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "An exploratory analysis found that twice-daily practice was associated with improvements in stress, wellbeing, quality of life, depression and burnout."

**Issue B. "Primary outcomes" pluralised** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "all primary outcomes self-reported"
- **Abstract says:** "Symptoms of perceived stress (primary outcome)" — singular.
- **Problem:** Small imprecision; the trial had one primary outcome, with the rest secondary.
- **Suggested fix:** "primary outcome (perceived stress) and all secondary outcomes self-reported."

### Study 6: AI-written stories only calm you down if they are both coherent and about you
**PMID:** 42714089 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=87, three conditions, the Complete Condition as the only beneficial one, and the Self-Referential-Only backfire on positive affect all match.

**Issue A. Pitch reframes a reading study as a test of journaling** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Journaling advice, tested to destruction — and a warning about the half-done version."
- **Abstract says:** "AI text generation technology was employed to manipulate these components… Participants completed pre- and post-exposure assessments of state anxiety and affect."
- **Problem:** Participants *read* AI-generated text; nobody wrote anything. Expressive writing/journaling involves a different process (self-generated construction of narrative), so a pitch built on "journaling advice tested" rests on a result the study didn't produce. The "Why it matters" line ("'Writing it out' is one of the most commonly recommended emotion regulation tools") has the same problem.
- **Suggested fix:** "The ingredients of narrative-based emotion regulation, pulled apart — and a warning about the half-done version. Note the manipulation was reading AI-generated text, not writing, so this is adjacent to journaling research rather than a direct test of it."

**Issue B. "First experiment" overstates the abstract's own claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first experiment to separate its ingredients"
- **Abstract says:** "its active components have rarely been isolated experimentally"; the authors describe the result as "initial experimental evidence."
- **Problem:** "Rarely" is not "never"; the authors don't claim priority.
- **Suggested fix:** "this is one of the first experiments to separate its ingredients."

**Issue C. Sample characteristics not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "findings from a Chinese student-age sample."
- **Abstract says:** No participant demographics beyond N=87; the only China link is the authors' affiliation (Jiangsu Normal University).
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — the sample nationality and age band need confirming from the full text before appearing in Caveats.
- **Suggested fix:** "single-site sample [confirm nationality and age profile from full text]."

### Study 7: Teenagers' emotional habits track their own reading of the family, not their parents'
**PMID:** 42753047 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=1,875 dyads, adolescent mean age 13.06, 76.5% mothers, polynomial regression/response surface analysis, and the closeness→reappraisal / conflict→suppression split are all accurate.

**Issue A. "Consistently" firmer than the abstract's hedge** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "consistently across early, middle and late adolescence"
- **Abstract says:** "These associations were generally consistent across early, middle, and late adolescence."
- **Problem:** Dropping "generally" converts a hedged statement into an absolute one.
- **Suggested fix:** "generally consistent across early, middle and late adolescence."

**Issue B. Causal verb for cross-sectional associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "closeness and conflict shape different regulation strategies rather than being two ends of one scale"
- **Abstract says:** "closeness was more strongly linked to reappraisal, whereas conflict was more closely linked to suppression."
- **Problem:** "Shape" implies directional influence in data the digest itself notes is cross-sectional.
- **Suggested fix:** "closeness and conflict are linked to different regulation strategies rather than being two ends of one scale."

### Study 8: Gay and bisexual women veterans gain more from emotion-skills therapy after military sexual trauma
**PMID:** 41411035 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=161 with 42 sexual minority women, STAIR vs PCT, comparable baseline symptoms, and greater SMW improvement on emotion regulation, maladaptive beliefs and depression (all p = .002) are accurate.

**Issue A. Headline ties the differential benefit specifically to STAIR** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Gay and bisexual women veterans gain more from emotion-skills therapy after military sexual trauma"
- **Abstract says:** "SMW started off with comparable symptoms to heterosexual women but improved more on emotion regulation…, maladaptive beliefs…, and depression symptoms… than heterosexual participants. Regardless of sexual orientation, participants had greater benefits in STAIR." Conclusion: "SMW veterans may particularly benefit from therapies that focus on emotion regulation… (e.g., STAIR, PCT)."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the SMW advantage across the trial, not as a STAIR-specific interaction, and the authors explicitly name both STAIR *and* PCT in their conclusion. The headline promises a treatment-by-orientation effect the abstract doesn't report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Gay and bisexual women veterans improved more than heterosexual peers in trauma therapy after military sexual trauma."

**Issue B. Alliance/satisfaction comparison probably misattributed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Therapeutic alliance and satisfaction were high and did not differ by sexual orientation."
- **Abstract says:** "Therapeutic alliance and treatment satisfaction were high in both STAIR and PCT with no difference between groups."
- **Problem:** In context, "groups" most plausibly refers to the two treatment arms named in the same sentence, not to sexual orientation. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Therapeutic alliance and satisfaction were high in both STAIR and present-centered therapy, with no difference between the two [confirm whether orientation was also compared]."

### Study 9: Brain scans link punishment sensitivity to a surprising pattern of prefrontal wiring
**PMID:** 42722323 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=357 healthy university students, VBM plus resting-state connectivity, negative punishment-sensitivity/reappraisal correlation, positive dlPFC grey matter association, dlPFC–precuneus connectivity and the suppression effect are all reported accurately, including the "opposing directions of the direct and indirect effects" detail.

**Issue A. Measurement modality asserted in Caveats but not stated in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "emotion regulation and punishment sensitivity both self-reported questionnaires; no task-based validation of reappraisal ability."
- **Abstract says:** "the present study integrated behavioral measurements, voxel-based morphometry (VBM), resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC), and mediation analysis."
- **Problem:** "Behavioral measurements" is not specified as questionnaire-only; the abstract also refers to "cognitive reappraisal ability." The caveat may be correct but is unverifiable from abstract only, and it is doing work in the pitch ("ask directly whether this is compensation").
- **Suggested fix:** "punishment sensitivity and reappraisal assessed behaviourally rather than via a validated reappraisal task [confirm instruments from full text]."

### Study 10: Bottling up feelings — not spirituality — predicts problematic porn use
**PMID:** 42766058 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=121, mean age 27.3, 53.7% male, the four significant predictors, 38.2% of variance, and the nulls for spirituality, self-esteem and age all match.

**Issue A. Pitch claims a religion-vs-spirituality contrast the study didn't measure, and swaps the outcome variable** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Spiritual but not religious people show no elevated distress about their porn use — the moral-incongruence effect appears to be religion-specific."
- **Abstract says:** "spirituality, self-esteem, and age had no significant relationship with PPU… These findings provide initial evidence that, unlike religion, spiritual values are not incongruent with pornography use."
- **Problem:** Two problems. First, religiosity was not measured in this sample — the religion contrast comes from prior literature, so the study cannot show the effect is "religion-specific"; it can only show spirituality was null here. Second, the outcome was problematic pornography use (PPU), not "distress about porn use"; psychological distress was a separate predictor variable in the same model, so the swap is genuinely confusing.
- **Suggested fix:** "In this sample, spirituality showed no relationship with problematic pornography use — a contrast with the well-replicated religiosity finding, though religiosity wasn't measured here. Pair that with the finding that habitual emotion suppressors report more problems."

### Study 11: Tracking the eyes while making an argument changes whether people act on it
**PMID:** 42784902 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=230, Germany, online, forward/backward digit span, fruit-and-vegetable audio message, moving-dot condition, two-week self-reported consumption, higher forward span benefiting most, and non-significance when spans are tested separately are all accurate.

**Issue A. Headline wording misidentifies whose eyes and whose argument** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Tracking the eyes while making an argument changes whether people act on it"
- **Abstract says:** "participants (N = 230) were all exposed to an auditory persuasive message… Half of them were asked to follow with their eyes a neutral, horizontally regular moving stimulus."
- **Problem:** The headline reads as though the *persuader's* eye movements were tracked while arguing; in fact recipients moved their own eyes while *receiving* a message, and nothing was "tracked" — it was an induced eye-movement task.
- **Suggested fix:** "Moving your eyes while hearing an argument changes whether you act on it."

**Issue B. Effect size described as small** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "effect sizes small (η² ≈ 0.05)"
- **Abstract says:** "Combining both measures in one difference score revealed significant moderation (p < .001, η2 = 0.055)."
- **Problem:** By conventional benchmarks η² ≈ 0.055 sits at small-to-medium, not plainly "small"; the separately-tested interactions were the small ones (η² = 0.01–0.012).
- **Suggested fix:** "effect sizes modest (η² = 0.055 for the combined measure; η² ≈ 0.01 when spans are tested separately)."

### Study 12: Anxious attachment predicts which children with ADHD stay traumatized a year after an earthquake
**PMID:** 41910948 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=124, 3- and 12-month assessments after the 2023 Türkiye earthquakes, the symptom-trajectory pattern (trait anxiety stable), and the medication association are all accurate.

**Issue A. Headline assigns PTSD persistence to anxious attachment when the abstract assigns it uniquely to avoidant** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Anxious attachment predicts which children with ADHD stay traumatized a year after an earthquake"
- **Abstract says:** "Poor family functioning and insecure attachment styles, particularly anxious attachment, were robust predictors of elevated emotional symptoms at follow-up. Avoidant attachment was uniquely associated with PTSD symptoms but not with depression or anxiety."
- **Problem:** "Stay traumatized" reads as PTSD, and the abstract explicitly singles out *avoidant* attachment for PTSD. The body text gets this right; the headline reverses the attachment style attached to the trauma outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attachment style predicts which children with ADHD still have elevated symptoms a year after an earthquake — with avoidant attachment specifically tied to PTSD."

**Issue B. "Best predictor" ranking not reported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the best predictor is how the family functions"
- **Abstract says:** "Poor family functioning and insecure attachment styles… were robust predictors of elevated emotional symptoms at follow-up."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports family functioning and insecure attachment together as robust predictors; it does not rank them.
- **Suggested fix:** "among the strongest predictors is how the family functions."

### Study 13: Unit support protects deployed fathers from parental burnout — until combat exposure gets severe
**PMID:** 42671804 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=105 Israeli military fathers, four burnout dimensions, the suppression associations, the exposure-level moderation and the reappraisal-moderated marital satisfaction finding all match.

**Issue A. Unit support described as predicting "burnout" generally** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Unit support predicted lower burnout — but only at low and moderate exposure levels"
- **Abstract says:** "higher unit support predicted lower emotional exhaustion and emotional distancing, but these protective effects were significant only at low and moderate war-related exposure levels."
- **Problem:** The association held for two of the four burnout dimensions, not the construct as a whole; the digest names all four dimensions earlier, so the generalisation is noticeable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Unit support predicted lower emotional exhaustion and emotional distancing — but only at low and moderate exposure levels."

**Issue B. Headline causal verb conflicts with the pitch's own instruction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Unit support protects deployed fathers from parental burnout" / "Frame the moderation honestly as a limit on a buffer, not a causal claim."
- **Abstract says:** Cross-sectional self-report questionnaires analysed with hierarchical regressions; "predicted lower emotional exhaustion and emotional distancing."
- **Problem:** The headline asserts the causal claim the pitch angle explicitly tells the writer to avoid — an internal inconsistency an editor will notice.
- **Suggested fix:** "Unit support tracks with less parental burnout in deployed fathers — until combat exposure gets severe."

### Study 14: A systematic review maps what prayer, chanting and meditation do to the brain
**PMID:** 42670176 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

105 studies (1998–2026), databases and search cut-off, traditions, practice-specific signatures and structural findings in long-term practitioners are all accurately rendered, and the erratum is correctly flagged.

**Issue A. "PRISMA-registered" conflates a reporting guideline with registration** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A PRISMA-registered systematic review searched Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE and ProQuest to June 2026"
- **Abstract says:** "Adhering to the PRISMA 2020 guidelines and registered on the Open Science Framework, Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE, and ProQuest (23 sub-databases) were searched from inception to June 30, 2026."
- **Problem:** PRISMA is a reporting standard; the protocol registration was on OSF. Small but the kind of thing a methods-literate reader will catch.
- **Suggested fix:** "A PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review, pre-registered on the Open Science Framework, searched Scopus, PubMed/MEDLINE and ProQuest to June 2026."

**Issue B. Claims about prayer's neglect in the literature are not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "prayer, the most widely practiced of them all, barely features in secular neuroscience" / "half the planet prays"
- **Abstract says:** The review synthesised prayer studies alongside meditation and others; it notes prior reviews were "limited to single practice types" but gives no comparative volume of prayer vs meditation research, and no prevalence figures.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only. These are the load-bearing claims of the pitch hook, so they need sourcing before the pitch goes out — the review's own inclusion of prayer studies partly undercuts "barely features."
- **Suggested fix:** "prayer has attracted far less neuroscientific attention than meditation [verify from the review's study-count breakdown in the full text]."

### Study 15: A psychodynamic online program shifts a personality trait in 10 weeks
**PMID:** 42735080 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

N=30, 10 weeks of therapist-supported IPDT, IPIP-NEO 300 as the primary outcome, d = 0.64 maintained at 6 months, secondary improvements and the defence-mechanism predictor (β = .63) are all accurate, and the uncontrolled design is properly caveated.

**Issue A. Author described as the developer of the therapy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "authors include the developer of the therapy approach (Frederick, Center for Courageous Living)."
- **Abstract says:** "Frederick RJ… Center for Courageous Living, Los Angeles, California, United States" — no statement of the author's role in developing affect phobia therapy, and no declared conflict of interest.
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only, and potentially wrong: affect phobia therapy is generally credited to Leigh McCullough. The conflict-of-interest point may still stand in some form, but the specific attribution needs checking.
- **Suggested fix:** "authors include a clinician-author associated with affect phobia therapy (Frederick, Center for Courageous Living) — verify the nature of the affiliation and any undeclared interest."

**Issue B. Headline asserts change from an uncontrolled pilot** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A psychodynamic online program shifts a personality trait in 10 weeks"
- **Abstract says:** "Results from an Uncontrolled Pilot Study"; "affect-focused IPDT shows promise… These results need to be replicated using a randomized controlled trial design."
- **Problem:** "Shifts" states an effect that a single-arm design with 30 participants cannot separate from regression to the mean or expectancy — which the digest's own caveats acknowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Neuroticism fell after a 10-week psychodynamic online programme — in an uncontrolled pilot of 30 people."

### Study 16: A transdiagnostic therapy adapted into Urdu passes its first test in Pakistan
**PMID:** 42768329 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

No factual errors found. N=51 (n=25/n=26), 14-week UA-UP vs waitlist, retention 82.4%, homework completion 79%, high CSQ-8 satisfaction, anxiety g = 1.18 and depression g = 1.15, and the qualitative emotional-awareness theme all match the abstract. Framing is consistent: the digest repeatedly labels this a feasibility pilot, warns explicitly against reading the effect sizes as efficacy, and flags the waitlist control and retrospective registration in Caveats. The 4/10 fit score is appropriately conservative.

### Study 17: Putting people in a bad mood reveals different brain networks in heavy internet users
**PMID:** 42773324 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

20 vs 20 matched participants, pre/post mood-induction resting-state fMRI, independent component analysis, successful mood induction in both groups, and four affected networks are all accurate.

**Issue A. "Did not survive permutation testing" is firmer than the abstract's wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the prefrontal-parietal default mode and dorsal attention effects survived permutation testing, while the limbic and temporo-sensorimotor findings did not."
- **Abstract says:** "The prefrontal-parietal Default Mode and Dorsal Attention effects were robust to non-parametric permutation testing, whereas the temporo-sensorimotor and limbic effects were preliminary."
- **Problem:** "Preliminary" is the authors' chosen hedge; "did not survive" asserts an explicit failed test that the abstract does not report. The Caveats line repeats this. The direction of the caution is right, so this is a wording fix rather than a substantive error.
- **Suggested fix:** "the prefrontal-parietal default mode and dorsal attention effects were robust to permutation testing, while the limbic and temporo-sensorimotor findings are described by the authors as preliminary."
