# Topic Memory: psychotherapy
_Last updated: 2026-09-11_

## Established findings
- Psychotherapy produces broad symptom improvement across modalities and populations; the open questions in current literature are about moderators, timing and measurement rather than overall efficacy (multiple, Sep 2026 run).
- Trauma-focused and integrated psychotherapy for comorbid PTSD + substance use beat treatment as usual on alcohol severity, without the feared destabilization; veterans benefit more than civilians (PMID 41114957).
- Demand for psychotherapy in the US has risen sharply post-2020 (9.5% to 13.4%, 2019-2023), with symptom change and insurance explaining under half the increase (PMID 42723007).

## Emerging threads
- Measurement dependence: sudden gains identified differently by different scales (PMID 40955789); relational gains appear only years after discharge (PMID 42695247); access metrics mask treatment dose gaps (PMID 42592892).
- Automation in the therapy room: LLM session summaries approach clinician quality on relationship/internal-functioning content (PMID 42715612), but sentiment tools show near-zero turn-level agreement with human coders (PMID 42708250).
- Biological and bodily moderators of therapy: progesterone level on session day predicts less avoidance improvement in exposure therapy (PMID 42700422); therapist-child nonverbal coordination works via in-session emotion regulation (PMID 42721331).
- Psychedelic-assisted therapy's consumer and formulation politics: public naturalness bias favors mushroom-derived over identical synthetic psilocybin (PMID 42678038); botanical psilocin may match psilocybin with fewer adverse effects, with industry co-authorship (PMID 42668404).
- Cultural and secular assumptions in delivery: Muslim clients self-censor religion in UK therapy (PMID 42068016).
- Clinician training gaps: European psychiatric trainees acquire suicide-risk competence largely through experiencing a patient suicide, with poor postvention support (PMID 42622500).
