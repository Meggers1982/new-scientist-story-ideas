# Topic Memory: psychotherapy
_Last updated: 2026-10-01_

## Established findings
- Psychotherapy reliably improves symptoms across modalities; live questions are moderators, timing, mechanism and measurement, not overall efficacy (multiple, Sep-Oct 2026 runs).
- Patients usually excluded or deprioritized do not respond worse: PTSD + substance use (PMID 41114957, Sep 2026); comorbid BPD on esketamine (PMID 42322927, Oct 2026).
- Benefits accrue past the usual endpoint: relational gains grow for years after discharge (PMID 42695247); psychodynamic therapy's functioning advantage wider at 36 than 24 months (PMID 42716057).
- Early dropout among minoritized and religious clients is better explained by service fit than patient motivation (PMIDs 42068016, 42592892, 42726438).
- US psychotherapy use rose 41% (2019-2023); symptoms and insurance explain under half (PMID 42723007).

## Emerging threads
- Measurement and replication fragility: sudden gains vary by scale (PMID 40955789); a published group-session typology fails to replicate (PMID 42295247).
- Computational tools underperform on their stated task but track alliance: sentiment analysis (PMID 42708250), digital-phenotyping dashboards and first-person pronouns (PMID 42755118); AI session notes near-clinician on relational content (PMID 42715612).
- Mechanism research splitting by type — relational/bodily pathways hold (PMID 42721331), cognitive/neuro pathways do not (PMID 42800322).
- Depth traditions generating first outcome data, of very uneven rigor (PMIDs 42716057, 42779379, 42736557, 42775480).
- Client-side predictors: session comfort over emotional depth (PMID 42530984); personal-versus-general expectancy gap (PMID 42789775).
- Drug-plus-therapy beyond psychedelics — oxytocin targeting a therapy process, not a symptom (PMID 42372695); earlier hormonal moderation of exposure therapy (PMID 42700422).
- Non-specific trial-participation effects large enough to swamp training content (PMID 42698100).
