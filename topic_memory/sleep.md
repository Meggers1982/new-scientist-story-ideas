# Topic Memory: sleep
_Last updated: 2026-08-22_

## Established findings
- Sleep duration alone is a weak predictor of mood and mental-health outcomes; quality and multi-night accumulation outperform it (PMID 42617377, Aug 2026).
- Sleep–mental-health associations are U-shaped: both <6 h and ≥9 h flag risk, with long sleep often the stronger signal (PMIDs 42546640, 42628578, Jul–Aug 2026).
- Sleep problems cluster with severe depression phenotypes: ~50% of depressive episodes meet nightmare-disorder criteria, with actigraphy-confirmed fragmentation (PMID 42617369, Aug 2026).

## Emerging threads
- Measurement crisis: composite metrics (Sleep Regularity Index, sleep scores, SII/SIRI/PIV inflammatory indices) may hide clinically meaningful asymmetries — e.g. bedtime vs wake-time regularity diverging in autistic children (PMID 42593049).
- Directionality being re-tested with lagged/genetic designs, and coming out one-way rather than bidirectional: sleep→depression but not reverse (PMID 42541934); PTSD→OSA but not reverse, with cerebellar/cortical heritability enrichment (PMID 42611531).
- Sleep as a structurally rationed resource rather than a personal choice: hustle culture and sleep stigma in women of colour (PMID 42624738), nonstandard shifts and toddler bedtime routines buffered by social capital (PMID 42545383), institutional waking in intellectual-disability care (PMID 42613717). Strong candidate for a future standalone feature.
- Nulls worth tracking: sleep quality did not predict firefighter resilience over 2 years (PMID 42495965); overnight sleep did not modulate retention of incidentally heard pseudowords (PMID 42593994).
- Chronotherapeutics (sleep deprivation + light) remain under-covered for general readers; eNAMPT/NAD+ as a possible shared response marker with drug treatment (PMID 41903946) — preliminary, N=46.
