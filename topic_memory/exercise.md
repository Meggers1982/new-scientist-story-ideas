# Topic Memory: exercise
_Last updated: 2026-08-26_

## Established findings
- Mind-body and general exercise interventions for mental health show consistent small-to-moderate benefits but rest heavily on small, unblinded, self-report trials with likely publication bias (PMID 42412001, Aug 2026).
- People with severe mental illness (bipolar disorder) are markedly more sedentary than the general population; self-reported activity diverges sharply from objectively measured (PMID 42545207, Aug 2026).

## Emerging threads
- Non-monotonic and inverted-U dose relationships challenge "more is better" framing: mind-body dose ceiling ~490 MET-min/week (PMID 42412001); elevated suicidal ideation at 1–3 days/week aerobic activity in victimized teens, attenuated only at 4+ (PMID 42605073).
- Self-replication and pre-registration are trimming headline claims: exercise fails to preserve retrieval-induced forgetting in the original lab's own replication (PMID 42579016); static norms beat dynamic norms on behavioral exercise outcomes (PMID 42615122).
- Exercise as null arm / weaker lever than expected: morning outdoor exercise lost to bright classroom light and mindfulness for adolescent sleep (PMID 41761836).
- Adverse-effect blind spot in wellness practices — brief compassion exercise increased anger and worsened affect (PMID 42520720).
- Autonomy and choice, not exercise dose, may drive workplace wellness effects; burnout unmoved by perks (PMID 42362858).
- Health behaviors as social scaffolding: adolescent drinking, smoking and exercise all predicted less later loneliness in 28-year Norwegian cohort (PMID 42544718).
- Cracks in measurement foundations: interoceptive sensitivity does not correlate across cardiac, respiratory and esophageal domains (PMID 42526739); "listen to your body" complicated by deliberate resource-discordant training choices (PMID 42034345).
- Exercise as compensatory/disordered behavior — calorie-compensation "drunkorexia" subtype clusters with excessive exercise and body dissatisfaction (PMID 42641412).
- Technology angles building: VR-supported PE gains partly retained at 4-week follow-up (PMID 42556203); momentary hunger and low coping self-efficacy predict exercise lapses, relevant to just-in-time adaptive interventions (PMID 40415394).
