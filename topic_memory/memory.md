# Topic Memory: memory
_Last updated: 2026-09-19_

## Established findings
- Enactment/physical rehearsal beats mental simulation and passive encoding for intentions and instructions, across ages and independent of vision (42627881, 42622513; Aug 2026) — but enactment is also the failure point when instructions arrive in a second language (42717310; Sep 2026).
- Offloading is double-edged: expected external access suppresses encoding (42622798) and transcription errors rise under load (42607389), yet shunting items into a motor routine (cumulative typing) expands effective span (42754727; Sep 2026).
- Dual-task/eye-movement recall reduces vividness and unpleasantness of aversive memories, emotion-generally and even without conscious reactivation (42622123, 42531952; Aug 2026).
- Sleep supports source memory and downstream social behavior, not just item retention (42492165; Jul 2026); overnight consolidation is emerging as a shared vulnerability across insomnia (42537590) and epilepsy (42748517).

## Emerging threads
- Core clinical memory theory under attack: intrusive memories are not cleanly cue-specific (42759257); conditioning-based treatment targets may need rethinking.
- PTSD reframed away from fear: genital responses at recall in a third of 1057 sexual-abuse patients, with disgust and self-hatred (not shame) tracking severity (42704048); personalized network subtypes (42607016).
- Positive-affect regulation as causal, not symptomatic: dampening a positive memory makes negative social beliefs more rigid (42727265), complementing positive-memory therapy work (42623486).
- Well-powered nulls and preregistered self-reanalyses are reversing canonical claims: no cultural offset in earliest memory (42757868), intact attentional boost in depression (42706587), consequences beating antecedents in narrative memory (42760449), segmentation dissociated from encoding (42758465).
- Volition, not control, as the memory-relevant ingredient (42753975) — relevant to adaptive learning software that chooses items for learners.
- Biomarker-to-behavior gap persists at scale: gamma power tracks face memory over 4 years in autism but is not clinically usable (42721928); network-guided stimulation moved connectivity, not memory (42610835).
- Exposure attribution in brain-injury cohorts: football years act directly on cognition, military service only via PTSD (42753419); large self-enrolled online cohorts now carry this evidence base.
- Outcome measurement over decades: quality of life good 10–44 years after epilepsy surgery even with ongoing seizures (42727324) — response shift as a problem for patient-reported outcomes.
- Data-quality/meta-science: ~a third of online participants go off task during encoding; warnings don't fix it (42608507).

## Feature ideas already pitched
- Aug 2026: "Memory is enacted, not stored" — embodied encoding (enactment, handwriting) versus the cost of offloading to notes, AI summaries and voice interfaces (42627881, 42622513, 42607453, 42622798, 42607389). New Scientist Features, BBC Future, The Atlantic, Wired.
- Sep 2026: memory science's demolition phase — a cluster of nulls, reversals and preregistered self-reanalyses retiring textbook claims (42757868, 42759257, 42760449, 42758465, 42706587). New Scientist Features, Undark, Guardian science, Nature news feature.
